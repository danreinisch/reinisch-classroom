Missed Class?
Start Here.

Being absent creates a gap. It does not require a memorial service. The goal is to figure out what you missed, rebuild the important pieces, and rejoin the class without turning one absence into three more lost days.

CheckNotesWorkAskRejoin

The big idea

Find the gap. Rebuild what matters. Keep moving.

Find

What did I miss?

Use ReinischClassroom to see the lesson, assignment, reading, and status.

Rebuild

What do I need to catch up?

Turn to the missed Guided Notes, use the presentation/resources, and complete what you can.

Rejoin

What do I do now?

Ask about the part that is still unclear, make a plan, and join today’s work.

Important: “I was absent” explains why there is a gap. It does not have to become the reason the gap stays there.

Step 1 • Check

Start with ReinischClassroom before starting a scavenger hunt.

Assignments

Check the correct class

Look at the assignment and its status.

Resources

Find the lesson tools

Presentations, Guided Notes support, and reference materials.

Library

Find the reading

Use the classroom book or reading if that is what you missed.

Status / Grades

Check what already happened

Do not redo the wrong thing because you skipped the status check.

Step 2 • Rebuild

Turn to the Guided Notes you missed. They are part of the recovery plan.

1

Find the missed notes

The pages stay in your binder. Turn to the lesson you missed.

2

Open the lesson/resource

Use ReinischClassroom to find the related presentation or material.

3

Complete what you can

Recover the information you can from the lesson materials.

4

Mark the unclear part

Now you know exactly what to ask about.

Why this works: you come to me with a smaller, clearer problem instead of handing me the entire absence and saying, “Fix this.”

Step 3 • Work

Do what you can before waiting for help.

Step 4 • Ask

Ask about the missing piece — not the entire universe.

“I found yesterday’s assignment. I understand questions 1–3, but I do not understand what question 4 is asking.”
“I filled in most of the Guided Notes. What goes in this section?”
“I found the reading. Where did the class stop?”
“I missed two days. What are the two things I need to do first?”
That last sentence is gold: it turns a backlog into a priority list.

If you missed several days

Do not build a panic pile. Prioritize.

The panic-pile method

Open everything at once

Stare at six assignments, decide it is impossible, accomplish nothing, feel worse. Tremendous system. Zero stars.

VS.
The useful method

Find the next two priorities

Check ReinischClassroom, identify what is most important, ask what should come first if you are unsure, then start one thing.

Step 5 • Rejoin

Catch up without missing today while catching up on yesterday.

1

Check

See what you missed.

2

Recover

Notes, lesson, reading.

3

Start

Do what you can.

4

Ask

Get the missing piece.

5

Rejoin

Return to today’s work.

The goal is recovery, not punishment. One absence should not keep owning your schedule after you are back.
