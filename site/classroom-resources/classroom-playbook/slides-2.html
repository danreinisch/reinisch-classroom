Expectation 3

Participation is not a volume contest.

You do not have to talk the most or pretend to love every activity. You do have to engage with the work in some real way.

A tiny routine with a ridiculous payoff

Listen → clarify → start.

1

Let the direction finish

Try not to ask the question while I am still answering it. This saves all of us from unnecessary reruns.

2

Ask for the missing piece

“What are we doing?” makes us restart the movie. A specific question gets you moving.

3

Do the first thing

You do not need perfect confidence before taking step one.

Expectation 5

Help me help you.

“I don't get it” is a perfectly good alarm bell. Add one detail and we can usually fix the problem a lot faster.

“I understand ___, but I’m stuck on ___.”
“Can you show me one example?”
“Which step should I do first?”
“Can I use the word bank / notes / sentence frame?”
“I need a minute, but I’ll come back to it.”
“Can you check whether I started this correctly?”

The usual learning loop

Most class periods follow a familiar rhythm.

Not every day uses every step. This is the basic spine so the room feels predictable. We do not need to reinvent school from scratch every hour.

The paper system

Your binder is Future You's backup system.

Guided notes give you enough structure to think about the lesson instead of copying everything at panic speed.

WHS rules + classroom use

The Chromebook is a class tool. Personal electronics are not part of class.

The school handbook sets the rules. My directions tell you how we use the school-issued Chromebook for the task — they do not override those rules.
