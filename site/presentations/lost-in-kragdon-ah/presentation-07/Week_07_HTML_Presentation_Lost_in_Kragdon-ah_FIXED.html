Today's Grammar Focus: What Are Verb Tenses?

A verb tense tells us WHEN an action happens. The word "tense" comes from a Latin word meaning "time." Today we learn the three main tenses!

Day 1: What Are Verb Tenses?

Understanding WHEN Actions Happen

Understanding Verb Tenses

WHAT ARE VERB TENSES? Verb tenses are different forms of action words (verbs) that tell us WHEN something happens. The same action can be written in past, present, or future tense.
WHY DO WE NEED VERB TENSES? Verb tenses help readers understand the TIME of events. Without them, we wouldn't know if something already happened, is happening now, or will happen later. Stories would be confusing!
HOW DO I USE THEM? Change the ending or form of the verb to match when the action happens. "Walk" becomes "walked" (past) or "will walk" (future). We'll learn the patterns!

Day 1: The Three Main Tenses

Past, Present, and Future

PAST TENSE

Actions that ALREADY HAPPENED

Yesterday, last week, before

Alex jumped into the hole.

PRESENT TENSE

Actions happening RIGHT NOW

Today, currently, always

Alex jumps into the hole.

FUTURE TENSE

Actions that WILL HAPPEN

Tomorrow, next week, later

Alex will jump into the hole.

Notice the Pattern

  • Past: Usually add -ed to the verb (jump → jumped)
  • Present: Use the base verb, sometimes add -s (jump, jumps)
  • Future: Add "will" before the verb (will jump)

Day 1: Past Tense Verbs

Regular and Irregular Verbs

Regular Past Tense

Most verbs: just add -ed

  • jump → jumped
  • attack → attacked
  • pull → pulled
  • pierce → pierced
  • discover → discovered

Irregular Past Tense

Some verbs change completely!

  • fall → fell
  • hold → held
  • run → ran
  • see → saw
  • fight → fought

How to Remember

Most verbs follow the -ed rule. The irregular ones are common words that you'll learn to recognize. When in doubt, ask: "Does this SOUND right?" Your ear often knows!

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 1

MOTLEY

Definition: A mixture of different things that don't match; varied and mismatched.

Understanding Motley

WHAT IS IT? Motley describes a group of things that are all different and don't seem to belong together. It often suggests a random or poor collection.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? The weapons the village offered were described as "a motley gathering" - farm tools, wooden swords, and other random items that weren't real weapons.
HOW DO I USE IT? "The motley crew of volunteers included farmers, teachers, and teenagers."

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 2

IMPETUOUS

Definition: Acting quickly without thinking about the consequences; rash and impulsive.

Understanding Impetuous

WHAT IS IT? Being impetuous means doing things on impulse without stopping to think first. It can be brave, but also dangerous.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? Alex realized he "had been impetuous once again" when he jumped into the hole with the dandra-ta without a plan for getting out.
HOW DO I USE IT? "His impetuous decision to climb the cliff without ropes nearly cost him his life."

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 3

POULTICE

Definition: A soft, moist mixture of healing materials spread on cloth and applied to wounds.

Understanding Poultice

WHAT IS IT? A poultice is a traditional healing treatment - a paste or mixture applied to the skin to draw out infection or poison and promote healing.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? Frina-eh uses poultices made with green powder to draw the poison out of Werda-ak's leg wound after he is scratched by a dandra-ta tail spike.
HOW DO I USE IT? "The healer applied a warm poultice to the infected cut."

PRIMAL

DEHYDRATED

Day 2: Changing Verb Tenses

How to Switch Between Tenses

Converting Verb Tenses

WHAT IS TENSE CONVERSION? Changing a verb from one tense to another while keeping the same meaning. For example, "walk" can become "walked" (past) or "will walk" (future).
WHY IS IT IMPORTANT? Writers need to keep their tenses consistent. If you start a story in past tense, you should stay in past tense. Switching randomly confuses readers!
HOW DO I DO IT? Start with the base verb (the simplest form). Then apply the rules: add -ed for past, add -s (if needed) for present, add "will" for future.

Day 3: Verb Tenses in Context

Finding All Three Tenses in Writing

Find the Tenses in This Passage

"Alex looked at Werda-ak, who was still weak. 'You need more rest,' Alex said. 'We will leave when you are ready.'"

Tense Analysis

  • looked, was, said = PAST (narration - what happened)
  • need, are = PRESENT (dialogue - current situation)
  • will leave = FUTURE (dialogue - plan for later)

Writers often mix tenses when they include dialogue, because characters talk about now and the future!

Day 4: Writing Prompt

Your Prompt

How did Frina-eh save Werda-ak's life? Use details from Chapter 21 to explain what she did.

Details to Include

  • What happened to Werda-ak?
  • What treatments did Frina-eh use?
  • How long did it take for him to recover?
  • Why was her help so important?

Topic Sentence Starter

Day 4: Writing Structure

Building Your Response

Remember Your Writing Structure

  • Topic Sentence: State that Frina-eh saved Werda-ak
  • Supporting Detail 1: What happened (poisoned by dandra-ta spike)
  • Supporting Detail 2: First treatment (Alex cut wound, Frina-eh used poultice)
  • Supporting Detail 3: Ongoing care (changed bandages, gave water)
  • Conclusion: Restate that her treatment worked

Keep Your Tenses Consistent!

Since you're writing about events that already happened, use past tense throughout your response. Don't switch between tenses!

Day 4: Writing Checklist

Check Your Work Before Submitting

Verb Tense Checklist

  • Are all your verbs in past tense?
  • Did you use -ed endings correctly?
  • Did you check your irregular verbs (was, had, went, took)?
  • Did you avoid switching to present or future tense accidentally?

Content Checklist

  • Do you have a topic sentence that answers the question?
  • Do you have at least three details from the story?
  • Do you have a conclusion sentence?

Day 4: Model Response

Example Answer

Frina-eh saved Werda-ak's life by using her healing skills and never giving up. First, Werda-ak was poisoned when a dandra-ta tail spike scratched his leg during the battle. Second, Frina-eh sprinkled green powder into the wound and applied poultices to draw out the poison. Third, she stayed with him all night, changed his bandages every few hours, and dripped water into his mouth to keep him hydrated. Because of Frina-eh's skill and dedication, Werda-ak recovered and was able to continue the journey.

Notice:

Every verb is in past tense because we're writing about events that already happened. The tenses are consistent throughout!
