Today's Grammar Focus: Complete Sentences

A complete sentence has three parts: a subject, a verb, and a complete thought. Today we will learn to identify all three!

Day 1: What is a Complete Sentence?

The Three Essential Parts

Understanding Complete Sentences

WHAT IS A COMPLETE SENTENCE? A complete sentence is a group of words that expresses a full idea. It has a subject (who or what), a verb (action or being), and makes sense all by itself.
WHY DO WE NEED COMPLETE SENTENCES? Complete sentences help readers understand exactly what we mean. Incomplete sentences (fragments) leave readers confused, asking "Who? What happened? What about it?"
HOW DO I WRITE ONE? Ask yourself three questions: (1) Who or what is this about? (2) What are they doing or being? (3) Does it make sense on its own? If you can answer all three, you have a complete sentence!

Day 1: The Three Parts of a Sentence

Subject, Verb, and Complete Thought

Breaking Down a Complete Sentence

Alex studied the map carefully.
  • Alex = The SUBJECT (Who is the sentence about?)
  • studied = The VERB (What action is happening?)
  • the map carefully = Completes the thought (Makes sense on its own!)

The Complete Sentence Test

Read your sentence out loud. Ask yourself:

  • Can I point to the WHO or WHAT? (Subject)
  • Can I find the ACTION or BEING word? (Verb)
  • Does it make sense BY ITSELF? (Complete thought)

If you answer YES to all three, you have a complete sentence!

Day 1: What is a Sentence Fragment?

Incomplete Sentences

Understanding Fragments

WHAT IS A FRAGMENT? A fragment is an incomplete sentence. It is missing the subject, the verb, or a complete thought. It looks like a sentence (starts with a capital, ends with a period) but something is missing.
WHY ARE FRAGMENTS A PROBLEM? Fragments confuse readers. They leave questions unanswered: "Who did this? What happened? So what?" Good writing answers these questions.
HOW DO I SPOT A FRAGMENT? Read it out loud. If it leaves you asking "Who? What? So what?" it's probably a fragment. If it sounds incomplete or like part of a bigger idea, it needs to be fixed.

Day 1: Types of Fragments

What's Missing?

Missing Subject

FRAGMENT:

"Ran to the railing."

WHO ran? We don't know!

FIXED:

"The man ran to the railing."

Missing Verb

FRAGMENT:

"The horses from Tonton-ah."

What about them? What did they DO?

FIXED:

"The horses from Tonton-ah were strong."

Incomplete Thought

FRAGMENT:

"Because the air is heavy."

So what? What happens because of this?

FIXED:

"They don't hurry home because the air is heavy."

-ING Fragment

FRAGMENT:

"Running toward the horses."

WHO is running?

FIXED:

"Werda-ak was running toward the horses."

Day 1: Practice - Fragment or Complete?

Test Your Skills

Example 1 from Chapter 14:

"Alex sat down on a bench."

  • Subject: Alex - YES!
  • Verb: sat - YES!
  • Complete thought: YES! We know what happened.

COMPLETE SENTENCE!

Example 2:

"Hot and humid."

  • Subject: NONE - Who or what is hot and humid?

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 1

INDOCTRINATION

Definition: Training someone to accept a set of beliefs without questioning them.

Understanding Indoctrination

WHAT IS IT? Indoctrination is when someone is trained to believe something so strongly that they follow orders without asking questions - even dangerous orders.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? The attackers were so indoctrinated that they would rather die than be captured and reveal information. This shows how powerful their training was.
HOW DO I USE IT? "The soldier's indoctrination made him follow orders without questioning them."

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 2

SKEPTICAL

Definition: Doubting that something is true; not easily convinced.

Understanding Skeptical

WHAT IS IT? Being skeptical means you need more proof before you believe something. You question information rather than accepting it immediately.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? Preta-eh is skeptical of Verda-eh's story about how she learned about Lusta-ah. She doesn't fully believe the explanation.
HOW DO I USE IT? "I was skeptical of the excuse because it seemed too convenient."

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 3

UNCHARTED

Definition: Not shown on a map; unknown or unexplored.

Understanding Uncharted

WHAT IS IT? Uncharted means a place that has not been mapped or explored. It's a blank space where no one knows what's there.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? Alex notices a large uncharted area on the map. He thinks this might be where Lusta-ah is hiding - they left their own territory off the map on purpose!
HOW DO I USE IT? "The explorers ventured into uncharted territory, not knowing what they would find."

Day 2: Identifying Subjects

Finding the WHO or WHAT

Finding the Subject

WHAT IS A SUBJECT? The subject is the WHO or WHAT the sentence is about. It's the person, place, thing, or idea that does the action or is being described.
WHY IS IT IMPORTANT? Without a subject, readers don't know WHO or WHAT you're talking about. The sentence becomes a fragment.
HOW DO I FIND IT? Ask: "Who or what is doing the action?" or "Who or what is being described?" The answer is your subject.

Practice: Find the Subject

"Kilta-eh examined the wound carefully." - Subject: Kilta-eh

"The horses walked slowly down the path." - Subject: The horses

"Alex and Senta-eh studied the map." - Subject: Alex and Senta-eh

Day 2: Identifying Verbs

Finding the Action or Being Word

Finding the Verb

WHAT IS A VERB? The verb is the action word (run, jump, study) or a state of being word (is, are, was, were, seems). It tells what the subject DOES or IS.
WHY IS IT IMPORTANT? Without a verb, nothing happens in the sentence. The reader doesn't know what action occurred or what state exists.
HOW DO I FIND IT? Ask: "What is the subject DOING?" or "What IS the subject?" The answer is your verb.

Practice: Find the Verb

"Preta-eh laughed at the sight." - Verb: laughed

"The air is heavy in Lusta-ah." - Verb: is

"Alex rolled up the map." - Verb: rolled

Day 2: Signal Words That Create Fragments

Watch Out for These!

Danger Words

These words often start dependent clauses that CANNOT stand alone:

Word Fragment Example Fixed Sentence
Because "Because they were tired." "They stopped because they were tired."
When "When Alex looked at the map." "When Alex looked at the map, he noticed something strange."
If "If the healer arrives." "If the healer arrives, she will help."
Although "Although it was raining." "Although it was raining, they kept traveling."

Day 2: Fixing Fragments Practice

Transform Fragments into Complete Sentences

Fix These Fragments

Fragment: "The soldiers who arrived late."

What's missing? The VERB - what did they do?

Fixed: "The soldiers who arrived late were scolded."


Fragment: "Running through the forest."

What's missing? The SUBJECT - who is running?

Fixed: "Monda-ak was running through the forest."

Day 3: Senta-eh's Fear

Character Development

Senta-eh's Backstory

When Senta-eh was just three years old, her older brother put her on an alecs-ta (a donkey-like creature). She fell off and hit her head so badly her parents thought she would die.

This is the only fear the fierce warrior has ever allowed herself to have.

Alex's Advice (from Frank Herbert's "Dune")

"I must not fear. Fear is the mind-killer. Fear is the little-death that brings total obliteration. I will face my fear. I will permit it to pass over me and through me."

The Result

Senta-eh decides to ride. She conquers her fear!

TOKIN-AK

Day 3: Sentence Analysis - Chapters 15-16

Examining Author's Sentences

Complete Sentence from Chapter 15

"The man turned his face to them as they approached."

  • Subject: The man
  • Verb: turned
  • Complete thought: Yes!

What If It Were a Fragment?

"As they approached." - This ALONE would be a fragment!

It has a subject (they) and verb (approached), but it's an incomplete thought because of the word "As."

The author correctly attached it to "The man turned his face to them."

Day 3: Alex's Character Insight

What Tokin-ak Notices

A Rare Quality

Tokin-ak says to Alex: "It is a rare man who, when presented with new evidence, can change his mind."

What This Means

At first, Alex was skeptical of the blind old man. How could he possibly help?

But after seeing Tokin-ak's abilities, Alex changed his mind and accepted his help.

This shows Alex is open-minded - he adjusts his beliefs when he learns new information.

What We've Learned

Element Question to Ask Example

  • What did Lanta-eh do for Tonton-ah?

Day 4: Writing Prompt 2

(For Language Arts 1-4 SC only)

Your Prompt

What makes Tokin-ak mysterious and unusual? Use details from Chapter 16 to explain.

Details to Include

  • What is his physical condition? (blind)
  • What impossible things does he know?
  • How does he explain his knowledge?
  • What does he know about Lanta-eh?

Topic Sentence Starter

"Tokin-ak is a mysterious character because he knows things he shouldn't be able to know."

Day 4: Writing Checklist

Check Your Work Before Submitting

Complete Sentence Checklist

  • Does every sentence have a SUBJECT? (Who or what?)
  • Does every sentence have a VERB? (Action or being?)
  • Does every sentence express a COMPLETE THOUGHT?
  • Did you avoid starting sentences with "Because" or "When" unless you finished the thought?

Content Checklist

  • Do you have a topic sentence that answers the question?
  • Do you have at least three details from the story?
  • Do you have a conclusion sentence?

Day 4: Model Response

Example Answer to Prompt 1

Preta-eh gives Alex and his team horses for several important reasons. First, she feels terrible that they were attacked while they were her guests. She wants to make up for the danger they faced in Tonton-ah. Second, Lanta-eh blessed Tonton-ah with good fish harvests, so Preta-eh feels she owes Alex and his team for this gift. Third, the horses will help them travel much faster on their long journey to find Lanta-eh. Because of her guilt and gratitude, Preta-eh gives them this generous gift to help their mission succeed.

Notice:

Every sentence is complete with a subject, verb, and complete thought. The last sentence starts with "Because" but finishes the thought by attaching to "Preta-eh gives them this generous gift."
