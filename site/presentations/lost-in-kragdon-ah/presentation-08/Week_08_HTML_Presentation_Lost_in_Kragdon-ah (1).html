Today's Grammar Focus: Tense Consistency

When writing a paragraph, you should use the SAME tense throughout. Jumping between tenses confuses readers!

Day 1: What Is Tense Consistency?

Keeping Your Writing Smooth

Understanding Tense Consistency

WHAT IS TENSE CONSISTENCY? Tense consistency means using the SAME verb tense throughout a paragraph or passage. If you start in past tense, STAY in past tense!
WHY IS IT IMPORTANT? Switching tenses randomly confuses readers. They won't know WHEN events are happening. Consistent tenses create a clear timeline.
HOW DO I DO IT? Before writing, decide: Am I telling about something that already happened (past), something happening now (present), or something that will happen (future)? Then stick with that choice!

Day 1: Consistent vs. Inconsistent

See the Difference

INCONSISTENT (Confusing!)

"Alex looked at Werda-ak. He notices the boy was tired. The team will stop to rest."

This jumps between past, present, and future! The reader gets confused about when things happen.

CONSISTENT (Clear!)

"Alex looked at Werda-ak. He noticed the boy was tired. The team stopped to rest."

All verbs are in PAST tense. The reader knows all events already happened!

The Golden Rule

Pick ONE tense for your paragraph and stick with it. Check every verb to make sure it matches!

Day 1: Signal Words

Words That Tell Us Which Tense to Use

PAST Signal Words

  • yesterday
  • last week/month/year
  • before
  • earlier
  • once
  • ago
  • already

PRESENT Signal Words

  • today
  • now
  • currently
  • at this moment
  • always
  • every day
  • right now

FUTURE Signal Words

  • tomorrow
  • next week/month/year
  • later
  • soon
  • eventually
  • in the future
  • someday

Signal Words Are Clues!

When you see a signal word, it tells you which tense to use. "Yesterday, Alex climbed." "Tomorrow, they will reach the mountain."

Day 1: Practice - Fix the Inconsistency

Find and Fix the Tense Errors

Example 1:

"Yesterday, Monda-ak catches a rabbit and brings it to Alex."

Problem: "Yesterday" is a PAST signal word, but "catches" and "brings" are present tense!

"Yesterday, Monda-ak caught a rabbit and brought it to Alex."

Example 2:

"Tomorrow, they climbed the mountain."

Problem: "Tomorrow" is a FUTURE signal word, but "climbed" is past tense!

"Tomorrow, they will climb the mountain."

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 1

ELEVATION

Definition: The height of a place above sea level; how high up something is.

Understanding Elevation

WHAT IS IT? Elevation measures how high something is compared to sea level. Mountains have high elevation; valleys have low elevation.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? The path "rose in elevation" as they climbed toward Hakun-ah. Higher elevation meant fewer predators but colder temperatures.
HOW DO I USE IT? "The mountain has an elevation of 10,000 feet above sea level."

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 2

REVERIE

Definition: A state of being lost in pleasant thoughts; daydreaming.

Understanding Reverie

WHAT IS IT? A reverie is when your mind drifts away into deep thought or daydreaming. You're awake but lost in your own thoughts.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? Alex "was snapped out of his reverie" when Senta-eh shouted. He had been thinking deeply about battles and strategies.
HOW DO I USE IT? "The student was lost in a reverie until the teacher called her name."

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 3

MONASTERY

Definition: A building where monks or other religious people live, work, and pray together.

Understanding Monastery

WHAT IS IT? A monastery is a community of religious people (like monks) who live together, often in a remote or secluded place.
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? Hakun-ah is a monastery high in the mountains where the holy men live. Alex and his team will spend the entire winter there.
HOW DO I USE IT? "The ancient monastery sat on top of the mountain, far from any town."

Day 2: Vocabulary Words 4-6

TRIBUTE

Definition: A payment or gift given to show respect or as a form of thanks.

"Some tribes paid tribute to the monastery by bringing them meat several times a year."

REVITALIZED

Definition: Given new life, energy, or strength; refreshed and renewed.

"After the winter rest, humans and animals alike were revitalized."

THAW

Definition: When frozen things (like snow or ice) melt and become warmer; the end of a cold period.

Day 2: Subject-Verb Agreement Review

Making Subjects and Verbs Match in Every Tense

Subject-Verb Agreement Across Tenses

WHAT IS IT? Subject-verb agreement means the verb must match whether the subject is singular (one) or plural (more than one). This rule applies in ALL tenses!
WHY IS IT IMPORTANT? Incorrect agreement sounds wrong and makes writing unclear. "He walk" sounds wrong; "He walks" is correct.
HOW DO I DO IT? Check: Is the subject singular or plural? Then choose the verb form that matches. This is especially tricky in present tense!

Day 2: Subject-Verb Agreement in Each Tense

The Rules for Singular and Plural

Agreement Across All Tenses

Tense Singular Subject Plural Subject
Past Alex walked They walked
Present Alex walks (add -s!) They walk (no -s)
Future Alex will walk They will walk

Tricky Part: Present Tense!

In present tense, singular subjects (he, she, it, Alex) need verbs ending in -s. Plural subjects (they, we, the monks) do NOT add -s.

  • The monk walks slowly. (singular - add -s)
  • The monks walk slowly. (plural - no -s)

Day 2: When Tense Shifts Are Okay

Dialogue Is Different!

Acceptable Tense Shift in Dialogue

"Alex looked at Tokin-ak and asked, 'Where will we go tomorrow?'"

The narration (who said it) stays in past tense. The dialogue (what was said) can use present or future because that's what the character actually said!

The Rule

  • Narration: Keep in ONE tense (usually past for stories)
  • Dialogue: Use whatever tense the character would actually say
  • Don't mix tenses WITHIN narration or WITHIN one character's speech

Example from the Story:

"Tokin-ak said, 'We will talk tonight, then you are free to do whatever you want.'"

Narration: past ("said"). Dialogue: future and present (what Tokin-ak actually says).

Day 4: Writing Workshop

Putting It All Together

Today's Goals:

Day 4: Writing Prompt

Your Prompt

Why did Alex and his team have to stay at Hakun-ah for the winter? What did they do during this time?

Details to Include

  • Why couldn't they continue their journey?
  • What did each team member do during the winter?
  • What did Alex learn from the monks?
  • What gift did Tokin-ak give Alex?

Topic Sentence Starter

"Alex and his team had to stay at Hakun-ah because the mountain pass was blocked by snow."

Day 4: Writing Structure

Building Your Response

Remember Your Writing Structure

  • Topic Sentence: State WHY they had to stay (snow blocked the pass)
  • Supporting Detail 1: What Werda-ak did (rested, made friends)
  • Supporting Detail 2: What Alex did (chopped wood, learned chaimi)
  • Supporting Detail 3: What happened when spring came (thaw, pendant gift)
  • Conclusion: Summarize the winter stay and their readiness to continue

Verb Tense Reminders!

  • Use past tense throughout (events already happened)
  • Check subject-verb agreement in every sentence
  • Watch out for irregular verbs: had (not "haved"), taught (not "teached"), gave (not "gived")

Day 4: Writing Checklist

Check Your Work Before Submitting

Tense Consistency Checklist

  • Are ALL your verbs in past tense?
  • Did you avoid switching to present or future in narration?
  • Did you use correct irregular past tense forms?

Subject-Verb Agreement Checklist

  • Does every singular subject have a matching verb?
  • Does every plural subject have a matching verb?
  • In present tense (if any), did you add -s for he/she/it?

Content Checklist

  • Do you have a topic sentence that answers the question?
  • Do you have at least three details from the story?
  • Do you have a conclusion sentence?

Day 4: Model Response

Example Answer

Alex and his team had to stay at Hakun-ah because the mountain pass was blocked by snow and ice. First, Tokin-ak explained that they could either freeze trying to cross or wait for the thaw. Second, each team member spent the winter productively: Werda-ak rested his leg and made friends, Senta-eh crafted new arrows, and Alex chopped wood and learned chaimi from the monks. Third, when spring finally arrived, Tokin-ak gave Alex a special pendant that might help him in a "time of need." The winter at Hakun-ah revitalized the entire team and prepared them to continue their journey.

Notice:

Every verb is in past tense. Subject-verb agreement is correct throughout. The irregular verbs (had, was, spent, made, gave, arrived) are all in correct past tense form!
