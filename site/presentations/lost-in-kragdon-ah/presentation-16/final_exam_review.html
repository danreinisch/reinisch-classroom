Verb Tenses

Skill 1 of 6 — Keeping your verbs consistent

The Three Tenses

  • Past tense = already happened → walked, ran, built
  • Present tense = happening now → walks, runs, builds
  • Future tense = will happen → will walk, will run, will build

The Rule

All verbs in a sentence should match the same tense unless there is a clear reason for a shift.

Tense Shift Error

"The warrior drew his sword and charges forward."

→ "drew" is past, but "charges" is present. They don't match!

Verb Tenses — Practice Strategy

Skill 1 of 6 — How to spot tense errors on the exam

Step-by-Step Approach

  • Step 1: Find every verb (action word) in the sentence.
  • Step 2: Label each one — is it past, present, or future?
  • Step 3: Do they all match? If ONE is different, that's your answer.
  • Step 4: Look for "will" — it always signals future tense.

Try This

"The explorer mapped the trail and marks each turn on his notebook."

→ "mapped" = past. "marks" = present. The error is "marks" — it should be "marked."

Context Clues & Word Parts

Skill 2 of 6 — Figuring out words you don't know

Context Clues

The words around an unfamiliar word give you clues about its meaning.

Example

"The famished travelers ate everything on their plates within minutes."

→ Clue: "ate everything within minutes" = very hungry

Word Parts

  • Prefix = beginning → un-, re-, pre-, mis-, dis-
  • Root = core meaning → port (carry), struct (build)
  • Suffix = ending → -ful, -less, -able, -ment, -tion

Break it down

un + break + able = not able to be broken

Context Clues — Practice Strategy

Skill 2 of 6 — Key prefixes, roots, and suffixes to know

Common Prefixes

  • un- = not → unhappy = not happy
  • re- = again → rebuild = build again
  • pre- = before → preview = view before
  • mis- = wrong → misjudge = judge wrongly
  • dis- = opposite → disappear = opposite of appear
  • anti- = against → antithesis = against/opposite

Common Suffixes

  • -ful = full of → hopeful = full of hope
  • -less = without → fearless = without fear
  • -able = can be done → breakable = can be broken
  • -ment = result of → enjoyment = result of enjoying
  • -tion = act of → exploration = act of exploring

Exam Tip

If you see a word you don't know, break it apart first. Then look at the sentence around it for more clues.

Sentence Structure & Transitions

Skill 3 of 6 — Complete sentences, fragments, run-ons, and connectors

Three Sentence Types

  • Complete Sentence = subject + verb + complete thought
  • Fragment = missing a subject, verb, or complete thought
  • Run-On = two thoughts smashed together without punctuation

Spot the difference

✓ "The messenger delivered the scroll at dawn."

✗ "Delivering the scroll at dawn."

✗ "The messenger arrived he delivered the scroll."

Transition Word Types

Time Order

first, next, then, finally

Contrast

however, but, although, on the other hand

Cause/Result

therefore, as a result, consequently, so

Addition

also, in addition, furthermore

Transitions — Practice Strategy

Skill 3 of 6 — Choosing the right connector

Ask Yourself: What is the RELATIONSHIP between the two sentences?

  • Are they in order? → Use then, next, finally
  • Is there a surprise or change? → Use however, nevertheless, although
  • Did one cause the other? → Use as a result, therefore, consequently
  • Are they adding similar ideas? → Use also, in addition, furthermore

Try This

"The scouts found footprints in the mud. _______, they followed the trail deeper into the forest."

→ Relationship: time order / sequence → Answer: Then

Making Inferences

Skill 4 of 6 — Reading between the lines

What Is an Inference?

An inference is a conclusion you figure out by combining:

  • What the text SAYS (the clues the author gives you)
  • What you already KNOW (your background knowledge)

The answer is NOT stated directly — you have to figure it out from the evidence.

What to Look For

  • Character actions — What do they DO? Why?
  • Details the author chose — Why include this detail?
  • Emotions and reactions — How does the character feel?
  • What's missing — What does the author NOT say?

Example

"Ella grabbed two extra blankets and stacked firewood by the door."

→ Inference: She is preparing for cold weather.

Making Inferences — Practice Strategy

Skill 4 of 6 — Text clues + background knowledge = inference

The Inference Formula

  • Step 1: Read the passage carefully. Underline key details.
  • Step 2: Ask: "What do ALL of these details have in common?"
  • Step 3: Think: "Based on what I know about the real world, what makes sense?"
  • Step 4: Choose the answer that is SUPPORTED by the text — not just a guess.

Try This

"The old man polished the medal every evening. He kept it in a velvet box on the mantle. Once, when a visitor tried to pick it up, he gently but firmly took it back."

→ All details point to the same conclusion: the medal has deep personal meaning to him.

Cause & Effect

Skill 5 of 6 — Why it happened and what happened as a result

Cause vs. Effect

  • CAUSE = the REASON / WHY something happens
  • EFFECT = the RESULT / WHAT happens

Example

"Because the river flooded, the village had to evacuate."

Cause = flood | Effect = evacuation

Signal Words

because since due to

↑ These point to the CAUSE

so therefore as a result

↑ These point to the EFFECT

Chain of events

Sometimes: A causes B, B causes C, C causes D...

Cause & Effect — Practice Strategy

Skill 5 of 6 — Finding the signal words

Step-by-Step Approach

  • Step 1: Find the signal word (because, so, since, as a result).
  • Step 2: "Because" and "since" point backward to the cause.
  • Step 3: "So," "therefore," and "as a result" point forward to the effect.
  • Step 4: For chains: read the passage in order — each event triggers the next.

Try This

"The well dried up during the heat wave, so the shepherds moved their flock to the eastern hills."

→ "so" points forward to the effect. Cause = well dried up. Effect = shepherds moved.

Compare & Contrast

Skill 6 of 6 — Finding similarities and differences

Compare vs. Contrast

  • COMPARE = how things are ALIKE
  • CONTRAST = how things are DIFFERENT

Compare

"Both warriors carried shields into battle."

Contrast

"One fought with a sword; however, the other used a bow."

Signal Words to Know

Comparison (alike):

both similarly also alike likewise

Contrast (different):

however but unlike conversely on the other hand

Compare & Contrast — Practice Strategy

Skill 6 of 6 — What to look for on the exam

Step-by-Step Approach

  • Step 1: Identify the TWO things being compared or contrasted.
  • Step 2: Look for signal words (both, similarly, however, unlike).
  • Step 3: Ask: "What is the SAME?" and "What is DIFFERENT?"
  • Step 4: For character contrasts — look at what each person DOES in the same situation and what the RESULT is.

Try This

"Olen wrote his speech weeks in advance and practiced every night. His classmate Dara wrote hers the night before. Both of them, however, received applause from the audience."

→ Different: preparation style. Same: both got applause.

Question 25: Written Response

Writing a Strong Paragraph

What You'll Write

A one-paragraph response with:

  • Topic Sentence — tells the reader what the paragraph is about
  • Three Supporting Details — each starts with a transition word
  • Conclusion Sentence — wraps up and connects back to the topic

The prompt will ask you to describe something fun and creative!

Transition Words to Use

First Next Then After that Finally Also In addition

Example Structure

Topic: "If I could go anywhere, I would visit Japan."

Detail 1: "First, I would explore the temples in Kyoto..."

Detail 2: "Next, I would try authentic ramen..."

Detail 3: "Finally, I would ride the bullet train..."

Conclusion: "Japan would be the perfect trip because..."

Writing Checklist

Check these before you submit Question 25

Before You Submit, Check:

  • ☐ Does my topic sentence introduce the main idea?
  • ☐ Do I have three supporting details?
  • ☐ Does each detail start with a transition word?
  • ☐ Does my conclusion sentence wrap it up?
  • ☐ Does every sentence have a subject and a verb?
  • ☐ Did I check verb tense consistency?
  • ☐ Did I use correct capitalization and punctuation?
