Skills We Are Building

A CAUSE is WHY something happens. An EFFECT is WHAT happens as a result. This week, every decision triggers consequences — and one effect often becomes the cause of the NEXT event, creating a chain reaction.

Preview: This Week's Master Chain

Skill Reminder: Chain Reactions

In a chain, one EFFECT becomes the CAUSE of the next event. Strong readers trace these links to understand how one decision can lead to massive consequences.

Real-Life Cause and Effect

CAUSE: You stay up late playing video games.

EFFECT: You oversleep and miss the bus.

THAT EFFECT BECOMES A CAUSE: You miss the bus.

NEW EFFECT: You are late to school and miss the test review.

In stories (and in life), effects often become the cause of the next event. Good readers trace these chains to understand how one small decision can lead to enormous consequences.

Day 1: Author's Craft — Self-Doubt

The author includes Alex's inner thoughts: "Will this really work?" and "There are so many things that can go wrong."

CAUSE: The author shows us Alex's private doubts

EFFECT on the READER: We feel MORE tension — even HE is not sure the plan will work. Uncertainty creates suspense.

Skill Reminder: Authors Use Cause and Effect to Build Tension

When a character doubts their own plan, the reader doubts it too. That doubt CAUSES us to keep reading — we need to know if it works.

Day 1: The No-Fire Decision

CAUSE: Alex decides NOT to build a campfire

EFFECT: They stay hidden — no smoke for guards to investigate

What IF he HAD built a fire?

POSSIBLE EFFECT: Guards see smoke → investigate → find the group → mission fails before it starts

Sometimes the most important cause-and-effect chains are the ones that DIDN'T happen. Alex's decision to NOT do something prevented a chain of disaster.

Day 1: Discussion

Day 2: The Master Cause-and-Effect Chain

Birds released with burning cloth

→ Coops catch fire (wood + dry summer)

→ Fires spread to nearby wooden buildings

→ Citizens scream "Fire!" — total chaos

→ Gate guards run to help fight fires

→ Gate is unguarded — Alex walks through

→ Spy recognizes them — Senta-eh kills him

→ Guards notice — chase begins

→ Race to town hall — fight through guards and dogs

ONE decision (releasing the birds) created a chain of NINE events. That is cause and effect in action.

Day 2: "Working Too Well"

Unintended Consequences

Alex's INTENTION: Light a few fires as a distraction

ACTUAL EFFECT: The entire city burns — all buildings are wood, end of dry summer

Sometimes the EFFECT is much bigger than the CAUSE intended. Alex did not plan to destroy the city — but once 47 birds scattered to 47 locations in tinder-dry conditions, the fire was beyond anyone's control.

Skill Reminder: Unintended Consequences

An unintended consequence is an EFFECT that was NOT planned or expected. The cause was deliberate, but the full effect went beyond what anyone predicted. This happens in stories AND in real life.

Day 3: The Final Fight — Cause and Effect

Draka-ak is fresh; Alex is exhausted

→ Fight favors Draka-ak — each blow gets closer

→ Alex recognizes he will lose a long fight

→ He fakes a stumble to look weak

→ Draka-ak sees "weakness" and rushes in

→ Alex throws his ax into Draka-ak's face

Alex used Draka-ak's own arrogance against him. The fake stumble CAUSED Draka-ak to drop his guard, which CAUSED his death. Intelligence defeated strength.

Skill Reminder: Character Traits Can Be CAUSES

Draka-ak's arrogance is not just a personality trait — it is the CAUSE of his defeat. When a character flaw leads to a consequence, that is cause and effect driven by character.

Day 3: Author's Craft — "Bad Action Movie"

The author writes: "If it had been a bad action movie, the villain would have been holding the girl as a shield."

WHY the author does this: It shows Draka-ak is a REAL, confident villain — not a cliched movie villain. This makes the fight feel more dangerous because we know he believes he can win.

Day 4: Writing With Cause and Effect

Prompt

Write about a cause-and-effect chain from Chapters 38-40. Start with one CAUSE, explain the EFFECT, then explain how that effect CAUSED something else. Use signal words like "because," "as a result," "which led to," or "therefore."

Paragraph Structure

  • Sentence 1: State the original CAUSE and its immediate EFFECT.
  • Sentence 2: Explain how that effect CAUSED something else to happen.
  • Sentence 3: Continue the chain one more step (or wrap up).

Day 4: Sentence Starters

Use These to Get Started

"Because [cause], [effect] happened."

"[Event] happened, which led to [next event]."

"As a result of [cause], [effect] occurred."

"This caused [next event], which then..."

"Therefore, the final outcome was..."

Day 4: Model Response

Because Alex released 47 birds with burning cloth attached to their legs, fires broke out across the city as a result of the birds landing on wooden structures in tinder-dry conditions. This caused the gate guards to abandon their posts to help fight the fires, which led to Alex's group being able to walk through the unguarded gate without being stopped. Therefore, one creative decision set off a chain reaction that allowed three people and a dog to infiltrate a city of thousands.

What Makes This Strong

  • Traces THREE steps in the chain (birds → fires → guards leave → group enters)
  • Uses FOUR signal words (because, as a result, this caused, which led to, therefore)
  • Explains WHY each step leads to the next

Day 4: Writing Checklist

Cause-and-Effect Writing Checklist

  • Did you identify a clear CAUSE?
  • Did you explain the EFFECT?
  • Did you show how the effect CAUSED something else? (chain)
  • Did you use at least ONE signal word (because, so, as a result, which led to)?

Grammar Checklist

  • Capital letter at the start of every sentence?
  • Punctuation at the end of every sentence?
  • Every sentence is COMPLETE? (Subject + Verb + Complete Thought)

Reference: Skill Definitions

Cause and Effect

Cause: WHY something happens — the trigger, decision, or event that starts a chain.

Effect: WHAT happens as a result — the consequence or outcome.

Chain reaction: When one effect becomes the cause of the NEXT event, creating a linked sequence.

Unintended Consequences

An effect that was NOT planned or expected. The cause was deliberate, but the full outcome went beyond what anyone predicted. Example: Alex intended a distraction — he got a citywide inferno.

Reference: Step-by-Step Strategies

How to Identify Cause and Effect

1) Find an EVENT in the text. 2) Ask: "WHY did this happen?" — that is the cause. 3) Ask: "WHAT happened because of it?" — that is the effect. 4) Check: Does the effect become a cause for something else? If yes, you have a chain.

How to Trace a Chain

1) Start with the FIRST decision or event. 2) Ask "What happened next BECAUSE of this?" 3) Write it down. 4) Now ask the SAME question about the new event. 5) Repeat until you reach the final outcome. 6) Count the links — the more links, the bigger the chain.

How to Write About Cause and Effect

1) State the original cause clearly. 2) Use a signal word (because, as a result, which led to). 3) State the effect. 4) If you can, show how that effect caused the NEXT thing. 5) Wrap up with the final outcome or consequence.

How to Spot Unintended Consequences

1) Identify what the character INTENDED to do. 2) Identify what ACTUALLY happened. 3) Compare: Was the result bigger, different, or worse than planned? 4) If yes, that is an unintended consequence.
