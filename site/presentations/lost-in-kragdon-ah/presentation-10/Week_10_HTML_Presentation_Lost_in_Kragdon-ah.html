Today's ELA Focus: Complete Sentences

Every complete sentence needs THREE things: a subject, a verb, and a complete thought.

Day 1: What Makes a Complete Sentence?

The Three Essential Parts

Understanding Complete Sentences

WHAT IS A COMPLETE SENTENCE? A complete sentence has THREE parts: a SUBJECT (who or what), a VERB (action or being word), and a COMPLETE THOUGHT (makes sense on its own).
WHY DOES IT MATTER? Incomplete sentences (fragments) confuse readers. They leave questions unanswered. Complete sentences communicate clearly!
HOW DO I CHECK? Ask: "Who or what is doing something?" (subject), "What are they doing?" (verb), "Does this make sense by itself?" (complete thought).

Day 1: Subject + Verb + Complete Thought

Breaking Down the Parts

Example from Chapter 29

"Alex waved goodbye to Trema-ak."

  • Subject: Alex (who is doing the action)
  • Verb: waved (the action)
  • Complete thought: We know what happened - it makes sense alone

Fragment (Incomplete)

"The enormous city with hundreds of buildings."

Problem: No verb! What about the city?

Complete Sentence

"The enormous city had hundreds of buildings."

Fixed: Added the verb "had"!

Day 1: Fragments vs. Complete Sentences

Fixing Incomplete Sentences

Is This Complete or a Fragment?

Example 1: "Running toward the cliff."

FRAGMENT - No subject! Who is running?

Fixed: "Senta-eh was running toward the cliff."

Example 2:

"The moose with her calf."

FRAGMENT - No verb! What about the moose?

Fixed: "The moose protected her calf."

Example 3:

"Because the city was so big."

FRAGMENT - Incomplete thought! What happened because of this?

Fixed: "Because the city was so big, Alex was amazed."

Day 1: Capitalization and End Punctuation

Starting and Ending Sentences Correctly

The Rules

  • Capital Letter: Every sentence STARTS with a capital letter
  • Period (.): Use for statements - "They reached the city."
  • Question Mark (?): Use for questions - "Where is the ship?"
  • Exclamation Point (!): Use for excitement or strong emotion - "The moose was enormous!"

Examples from the Story

Statement: They traveled for forty-seven days.

Question: Have you been here before?

Exclamation: Run!

Day 2: Three Types of Sentences

Simple, Compound, and Complex

Simple Sentence

ONE complete thought

"Alex saw the city."

Subject + Verb

Compound Sentence

TWO complete thoughts joined by and, but, or, so

"Alex saw the city, and he was amazed."

Complex Sentence

One complete thought + one dependent part

"Because the city was huge, Alex was amazed."

Day 2: Compound Sentences

Joining Two Ideas

Understanding Compound Sentences

WHAT IS A COMPOUND SENTENCE? Two complete sentences (independent clauses) joined together by a COMMA + CONJUNCTION (and, but, or, so, yet).
WHY USE THEM? They show how two ideas are connected. They make writing more interesting than many short, choppy sentences.
HOW DO I WRITE ONE? Take two related sentences. Add a COMMA. Add a conjunction (and, but, or, so). Combine!

Example

Two Simple: "Werda-ak threw the rock. The moose charged."

Compound: "Werda-ak threw the rock, and the moose charged."

Day 2: Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

Words That Join Sentences

Remember FANBOYS

Letter Conjunction Use Example
F For Reason He ran, for the moose was charging.
A And Addition She climbed, and he distracted.
N Nor Negative She couldn't climb, nor could she jump.
B But Contrast The city was big, but Alex wasn't scared.
O Or Choice They could stay, or they could leave.
Y Yet Contrast He was tired, yet he kept going.
S So Result They were hungry, so they found food.

Day 2: Transition Words

Connecting Ideas Smoothly

Understanding Transition Words

WHAT ARE TRANSITION WORDS? Words or phrases that connect ideas and show the relationship between sentences or paragraphs. They act like bridges!
WHY ARE THEY IMPORTANT? They help readers follow your thinking. Without transitions, writing feels choppy and disconnected.
HOW DO I USE THEM? Place them at the beginning of a sentence (followed by a comma) or use them to connect two sentences.

Day 2: Types of Transition Words

Different Transitions for Different Purposes

Transition Word Categories

Purpose Transition Words
Time/Sequence First, Next, Then, After, Finally, Later, Meanwhile
Addition Also, Additionally, Furthermore, Moreover, In addition
Contrast However, But, Although, On the other hand, Instead
Cause/Effect Because, Therefore, As a result, So, Consequently
Example For example, For instance, Such as, Specifically
Conclusion In conclusion, Finally, Overall, In summary

Day 2: Vocabulary Word 1

UNSCATHED

Definition: Without being harmed or injured; unhurt.

Understanding Unscathed

WHAT IS IT? Unscathed means to escape from danger without any harm or injury. "Scathed" is an old word for "harmed."
WHY IS IT IN THE STORY? "They had a few close calls on the trail—run-ins with both animals and people, but they emerged unscathed." They weren't hurt!
WORD PARTS: UN- (not) + SCATHED (harmed) = NOT harmed

Day 2: Vocabulary Words 2-4

PREDICAMENT

Definition: A difficult, unpleasant, or embarrassing situation.

"Senta-eh was a warrior... but she was in a predicament." (Stuck on a cliff with a giant moose below!)

BUREAUCRACY

Definition: A system of government with many rules, officials, and procedures.

"They have a bureaucracy. Something I never thought I would see in this world." (Guards, rules, paperwork)

NEGOTIATE

Definition: To discuss and try to reach an agreement; to bargain.

"Alex tried to negotiate for passage on the ship." (He bargained for a deal)

Day 3: The Negotiation

Alex Gets Outsmarted

Trading for Passage

Alex tried to negotiate for passage to Lasta-ah (the correct name for Lusta-ah). The captain kept outmaneuvering him!

What Alex Learned (The Hard Way)

  • Their beautiful horses were worth LESS than ugly work horses here
  • He traded horses + saddlebags for passage... but didn't negotiate for ROOMS
  • He didn't negotiate to board the ship that night - had to sleep outside
  • He didn't negotiate for good food - got watery gruel instead of stew!

Transition Words in Action

"First, Alex offered the horses. However, the captain said they weren't valuable enough. Finally, they made a deal."

Day 3: Chapter 31 - The Ship

Life on Okrent-ah

Their "Accommodations"

  • No room - they stayed in the back with the animals!
  • Three milk beasts and six horses shared their space
  • They had to shovel animal droppings overboard
  • Food was watery gruel while other passengers got stew
  • Senta-eh got seasick from the rocking boat

A Surprise Visitor

Versa-eh appeared on the ship! She had left the man she was supposed to marry (he was old, smelled bad, and beat his other wives). She stole some of his valuables and used them to pay for the team's food for the rest of the voyage!

Day 3: The Man in the Robe

A Clue on the Ship

Discovery

Werda-ak noticed a man on the ship wearing a robe lined with the SAME fabric they found on the dead soldier at the beginning of their journey! This suggests the man might be from Lasta-ah - meaning they're heading in the right direction.

Alex's Decision

Alex decided NOT to approach the man. If the man figured out who they were, he might warn Lasta-ah that they were coming. Better to watch quietly from a distance.

Day 3: Using Transitions in Paragraphs

Connecting Ideas in Your Writing

Without Transitions (Choppy)

"Alex tried to negotiate. The captain was smarter. Alex didn't get a room. He had to sleep with animals."

With Transitions (Smooth)

"First, Alex tried to negotiate for passage. However, the captain was smarter and kept outmaneuvering him. As a result, Alex didn't get a room. Instead, he had to sleep with the animals."

Notice the Difference!

Transitions show HOW ideas connect: time order, contrast, cause/effect, or addition.

Day 3: Sentence Variety

Mixing Simple, Compound, and Complex

Example Paragraph with Variety

"Simple: The ship departed at dawn.

Compound: Senta-eh felt sick, and the smell of animals didn't help.

Complex: Although the journey was uncomfortable, they were finally heading toward Lasta-ah."

Why Mix Sentence Types?

  • Simple sentences: Clear, direct, punchy
  • Compound sentences: Show connections between equal ideas
  • Complex sentences: Show cause/effect, time, or conditions

Using all three makes writing more interesting!

  • Complete sentences: Subject + Verb + Complete Thought

Day 4: Writing Prompt

Your Prompt

What challenges did Alex and his team face when they arrived at Grinta-ah and boarded the ship?

Details to Include

  • What surprised them about the city?
  • What happened when Alex negotiated for passage?
  • What were conditions like on the ship?
  • How did Versa-eh help them?

Topic Sentence Starter

"Alex and his team faced many challenges when they arrived at Grinta-ah and boarded the ship to Lasta-ah."

Day 4: Writing Structure with Transitions

Building Your Response

Your Paragraph Structure

  • Topic Sentence: State the main idea (challenges they faced)
  • First, Supporting Detail 1: Describe the city and its rules
  • Next, Supporting Detail 2: Explain the negotiation problems
  • Additionally, Supporting Detail 3: Describe conditions on the ship
  • However, Contrast/Help: Explain how Versa-eh helped
  • In conclusion, Conclusion: Sum up the challenges

Vocabulary to Include

unscathed, predicament, bureaucracy, negotiate

Day 4: Writing Checklist

Check Your Work Before Submitting

Sentence Structure Checklist

  • Is every sentence COMPLETE? (Subject + Verb + Complete Thought)
  • Did you use at least ONE compound sentence? (with and, but, or, so)
  • Does every sentence start with a CAPITAL letter?
  • Does every sentence end with proper PUNCTUATION? (. ? !)

Transition Checklist

  • Did you use at least THREE transition words?
  • Do your transitions show the RIGHT relationship? (time, contrast, cause/effect)
  • Did you put a COMMA after transition words at the start of sentences?

Day 4: Model Response

Example Answer

Alex and his team faced many challenges when they arrived at Grinta-ah and boarded the ship to Lasta-ah. First, the city was unlike anything they had seen before, with guards, rules, and a bureaucracy that threatened to put troublemakers in chains. Next, Alex tried to negotiate for passage on a ship, but the captain outsmarted him at every turn. As a result, they traded their beautiful horses for passage but ended up with no room and terrible food. Additionally, they had to sleep in the back of the ship with animals and shovel manure overboard. Senta-eh got seasick, and the smell only made it worse. However, Versa-eh surprised them by appearing on the ship after escaping her arranged marriage. She paid for their food for the rest of the voyage. In conclusion, although Grinta-ah presented many challenges, they emerged unscathed and continued their journey toward Lasta-ah.
