Day 1: Chapters 45 & 46

The Journey Home + Across the Plains, to Home

Chapter 45 Summary — The Journey Home

The journey home from Lasta-ah is much safer than the trip there. Lanta-eh's presence seems to protect them — people flock to her at every stop. They arrive at Winten-ah on the summer solstice. New children play in the fields. Sekun-ak leads the village. Dan Hadaller is alive, but frail, carried on another man's back.

Chapter 46 Summary — Across the Plains, to Home

Dan writes his life story in a leather-bound book, then dies peacefully. Alex says goodbye to Lanta-eh (with a hug) and Senta-eh (by holding her hand). An honor guard escorts him across the plains. They fight off the ronit-ta one final time. Alex arrives at the beach — but the door is gone.

Arriving at Winten-ah

Chapter 45 — The Homecoming

They arrive on the summer solstice. The guard Balta-ak blows three sharp horn blasts. The call echoes from guard to guard all the way back to the village.

What Has Changed?

Winten-ah is both the same and different. The cliffs, cooking fires, and swaying grass are familiar. But the community is no longer mourning — new children play in the fields. Sekun-ak is now the established chief.

Emotional Moment

Watching young boys wrestle brings Werda-ak so vividly into Alex's mind that he cannot hold back tears. The loss is still fresh.

Dan Hadaller

Chapter 45 — An Old Friend

Dan Hadaller is alive — but he is frail, mostly bald, and has to be carried on another man's back down the cliff. He does not look well.

Alex's Promise

Alex tells Dan: "I'm going home soon. I'm taking you with me." He has brought Dan a present from Lasta-ah — courtesy of Versa-eh. Alex gives Dan a small leather-bound book and two crude pencils so Dan can write down his memories.

Discussion: Inference

What can you figure out about Dan's health based on how the author describes him? What clues does the text give us?

Celebrations & Gifts

Chapter 45 — Winten-ah Rejoices

Everyone wants to touch the heroes. Then all attention turns to Lanta-eh — the golden child, the Chosen One, returned at last.

The Horses

Karga-ah, the animal keeper, claims the three horses Versa-eh gave them. He is thrilled: "I can breed these! We will have beautiful animals for generations!" He seems more excited about the horses than Lanta-eh's return.

Discussion: Compare & Contrast

Compare how different characters react to the homecoming. How does Sekun-ak's reaction differ from Karga-ah's? What does each reaction tell you about that character?

Goodbyes

Chapter 46 — Letting Go

Alex Says Goodbye

  • Lanta-eh: She hugs him — the first hug he has received in six years. She says: "Hold on to your doubting ways. That is what makes you who you are."
  • Senta-eh: Alex holds her hand. She grips it fiercely. A deep well of pain is visible in her eyes. Then she stands and walks away.
  • Sekun-ak: Alex salutes him. Sekun-ak mimics it sharply.

Another Loss

Dan Hadaller — the man the tribe knew as Vanda-ak ("he who runs") — writes his life story for four days, then dies peacefully. Lanta-eh herself presides over his Winten-ah burial ceremony.

Discussion: Compare & Contrast

Compare how Alex says goodbye to Lanta-eh versus Senta-eh. What does each goodbye tell you about their relationship?

The Final Journey

Chapter 46 — Across the Plains

An honor guard of a dozen warriors, led by Senta-eh, escorts Alex across the plains. Sekun-ak himself joins the party.

The Ronit-ta

Halfway across the plain, the giant dire wolves attack from all sides. Senta-eh orders the bowmen to fire. Three arrows connect — one wolf goes down, two others yelp and run. The pack breaks.

Memories

Alex remembers his second day in Kragdon-ah — when he was a prisoner, not a hero. He saved Janta-ak from a ronit-ta with a fallen cudgel. Janta-ak later died at the battle of Denta-ah. "So many deaths."

The Door

Chapter 46 — The Ending

The Final Moment

Alex turns the corner to the beach. He can smell the salt water. His pulse races. He has waited six years for this moment.

"The door was gone."

After six years in Kragdon-ah, after a two-year rescue mission, after losing Werda-ak and Dan — Alex's way home has vanished.

Vocabulary — Chapters 45 & 46

Context Clues Practice

Perilous

Full of danger or risk — "not nearly as perilous as the trip had been"

Benediction

A blessing or prayer of good wishes — "she gave benedictions and blessings"

Relinquish

To give up or hand over willingly — "Senta-eh was more than happy to relinquish"

Foreclosed

When a bank takes back a house because payments were missed

Recuperate

To recover from injury or illness — "where Alex had recuperated after the sickness"

Verdant

Green and lush with growing plants — "a verdant valley"

Waned

Decreased or got weaker — "Dan's strength waned instead of improved"

Honor guard

A group of soldiers chosen to escort someone important as a sign of respect

Skill: Context Clues

Use the words around the unfamiliar word to figure out its meaning. The author often gives clues in the same sentence or the sentence right next to it.

Day 1 Discussion

Chapters 45 & 46 — Think About It

Inference

Why might the journey home be safer with Lanta-eh along? Why does Alex think about taking Senta-eh home with him, then let the idea go?

Cause & Effect

What causes Alex to cry when he sees the boys wrestling? What chain of events leads to the final moment on the beach?

Compare & Contrast

Compare Winten-ah when Alex left to how it looks when he returns. What is the same? What is different?

Sentence Structure

The author ends the book with four words: "The door was gone." Why is such a short sentence so powerful? How does its length match how Alex feels?

Skill Review: Making Inferences

Week 12 · Chapters 35–37

What Is It?

Using clues in the text + what you already know to figure out things the author does not say directly. Like a detective putting together evidence.

Example from the Book

In Chapter 45, Dan is carried on another man's back and described as "bent and broken." The author never says Dan is dying — but you can infer it from these clues.

Skill Review: Cause & Effect

Week 13 · Chapters 38–40

What Is It?

A CAUSE is WHY something happens. An EFFECT is WHAT happens as a result. One event leads to the next in a chain.

Example from the Book

CAUSE: Alex releases burning birds → EFFECT: Fires break out → CAUSE: Guards leave posts → EFFECT: Gate is unguarded → Alex enters the city.

Skill Review: Compare & Contrast

Week 14 · Chapters 41–44

What Is It?

Compare = how two things are ALIKE. Contrast = how two things are DIFFERENT. Characters, events, settings, and ideas can all be compared.

Example from the Book

Compare: Werda-ak and Dan both die on this journey. Contrast: Werda-ak dies suddenly in battle, while Dan dies peacefully after finishing his life story.

Skill Review: Context Clues

Week 9 · Chapters 26–28

What Is It?

Using surrounding words and sentences to figure out the meaning of unfamiliar words. Breaking words into prefixes, roots, and suffixes.

Example from the Book

"She gave benedictions and blessings as naturally as Monda-ak slobbered." — The word "blessings" right next to "benedictions" tells you they mean almost the same thing.

Skill Review: Sentence Structure

Weeks 10–11 · Chapters 29–34

What Is It?

Understanding how sentences are built and how transition words (because, however, therefore) connect ideas together. Short sentences create impact. Long sentences build detail.

Example from the Book

"The door was gone." — Four words. No explanation. The shortest sentence in the chapter creates the biggest emotional impact. The author chose this length on purpose.
