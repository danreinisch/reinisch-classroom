Day 1: Introduction to Quotation Marks

Showing Exact Words

Understanding Quotation Marks

WHAT ARE THEY? Quotation marks are the marks " " that go around the exact words someone speaks. They look like two small lines at the beginning and end of the spoken words.
WHY DO WE USE THEM? Quotation marks tell the reader: "These are the EXACT words the character said - not a summary or description, but their actual words."
HOW DO I USE THEM? Put opening quotation marks " right before the first spoken word. Put closing quotation marks " right after the last spoken word (after any punctuation).

Day 1: Anatomy of a Dialogue Sentence

The Parts of Dialogue

Breaking Down a Dialogue Sentence

Alex said, "We need to escape."
  • Alex said = The dialogue tag (tells who is speaking)
  • , = The comma (separates the tag from the quote)
  • " = Opening quotation mark (starts the spoken words)
  • W = Capital letter (first word of speech is always capitalized)
  • . = Period (ends the sentence - goes INSIDE the quotes)
  • " = Closing quotation mark (ends the spoken words)

Key Rule: Punctuation Goes INSIDE

Periods, question marks, exclamation points, and commas always go INSIDE the closing quotation mark.

Day 1: Two Dialogue Patterns

Tag First vs. Tag Last

Pattern 1: Tag FIRST

The dialogue tag comes BEFORE the spoken words.

Alex said, "We need to wait."

Rule: Put a comma after the tag, then start the quote with a capital letter.

Pattern 2: Tag LAST

The dialogue tag comes AFTER the spoken words.

"We need to wait," Alex said.

Rule: Put a comma INSIDE the quotes (not a period), then the tag with a period at the end.

Common Mistake: Using a Period Instead of a Comma

✗ "We need to wait." Alex said.

✓ "We need to wait," Alex said.

When the tag comes AFTER, use a comma inside the quotes, not a period!

Day 1: Questions and Exclamations in Dialogue

Special End Punctuation

Questions in Dialogue

If the spoken words are a question, use a ? inside the quotes.

"When do I fight?" Alex asked.

Notice: The question mark goes INSIDE the quotes. The tag "Alex asked" still ends with a period.

Exclamations in Dialogue

If the spoken words show strong emotion, use an ! inside the quotes.

"He can do it!" Werda-ak shouted.

Notice: The exclamation point goes INSIDE the quotes.

Quick Practice

Which is correct?

A: "Can you fight?" Rinka-ak asked.

B: "Can you fight"? Rinka-ak asked.

Answer: A - The question mark goes INSIDE the quotation marks.

Day 2: Capitalizing in Dialogue

The First Word Rule

Capital Letters in Dialogue

WHAT IS THE RULE? The first word inside quotation marks is ALWAYS capitalized, even if it comes in the middle of a sentence.
WHY? When someone speaks, they are starting a new thought. A new thought begins with a capital letter.
HOW DO I APPLY IT? Look at the first word INSIDE the opening quotation mark. That word gets a capital letter.

Examples

✓ Rinka-ak said, "This is a fight to the death."

✗ Rinka-ak said, "this is a fight to the death."


✓ "I choose no weapons," Alex said.

✗ "i choose no weapons," Alex said.

Day 2: Comma Placement - Tag First

When the Tag Comes Before

Pattern: Dialogue Tag FIRST

Structure: Tag + comma + opening quote + spoken words + end punctuation + closing quote

1
Write the dialogue tag: Alex said
2
Add a comma: Alex said,
3
Add opening quotation mark: Alex said, "
4
Write the spoken words (first word capitalized): Alex said, "I choose no weapons
5
Add end punctuation INSIDE, then closing quote: Alex said, "I choose no weapons."
Alex said, "I choose no weapons."

Day 2: Comma Placement - Tag Last

When the Tag Comes After

Pattern: Dialogue Tag LAST

Structure: Opening quote + spoken words + comma + closing quote + tag + period

Important: Use a comma (not a period) at the end of the spoken words if the tag follows!

1
Start with opening quotation mark: "
2
Write spoken words (first word capitalized): "I choose no weapons
3
Add comma INSIDE the quotes: "I choose no weapons,
4
Add closing quotation mark: "I choose no weapons,""
5
Add the tag and period: "I choose no weapons," Alex said.
"I choose no weapons," Alex said.

Day 2: Split Dialogue

Tag in the Middle

Understanding Split Dialogue

WHAT IS IT? Split dialogue is when the dialogue tag comes in the MIDDLE of what someone is saying. The spoken words are split into two parts.
WHY USE IT? Split dialogue adds variety to your writing and can create natural pauses in conversation.
HOW DO I PUNCTUATE IT? Use commas on BOTH sides of the tag. The second part does NOT start with a capital (unless it's a new sentence).
"If you win," Rinka-ak said, "I will set you free."

Breaking It Down

  • "If you win," - First part ends with comma inside quotes
  • Rinka-ak said, - Tag followed by comma
  • "I will set you free." - Second part starts with lowercase (same sentence)

Day 2: Split Dialogue - Two Sentences

When to Use a Capital After the Tag

If the Second Part is a NEW Sentence...

Use a period after the tag, and capitalize the first word of the new sentence.

"I will fight." Alex stood up. "When do we begin?"

Same Sentence (comma, lowercase)

"If you win," he said, "you will go free."

One complete thought split by the tag.

Two Sentences (period, capital)

"You will fight." He paused. "Are you ready?"

Two separate thoughts with action between.

Day 3: Dialogue Without Tags

When You Know Who is Speaking

Dialogue Without "Said"

WHAT IS IT? Sometimes dialogue appears without a tag like "said" or "asked." The quote stands alone or is followed by an action.
WHY USE IT? It creates variety and can speed up the pace of conversation. Too many "said" tags can become boring.
HOW DO I PUNCTUATE IT? Use a period at the end (inside the quotes) since there's no tag following. Actions before or after help show who is speaking.
Alex accepted the bag. "Thank you. This is very kind."

Notice:

The action "Alex accepted the bag" tells us who is speaking. The dialogue uses periods (not commas) because there's no dialogue tag.

Day 3: Action Beats

Showing Who Speaks Through Actions

What is an Action Beat?

An action beat is when a character does something before or after speaking. The action shows who is talking without using "said."

With Dialogue Tag

"Taste it," Rinka-ak said.

Uses "said" to show speaker.

With Action Beat

Rinka-ak offered the bag. "Taste it."

Action shows who speaks. Period ends the quote.

Important Difference

Dialogue tag: Uses comma inside quotes → "Taste it," Rinka-ak said.

Action beat: Uses period inside quotes → Rinka-ak smiled. "Taste it."

Day 4: Writing Workshop

Using Dialogue in Your Writing

Writing Structure: Topic Sentence + Three Details + Conclusion

  • Topic Sentence: A clear statement that answers the question.
  • Supporting Detail 1: First piece of proof from the story.
  • Supporting Detail 2: Second piece of proof from the story.
  • Supporting Detail 3: Third piece of proof from the story.
  • Conclusion: Restate your main idea in different words.

Dialogue Rules to Remember

  • " " Quotation marks go around spoken words.
  • Capitalize the first word inside quotes.
  • . ? ! End punctuation goes INSIDE the quotes.
  • , Use a comma before the tag when the tag comes last.

Day 4: Writing Checklist

Check Your Work Before Submitting

Dialogue Checklist (If You Use Dialogue)

  • ☐ Did you put quotation marks around spoken words?
  • ☐ Is the first word inside quotes capitalized?
  • ☐ Is the end punctuation inside the quotation marks?
  • ☐ Did you use a comma before the dialogue tag (if tag comes last)?

General Checklist

  • ☐ Does every sentence start with a capital letter?
  • ☐ Are all character names capitalized? (Alex, Rinka-ak, Grunta-ak)
  • ☐ Does every sentence end with . or ? or !?
  • ☐ Did you use apostrophes correctly? (Alex's, can't)

Content Checklist

  • ☐ Do you have a topic sentence that answers the question?
  • ☐ Do you have at least three details from the story?
  • ☐ Do you have a conclusion sentence?
