Day 1: Manta-ak's Plans

Where We Begin

Manta-ak has just returned from the Battle of Denta-ah. He was making plans to leave.

Key Fact Number 1

Manta-ak was planning to return home through the magical door to see his daughter Amy.

But before he could leave, Ganku-eh (the chieftain) asked him to stay for an important mission.

Capitalization Rule

Notice how Amy is capitalized because it is a name. People's names always start with capital letters.

Punctuation Rule: The Period

The sentence "He was making plans to leave" ends with a period . because it is a telling sentence.

Day 1: Ganku-eh Steps Down

A Sad Decision

Ganku-eh announces that she is stepping down as chieftain. Why is she stepping down?

Key Fact Number 4

Ganku-eh is stepping down because she is grieving and feels she failed the tribe.

Her husband Banda-ak died in battle. Children were also kidnapped while she was supposed to protect them. She is not stepping down because she is too old or because she wants to go on the mission.

Sekun-ak becomes the new chieftain. It is not Manta-ak and not Werda-ak. It is Sekun-ak.

Punctuation Rule: The Question Mark

The sentence "Why is she stepping down?" ends with a question mark ? because it is asking something.

Day 2: Punctuation and Capitalization

Today's Main Grammar Lesson

Yesterday we learned that names start with capital letters and that periods end telling sentences. Today we will learn more rules.

Why Does Punctuation Matter?

Punctuation helps readers understand your writing. Without it, sentences are confusing.

lets eat grandma (confusing)
Let's eat, Grandma! (clear)

Punctuation saves lives.

Day 2: The Period

Period .

A dot used at the end of a telling sentence (also called a statement).

Grammar Fact Number 1

A period goes at the end of a telling sentence.

The team followed the trail.

This sentence tells us something. It is not asking a question. It ends with a period.

More Examples:

Manta-ak led the rescue team.

The invaders have a two-week head start.

Day 2: Capital Letters at the Start

Grammar Fact Number 2

The first word of every sentence starts with a capital letter.

the invaders came from far away. (incorrect)
The invaders came from far away. (correct)

In the sentence "the invaders came from far away," which word should have a capital letter? The answer is "the" because it is the first word.

Two Rules for Capital Letters

1. The first word of a sentence gets a capital letter.
2. Names of people, animals, and places get capital letters.

Day 2: The Apostrophe

Apostrophe '

A mark used to show ownership (something belongs to someone).

Grammar Fact Number 3

An apostrophe shows that something belongs to someone.

Manta-ak's dog

The apostrophe plus the letter s shows that the dog belongs to Manta-ak.

The apostrophe is not a period or comma.

Period = . (ends a telling sentence)

Comma = , (separates items in a list)

Apostrophe = ' (shows ownership)

Day 2: The Question Mark

Question Mark ?

A mark used at the end of an asking sentence (a question).

Grammar Fact Number 4

A question mark goes at the end of an asking sentence.

Did the invaders set a trap?

This sentence is asking something. Questions end with a question mark.

How to tell the difference:

Telling: The invaders set a trap. (period)

Asking: Did the invaders set a trap? (question mark)

Day 2: Capital Letters for Names

Grammar Fact Number 5

Names of people, animals, and places always start with a capital letter.

monda-ak (incorrect) becomes Monda-ak (correct)

Why should "monda-ak" be written as "Monda-ak"? Because it is a name.

All of these need capital letters because they are names:

Manta-ak (person's name)

Monda-ak (animal's name)

Winten-ah (place name)

Kranda-ah (place name)

Day 3: Chapter 3 - The First Mighty River

The Journey Continues

Key Fact Number 10

The team travels for six days before reaching the mighty river. It is not three days and not ten days. It is six days.

During these six days, they hunt and fish for food and sleep in trees at night for safety.

Key Fact Number 11

The Kranda-ah is a mighty river (the Columbia River). It is not an animal and not a mountain. It is a river.

Capital Letter for Place Names

Kranda-ah is a place name, so it starts with a capital letter.

Day 3: Strange Creatures

Giant Skinks

Near the river, the team discovers unusual creatures sunning themselves on rocks.

Key Fact Number 12

They find giant skinks with bright blue tails. They are not snakes and not birds. They are skinks with blue tails.

These lizards are two to three feet long. That is much bigger than normal lizards.

Key Fact Number 13

When Monda-ak tries to catch a skink, he only catches the tail. The skink escapes.

Skinks can drop their tails when grabbed. The tail comes off, and the skink runs away.

Punctuation Rule: The Comma

In "The tail comes off, and the skink runs away," the comma separates two complete thoughts joined by "and."

Day 3: The River Problem

Why Can They Not Just Swim?

Key Fact Number 14

The team cannot swim across because the river is too fast. They would be swept away.

The Kranda-ah is huge and rushing incredibly fast. Swimming would be deadly. The problem is the speed of the current.

Apostrophe in Contractions

Can't equals can plus not. The apostrophe shows that letters are missing.

Examples: can't, don't, won't, isn't

Question Mark Practice

Why can they not just swim? This sentence asks a question, so it ends with a question mark.

Day 3: The Invaders' Trap

How Did the Invaders Cross?

The team finds a rope system spanning the river with a boat attached. But there is a problem.

Key Fact Number 15

The invaders crossed using a boat and then left it on the other side to trap their pursuers.

They did not burn a bridge. They did not swim across. They used a boat and then left it out of reach.

Punctuation Check

How did the invaders cross the river? This is a question, so it needs a question mark.

The invaders used a boat. This is a telling sentence, so it needs a period.

Day 3: Werda-ak's Mistake

A Dangerous Decision

Young Werda-ak volunteers to walk across the rope to get the boat. Manta-ak says no because it is too dangerous.

But Werda-ak is young and wants to prove himself.

Key Fact Number 16

Werda-ak disobeys Manta-ak, tries to cross the rope, and falls into the Kranda-ah River.

He does not successfully cross. He does not find another way. He falls in.

Exclamation Point

He falls into the river! The exclamation point shows strong feeling or excitement.

Cliffhanger: Will Werda-ak survive? We will find out in Chapter 4.

Day 3: Grammar Review

What We Have Learned This Week

Capital Letters

Use at the start of sentences and for names of people, animals, and places.

Period .

Use at the end of a telling sentence (statement).

Question Mark ?

Use at the end of an asking sentence (question).

Exclamation Point !

Use to show strong feeling or excitement.

Apostrophe '

Use to show ownership (Manta-ak's dog) or in contractions (can't, don't).

Comma ,

Use to separate items in a list or to join two complete thoughts.

Day 4: Writing Workshop

Today We Write

Use what you have learned about the story and about punctuation and capitalization.

Writing Structure: Topic Plus Three Details Plus Conclusion

  • Topic Sentence: Tell the main idea.
  • Detail 1: Give your first piece of evidence.
  • Detail 2: Give your second piece of evidence.
  • Detail 3: Give your third piece of evidence.
  • Conclusion: Wrap it up.

Remember Your Punctuation Rules

Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a . or ? or !

Day 4: Writing Checklist

Before You Turn In Your Writing

Punctuation and Capitalization Check

  • Does every sentence start with a capital letter?
  • Are all names capitalized? (Manta-ak, Werda-ak, Kranda-ah)
  • Does every sentence end with a period . or question mark ? or exclamation point !?
  • Did you use apostrophes correctly? (Manta-ak's team)
  • Did you use commas to separate items in a list?

Content Check

  • Do you have a topic sentence that answers the question?
  • Do you have at least three details from the story?
  • Do you have a conclusion sentence?
