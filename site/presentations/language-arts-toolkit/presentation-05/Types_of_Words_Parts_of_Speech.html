👤 Nouns

Definition

A noun is a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea. Nouns are the subjects and objects of sentences and form the backbone of communication.

Common Nouns

General names: dog city school book teacher

Proper Nouns

Specific (capitalized): Winfield Mrs. Garcia Missouri

Abstract Nouns

Ideas/feelings: happiness freedom courage loyalty

Collective Nouns

Groups: team family flock class audience

The teacher read a book about friendship to her students at Winfield.

💡 Quick Test

Can you put "the" or "a" in front of it? Can it be made plural? If yes, it is probably a noun!

the happiness ✓ • a school ✓ • dogs ✓

🏃 Verbs

Definition

A verb expresses an action, occurrence, or state of being. Every complete sentence needs at least one verb. Verbs tell us what the subject is doing.

Action Verbs

Physical or mental: run write think eat believe

Linking Verbs

Connect subject to description: is are was seem become

Helping Verbs

Support the main verb: can will should have might

Verb Tenses

Past: ran • Present: run • Future: will run

The children laughed loudly and played in the park while their parents watched.

💡 Quick Test

Can you change it to past tense? Can you add "-ing"? If yes, it is likely a verb!

run → ran ✓ • write → writing ✓ • think → thought ✓

Linking Verb Trick

Replace the verb with "=" — if it still makes sense, it is a linking verb:
"She is happy" → "She = happy" ✓

🎨 Adjectives

Definition

An adjective describes or modifies a noun or pronoun. Adjectives answer three questions: What kind? How many? Which one?

What kind?

red car • tall building • happy student • ancient ruins

How many?

three books • several friends • many ideas • few choices

Which one?

this chair • that movie • first place • last chance

Comparing

big → bigger → biggest
beautiful → more beautiful

The brave, young knight rode his white horse through the dark forest.

💡 Quick Test

Does it fit between "very" and a noun? "The very _____ dog" — if it works, it is an adjective!

the very happy dog ✓ • the very tall building ✓

Adjective vs. Adverb

Adjectives describe nouns (the fast car). Adverbs describe verbs (she drove fast). If it tells you about a thing = adjective. About an action = adverb.

⚡ Adverbs

Definition

An adverb modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb. Adverbs answer: How? When? Where? How often? To what extent?

How? (Manner)

quickly carefully loudly softly bravely

When? (Time)

yesterday soon always later now

Where? (Place)

here outside everywhere upstairs nearby

To what extent?

very extremely quite almost too really

She carefully and slowly walked upstairs yesterday.

💡 Quick Test

Many adverbs end in -ly. Try removing the word — if the sentence still works but loses detail about the action, it is an adverb.

She ran quickly → She ran (still works, lost detail = adverb!)

Watch Out: Not All -ly Words Are Adverbs!

friendly, lovely, lonely, and silly are actually adjectives. Always check: does it describe a noun (adjective) or an action (adverb)?

👉 Pronouns

Definition

A pronoun takes the place of a noun so we do not have to repeat it. Pronouns make sentences flow more smoothly.

Subject Pronouns

Do the action: I you he she it we they

Object Pronouns

Receive the action: me you him her it us them

Possessive Pronouns

Show ownership: my your his her its our their

Other Types

Reflexive: myself herself themselves
Interrogative: who what which

Without Pronouns vs. With Pronouns

Awkward: Sarah loves books. Sarah reads books every day because books make Sarah happy.

Smooth: Sarah loves books. She reads them every day because they make her happy.

Sarah loves books. She reads them every day because they make her happy.

💡 Quick Test

Can you replace it with a specific name or noun? If yes, it is a pronoun!

He went to the store → Jake went to the store ✓

📍 Prepositions

Definition

A preposition shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and another word. They usually indicate location, direction, time, or manner.

Location

in on under between behind beside

Direction

to from through across into toward

Time

before after during until since at

Other

with without about for by of

The cat jumped onto the table and knocked the vase off during the party.

💡 Quick Test: The Squirrel and the Tree

Does the word fit? "The squirrel ran _____ the tree."

up, around, behind, through, into, over → all prepositions!

Prepositional Phrases

A preposition + a noun = a prepositional phrase:
"The book on the shelf" (which book?) • "She ran through the park" (where?)

🔗 Conjunctions

Definition

A conjunction connects words, phrases, or clauses. They show relationships between ideas and help build complex sentences.

Coordinating (FANBOYS)

For And Nor But Or Yet So

Subordinating

because although when if since while unless

Correlative (Pairs)

either...or neither...nor both...and not only...but also

Why They Matter

Without conjunctions, every sentence is short and choppy. They are the glue of writing!

💡 FANBOYS Memory Trick

For • And • Nor • But • Or • Yet • So

I wanted pizza, but my sister wanted tacos, so we got both.

💡 Quick Test

Try removing the word. If the sentence breaks into two separate thoughts, it is a conjunction!

"I like dogs and cats" → "I like dogs. Cats." (needs the conjunction!)

📣 Interjections

Definition

An interjection is a word or phrase that expresses strong emotion or surprise. They usually stand alone with exclamation points or are set off by commas.

Surprise

Wow! Oh! Whoa! Really?

Joy

Hooray! Yay! Yes! Bravo!

Pain / Disgust

Ouch! Ow! Ew! Yuck!

Hesitation

Um... Well... Hmm... Uh...

Wow! That was an amazing performance. Bravo! You should be proud.

💡 Quick Test

Can it stand alone as its own sentence? Does it express a feeling? If yes, it is an interjection!

Punctuation Matters

Strong emotion = exclamation point: "Wow! That is amazing!"
Mild emotion = comma: "Well, I think we should go."

📄 Articles

Definition

Articles are special words before nouns that indicate whether the noun is specific or general. There are only three: the, a, and an.

Definite: the

Refers to a specific noun both speaker and listener know about.

"Give me the book" = a specific one

"The sun rises in the east" = only one

Indefinite: a / an

Refers to any one of a group, not specific.

"Give me a book" = any book

"She ate an apple" = one, not specific

A vs. An — The Sound Rule

Use a before consonant sounds: a dog, a university (sounds like "yoo")
Use an before vowel sounds: an apple, an hour (the "h" is silent)

The student brought a pencil and an eraser to the exam.

🎨 Put It All Together

See how ALL parts of speech work together in real sentences.

Wow! The brave firefighter quickly rescued a tiny kitten from the tall tree, and she carried it gently to its owner.

Breakdown

Wow! = interjectionThe, a = articlesbrave, tiny, tall = adjectivesfirefighter, kitten, tree, owner = nounsquickly, gently = adverbsrescued, carried = verbsfrom, to = prepositionsand = conjunctionshe, it, its = pronouns

Commonly Confused Pairs

  • Adjective vs. Adverb: "She is a careful driver" (adj) vs. "She drives carefully" (adv)
  • Preposition vs. Adverb: "She walked in" (adverb, no object) vs. "She walked in the door" (preposition + noun)
  • Pronoun vs. Adjective: "That is mine" (pronoun) vs. "That book is mine" (adjective)

Second Example Sentence

Oh! I almost forgot my new backpack at the busy airport, but a kind stranger quickly returned it to me.
