What We Are Learning

Our reading skill

Apply perspective and ethical-leadership analysis. We compare how imperial officers, Winten-ah leaders, volunteers, and occupied communities understand duty, freedom, risk, and responsibility.

I can…

  • Compare how culture shapes different ideas of duty.
  • Evaluate whether new evidence justifies a dangerous mission.
  • Explain how loyalty affects team and leadership decisions.

Point of View Changes the Story

Drogon remembers Panga’s defeat.

He experiences Alex’s victory as humiliation and lost status.

He thinks about his family and home.

The invading army also contains people separated from ordinary lives.

He accepts an unwanted mission.

Military culture makes obedience part of honor.

He calls Kragdon-ah the Lost Land.

His language frames the region as remote and available to outsiders.

Today’s bridge: Understanding a viewpoint explains choices without requiring readers to approve of the values behind them.

Wrap-Up

Week 12 Quick Reference

The story so far

New viewpoints complicate the conflict. Regional evidence gives Alex’s conscience a larger target, loyalty builds a team, and Urpan-ah proves that even a courageous mission must understand incentives and rules.

Keep asking

  • Can readers understand a viewpoint without endorsing it?
  • When does conscience justify community risk?
  • Why must strategy consider the other side’s incentive?
