📝 Writing Strong Arguments with Topic Sentences

What is an Argument?

An argument is when you take a position on something and support it with reasons and evidence. In writing, a strong argument convinces your reader that your position makes sense.

Example from Chapter 26:

Argument: Alex was the right person to help prepare the Winten-ah for war.

Why? Because he has knowledge from the modern world about weapons and warfare that the tribe doesn't have.

Slide 4 of 24

🎯 What is a Topic Sentence?

Definition:

A topic sentence is like a promise you make to your reader. It tells them what your paragraph will be about and what point you're going to make. In an argument, your topic sentence states your position clearly.

Strong Topic Sentence Example:

"Alex's knowledge of modern warfare made him the best choice to help the Winten-ah prepare for battle against the Denta-ah."

This sentence tells us exactly what the paragraph will argue and gives us a preview of why.

💡 A Good Topic Sentence:

  • States your position clearly
  • Is specific, not vague
  • Can be supported with evidence
  • Gives the reader direction

🔤 Understanding Different Types of Words

Why Word Types Matter:

Understanding different types of words helps you write more clearly and powerfully. Each type of word does a different job in your sentences. When you know what job each word does, you can choose the best words to express your ideas.

Today We'll Look At:

  • Naming words (nouns)
  • Action words (verbs)
  • Describing words (adjectives)
  • Words that tell how (adverbs)
  • Connecting words (conjunctions)
  • Position words (prepositions)
  • Emotion words (interjections)
Slide 12 of 24

👤 Naming Words (Nouns)

What They Do:

Naming words tell us about people, places, things, or ideas. They name what we're talking about.

People

Alex, Tinta-ak, warrior, leader

Places

village, battlefield, forest

Things

shield, sword, trebuchet

Ideas

respect, courage, leadership

From Chapter 28: "Alex designed the shield for the warriors in the village."
Naming words: Alex (person), shield (thing), warriors (people), village (place)
Slide 13 of 24

⚡ Action Words (Verbs)

What They Do:

Action words tell us what someone or something does, did, or will do. They show movement, thought, or existence.

Physical Actions

fought, dodged, struck, charged

Mental Actions

thought, planned, understood, worried

State of Being

was, is, became, seemed

From Chapter 28: "Alex dodged the attack and struck back quickly."
Action words: dodged (past action), struck (past action)
Slide 14 of 24

✨ Other Important Word Types

Describing Words (Adjectives):

Tell us more about naming words: strong warrior, composite shield, skilled fighter

Words That Tell How (Adverbs):

Tell us more about action words: moved quickly, fought skillfully, thought carefully

Connecting Words (Conjunctions):

Join parts of sentences together: and, but, because, although, while

Combined Example: "Alex fought skillfully and defeated the strong warrior because he used smart tactics."

🏗️ Building a Strong Argument

5-Part Argument Structure:

  1. Topic Sentence: State your position clearly
  2. Supporting Detail #1: First reason with evidence
  3. Supporting Detail #2: Second reason with evidence
  4. Supporting Detail #3: Third reason with evidence
  5. Conclusion: Restate your position and wrap up

💡 Pro Tip:

Each supporting detail should include BOTH a reason AND specific evidence or examples from the chapters!

Slide 18 of 24

Example: A Complete Argument

Topic Sentence: Alex proved he was the right leader for the warriors through his knowledge, preparation, and fighting skill.

Supporting Detail #1: First, Alex showed his knowledge by understanding the enemy's weapons. In Chapter 26, he described the trebuchet and crossbows to Ganku-eh, explaining exactly what the Winten-ah would face.

Supporting Detail #2: Second, Alex prepared smart defenses for his warriors. In Chapter 27, he designed the composite shield that combined straw, cloth, and leather to stop arrows while staying light enough to carry.

Supporting Detail #3: Third, Alex demonstrated his fighting skill against Tinta-ak. In Chapter 28, he used training and technique to defeat a much larger opponent without trying to humiliate him.

Conclusion: Through his knowledge, careful preparation, and skillful fighting, Alex showed why Ganku-eh chose him to lead the warriors into battle.

💡 Hints to Help You Write

If you think Alex SHOULD have fought:

  • Think about why the warriors doubted him at first
  • Consider what would have happened if he refused to fight
  • Remember how the warriors reacted after seeing him win
  • Think about what the fight taught them about leadership

If you think Alex should NOT have fought:

  • Consider the risks Alex took by fighting someone so much bigger
  • Think about other ways he could have shown his skills
  • Remember that he could have been killed
  • Consider if there were safer ways to earn respect

Remember to Include:

  • ✅ Specific details from Chapter 28
  • ✅ Explanations of HOW your evidence supports your argument
  • ✅ A clear position in your topic sentence
  • ✅ Three different reasons that support your position
