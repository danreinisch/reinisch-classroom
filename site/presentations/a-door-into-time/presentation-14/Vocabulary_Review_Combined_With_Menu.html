FEROCITY

What does it mean?

Extreme fierceness or savage violence

Word Type:

Naming word (noun)

-ity = state of being

In the Story:

The battle scenes show the ferocity of the fighting - not just regular violence, but extreme and savage attacks.

ALACRITY

What does it mean?

Eager willingness; cheerful readiness

Word Type:

Naming word (noun)

-ity = state of being

In the Story:

Characters show alacrity when they jump into action quickly and willingly, ready to do what needs to be done.

MONOTONOUS

What does it mean?

Dull, repetitive, and boring

Word Parts:

mono- = one

-ous = full of

Describing word (adjective)

In the Story:

The monotonous routine of camp life - doing the same thing over and over - makes the battle scenes feel even more intense by comparison.

VULNERABLE

What does it mean?

Exposed to harm or open to attack

Word Type:

Describing word (adjective)

In the Story:

Soldiers are vulnerable when they're not protected - exposed and open to being attacked or hurt.

LACONICALLY

What does it mean?

Using very few words; being brief

Word Type:

Word that tells how (adverb)

In the Story:

When characters speak laconically, they're not chatty - they say what needs to be said in just a few words.

PITIFUL

What does it mean?

Deserving pity; pathetically weak

Word Parts:

-ful = full of

Describing word (adjective)

In the Story:

Something pitiful makes you feel sorry for it - it's weak and deserves sympathy or compassion.

FOMENTING

What does it mean?

Stirring up trouble or rebellion

Word Type:

Action word (verb)

In the Story:

Fomenting rebellion means getting people worked up and ready to fight back - the opposite of calming things down.

BARRAGE

What does it mean?

A concentrated artillery bombardment; an overwhelming amount

Word Type:

Naming word (noun)

In the Story:

A barrage isn't just one attack - it's an overwhelming amount of attacks coming all at once.

ONSLAUGHT

What does it mean?

A fierce, overwhelming attack

Word Type:

Naming word (noun)

In the Story:

An onslaught is much more than a regular battle - it's fierce, overwhelming, and intense.

ENTRENCHED

What does it mean?

In a firmly established, protected position

Word Parts:

en- = in, into

Describing word (adjective)

In the Story:

Entrenched soldiers aren't just standing around - they're dug into protected positions that are hard to attack.

📝 Written Response Structure

1. Topic Sentence (1 point)

Clearly answer the question right away

2. Three Supporting Details (3 points)

Each detail needs:

  • A specific detail from the story
  • Your explanation of WHY it matters

3. Conclusion Sentence (1 point)

Wrap up and restate your main point in different words

✅ Good Topic Sentence Example

"Alex was motivated to fight because he wanted to honor his oath to Banda-ak."

Why is this good?

  • It directly answers the question
  • It's clear and specific
  • It sets up what you'll explain next

📚 Strong Supporting Details

What you need in EACH detail:

1. A specific vocabulary word or story detail

Example: "The word 'ferocity'..."

2. An explanation of how it helps understanding

Example: "...helps me picture just how fierce and violent the fighting was, not just that people were fighting."

  • Conclusion that wraps it all up

WITHERING

What does it mean?

Devastating or overwhelming

Word Type:

Describing word (adjective)

-ing = action of; present form

In the Story:

A withering attack isn't just strong - it's devastating and overwhelming, like it could destroy everything.

ATTRITION

What does it mean?

The gradual wearing down through continuous losses

Word Parts:

at- = toward

Naming word (noun)

In the Story:

Attrition isn't one big defeat - it's slowly wearing down through many small losses over time.

CONTINGENCY

What does it mean?

A group sent to perform a specific task

Word Parts:

con- = together, with

Naming word (noun)

In the Story:

A contingency is a group of people sent together to accomplish something specific.

DIMINISHED

What does it mean?

Made smaller or weaker

Word Parts:

di- = away, apart

Action word (verb)

In the Story:

When something is diminished, it's been made smaller, weaker, or less powerful than before.

EXHILARATED

🎭 EMOTION WORD!

This word helps us understand what characters are feeling!

What does it mean?

Filled with excitement and energy

Word Parts:

ex- = out, thoroughly

Describing word (adjective)

Why it matters:

When a character is exhilarated, they're not just happy - they're filled with extreme excitement and energy!

AFTERMATH

What does it mean?

What remains or comes after a big event

Word Parts:

after- = later

Naming word (noun)

In the Story:

The aftermath is what's left behind after a battle - the consequences and remains after the big event.

BACCHANAL

What does it mean?

A wild party or celebration

Word Type:

Naming word (noun)

In the Story:

A bacchanal isn't just a regular party - it's wild, loud, and out of control.

INTERMINABLE

What does it mean?

Endless; never seeming to stop

Word Parts:

in- = not

Describing word (adjective)

In the Story:

Something interminable feels like it goes on forever - it never seems to end.

INEXTRICABLY

What does it mean?

Impossible to separate; tied together

Word Type:

Word that tells how (adverb)

In the Story:

When things are inextricably linked, you can't pull them apart - they're tied together permanently.

SOMBER

🎭 EMOTION WORD!

This word helps us understand what characters are feeling!

What does it mean?

Dark, gloomy, or sad in mood

Word Type:

Describing word (adjective)

Why it matters:

When a character is somber, they're not just sad - they have a dark, heavy, gloomy mood that affects everything.

VISCERA

What does it mean?

Internal organs of the body

Word Type:

Naming word (noun)

In the Story:

Viscera refers to the internal organs inside the body - not bones or muscles, but organs like the heart and stomach.

🎭 Why Emotion Words Matter

Emotion words help us understand characters deeply!

Think about it:

  • Exhilarated vs. "happy" - Shows INTENSE excitement, not just pleased
  • Somber vs. "sad" - Shows a DARK, HEAVY mood, not just unhappy
  • Pitiful vs. "weak" - Makes us feel SYMPATHY and compassion

📝 Written Response Structure

1. Topic Sentence (1 point)

Clearly answer the question right away

2. Three Supporting Details (3 points)

Each detail needs:

  • A specific emotion word from the story
  • Your explanation of how it helps understand characters

3. Conclusion Sentence (1 point)

Wrap up and restate your main point in different words
