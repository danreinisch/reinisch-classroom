DAY 1

Chapter 29: "Basic Training"

Focus: Story Organization

What Is Story Structure?

Structure = How the author organizes or arranges the story

Think of it like building blocks:

• You can stack them in order (beginning → middle → end)

• You can show the end first, then explain how you got there

• You can arrange them by importance (most important → least important)

How Is Chapter 29 Organized?

PART 1

Alex trains a small group of leaders

PART 2

Those leaders each train their own groups

PART 3

Skills spread to hundreds of warriors

PART 4

Demonstration shows it all working together

This is "small to large" structure!

Example: The Shield Wall

Step 1: Alex shows Monda-ak how to lock shields together

Step 2: Monda-ak and others practice the formation

Step 3: Tinta-ak tries to break through alone - he can't!

Step 4: Everyone sees: working together is stronger than working alone

The author organizes this to show one clear lesson at a time.

The Pattern Alex Uses

Problem → Demonstration → Understanding

Problem: Warriors don't know how to fight together

Demonstration: Alex shows them the shield wall

Understanding: They learn teamwork beats individual strength

This pattern repeats throughout the chapter!

Why Does This Organization Help?

  • We see the army getting better step by step
  • Each new skill builds on the skills before it
  • We understand how they become an army, not just that they do
  • The structure shows progress clearly

Author's Choice: Instead of saying "Alex trained an army," the author shows us each step of the training. This makes the story more interesting and believable!

DAY 2

Chapter 30: "Janta-ak's Journey"

Focus: Vocabulary

dangerous

Type: Describing word (adjective)

Example from text: "Janta-ak faced dangerous challenges"

What it describes: The word "dangerous" describes what kind of challenges - challenges that could cause harm

unerringly

Breaking it down:

un-

means "NOT"

err

means "make mistakes"

-ing + -ly

tells HOW something happens

Unerringly = doing something without making mistakes

cautiously

Type: Word that tells HOW (adverb)

Example: "Janta-ak moved cautiously"

What it tells us: HOW Janta-ak moved - carefully, watching for danger

The -ly ending usually means a word tells HOW something happens

emaciated

Meaning: Extremely skinny from not eating enough

In the story: The children Janta-ak finds are emaciated - they are dangerously thin because they haven't had enough food

This word shows: How serious the situation is. "Skinny" is normal, but "emaciated" means someone is in danger from not eating.

grueling

Meaning: Very tiring and difficult

Example: "The grueling pace of the march"

Why use this word? The author could say "tiring" or "hard," but "grueling" is much stronger. It means SO tiring that it exhausts you completely.

This helps us understand how tough the journey really is!

rebuild

Breaking it down:

re-

means "AGAIN"

build

means "to make or construct"

Rebuild = build again

Other words with "re-": return, redo, rewrite, replay

warriors

Type: Naming word (noun)

Example: "The warriors marched forward"

What it names: Warriors is a naming word - it names the people who fight

The -s ending: Shows there is more than one warrior

mortality

Meaning: Death; the fact that all living things die

In the text: "The warriors contemplated their mortality"

What this means: The warriors thought about the fact that they might die in battle

Why it matters: This shows how serious and dangerous their situation is. They know death is possible.

DAY 3

Chapter 31: "Godat-ta"

Focus: Action Structure

How Authors Structure Action Scenes

Authors make choices about the SPEED and ORDER of action

SLOW DOWN

Make important moments last longer with lots of details

SPEED UP

Make less important moments go by quickly

BUILD UP

Add tension step by step

RELEASE

Let the tension go after the climax

Structure of the Godat-ta Scene

1. SLOW MARCH → Tension builds as they move through dangerous territory

2. SPOTTING THE BEAR → First moment of danger

3. FIRST CHARGE → Fast, chaotic action

4. SECOND CHARGE → Even more dangerous

5. SENTA-EH'S ARROW → SLOW MOTION for the most important moment

6. ESCAPE → Quick exit as tension releases

Why Slow Down Senta-eh's Arrow?

What the author does: Describes the arrow flying through the air in great detail - we see it leave the bow, arc through the air, and hit its target

Why this works:

  • Makes this moment feel more important
  • Builds suspense - will the arrow hit?
  • Lets us feel the relief when it works
  • Shows Senta-eh's skill and bravery

How the Author Builds Tension

  • First arrows miss - Creates worry and fear
  • Bear keeps charging - Danger keeps growing
  • Shield wall holds - Small victory but danger remains
  • Senta-eh takes aim - This is the last chance!
  • Arrow flies - Everything depends on this moment
  • Hit! - Tension releases, relief floods in

Each step makes the danger feel MORE real!

The Pattern: Slow → Fast → Slow

SLOW: Marching, watching, waiting (builds tension)

FAST: Bear charges! Chaos! Action! (peak excitement)

SLOW: Escaping, catching breath (tension releases)

This pattern keeps readers engaged - not too fast, not too slow, but changing pace to match the action!

Writing Day

Structure in Your Own Words

Written Response 1

Question: How does the author organize Chapter 29 to show the training getting better and better over time?

Success Tips:

  • Think about how Alex starts with just a few people
  • Notice how the training spreads to more and more warriors
  • Look at examples like the shield wall demonstration
  • Explain how each step builds on the one before it

Written Response 2

Question: How does the author arrange the Godat-ta battle scene in Chapter 31 to create excitement and suspense?

Success Tips:

  • Think about the steps: spotting → charge → arrow → escape
  • Notice how the author slows down important moments
  • Look at how tension builds with each step
  • Explain how the structure makes you worried and excited

Writing Structure to Use

1. Topic Sentence

Clearly answer the question

2. Supporting Detail 1

Give proof from the text and explain it

3. Supporting Detail 2

Give more proof from the text and explain it

4. Supporting Detail 3

Give more proof from the text and explain it

5. Conclusion

Wrap up and connect back to the question
