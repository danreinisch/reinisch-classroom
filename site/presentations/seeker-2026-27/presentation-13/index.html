Day 3 • ELA Concept

Precision Protects Credibility

“A hundred percent”

Ella expresses conviction about surveillance.

“I could be wrong”

She separates confidence from certainty.

“Hypothesis”

The explanation is proposed, not proven.

“Operate under the assumption”

The team chooses a working model while staying open to revision.

Today’s bridge: Cautious diction does not weaken an argument. It shows exactly how strong the evidence is—and where uncertainty remains.

Word Choice → Tone → Meaning

StepWhat to analyze
ContextWhat is happening when the language appears?
MeaningWhat does the word, phrase, or comparison literally mean?
Connotation / syntaxWhat attitude, association, rhythm, or emphasis does it add?
Reader effectWhat becomes clearer, more credible, or more emotional?

Wrap-Up

Week 13 Quick Reference

The story so far

The new team becomes understandable through craft: machine and biology are placed in parallel, humor interrupts terror, repetition organizes conflicting emotion, and cautious scientific diction makes uncertainty credible. Their most important tool may be the language of disciplined reasoning.

Keep asking

  • What clues reveal the word’s meaning in context?
  • What attitude or association does the word carry?
  • How do syntax and comparison influence the reader—not just decorate the sentence?
