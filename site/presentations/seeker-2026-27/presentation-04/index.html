What We Are Learning

Our reading skill

Introduce character analysis. We use actions, dialogue, thoughts, and relationships as evidence of motivation, values, and conflict.

I can…

  • Identify a character-revealing detail.
  • Infer a motivation or value from that detail.
  • Explain how competing values create conflict and shape a decision.

Day 1 • ELA Concept

Character Is Revealed Under Pressure

Action

What a person chooses when consequences are real.

Dialogue

What the person asks, promises, avoids, or challenges.

Relationship

How the person treats allies, rivals, and people with less power.

Conflict

Which values cannot all be satisfied at once.

Today’s bridge: A character trait is not a label pulled from thin air. It is a claim built from behavior, language, and context.

Build a Character Claim

StepWhat to do

Wrap-Up

Week 4 Quick Reference

The story so far

The mission begins as a problem of global power, but Chapters 10–12 make it a problem of character. The leaders need someone whose judgment can govern extraordinary power, and Kagan’s past and present explain why he is chosen.

Keep asking

  • Which details reveal values rather than merely describe events?
  • What changes Kagan—and what remains constant?
  • Where does a character’s strength become a possible weakness?
