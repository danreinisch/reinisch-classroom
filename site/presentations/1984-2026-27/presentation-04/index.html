Day 2 • ELA Concept

Memory Is Not a Database

Winston wants

A clear comparison between life before and after the Revolution.

The old man offers

Personal details about hats, beer, servants, and small insults.

The mismatch

Specific memories do not combine into a reliable social history.

The result

Winston’s frustration shows how difficult recovery becomes after records vanish.

Today’s bridge: Orwell does not simply say “memory fails.” He structures a failed interview so readers experience the gap between Winston’s question and the available answer.

Day 3 • ELA Concept

How an Object Becomes a Symbol

Object detail

Old glass encloses a piece of coral.

Story context

It comes from a time before the Party’s uniform present.

Character response

Winston values beauty and history more than usefulness.

Developing meaning

The object suggests a private, preserved world—but glass can break.

Today’s bridge: A symbol carries several related meanings that develop through context. The paperweight is not simply “the past”; it joins beauty, privacy, preservation, and fragility.

Wrap-Up

Week 4 Quick Reference

The story so far

Winston moves from destroyed proof to incomplete testimony to a beautiful object he can privately possess. The sequence shows his search changing: he cannot restore public history, so he tries to preserve a fragment of the past for himself.

Keep asking

  • What can physical evidence establish that memory cannot?
  • How does context give an object symbolic meaning?
  • Why might a preserved private world also be fragile?
