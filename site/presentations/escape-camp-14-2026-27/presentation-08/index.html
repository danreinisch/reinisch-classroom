Reading With Care

Real Events, Academic Language

Read with respect

These events involve real people. We analyze the author’s choices without treating suffering as entertainment.

Name harm precisely

Words such as coercion, punishment, trauma, and dehumanization are more useful than graphic retelling.

Separate then and now

Track what Shin understood during an event and what he understood years later.

Pause when needed

A short reset is responsible reading. Return ready to use evidence and careful language.

Day 2 • ELA Concept

The Source Evaluation Checklist

Authority

Who created it, and what relevant expertise or responsibility do they have?

Evidence

What data, documentation, testimony, or methods support it?

Purpose / perspective

Is it explaining, documenting, persuading, or advocating?

Currency / context

Is the date appropriate for the question?

Usefulness / limits

What does it clarify, and what can it not establish?

Today’s bridge: Do not reduce evaluation to “reliable / unreliable.” Most strong sources have a defined use and a defined boundary.

Synthesize Without Flattening

Evidence layerResponsible use
Book evidenceShin initially appears adaptable, then experiences nightmares, guilt, withdrawal, and treatment.
Companion sourceMay explain patterns in trauma, adjustment, or resettlement support.
SynthesisA pattern can clarify why safety and symptoms coexist.
LimitA general pattern cannot diagnose one person or replace his account.

Use “helps explain” or “is consistent with,” not “proves,” when the evidence does not establish direct causation.

Three Voices in a Synthesis

Book

Provides Shin’s sequence, reflections, relationships, and difficulties.

Companion source

Provides broader evidence, institutional context, or researched patterns.

Writer

Explains the relationship and preserves important differences.

Citation

Shows where borrowed information came from.

Today’s bridge: Synthesis is not source A plus source B. It is a defensible relationship between them.

Day 3 • Mini-Lesson

Paraphrase + Integrate + Cite

ActionIntegrity check
1. UnderstandRead the source passage until you can state the idea without looking.
2. ParaphraseUse new wording and sentence structure; keep the meaning accurate.
3. AttributeName the author or organization in the sentence.
4. ConnectExplain how the information clarifies or complicates the memoir.
5. CiteUse the required in-text and works-cited format.

Changing a few words is not a paraphrase. Attribution is required even when the wording is fully yours.

Wrap-Up

Week 8 Quick Reference

The story so far

Escape leads through China to legal protection and formal resettlement, but freedom does not erase trauma, isolation, or the need to learn unfamiliar systems. Responsible synthesis uses outside expertise to deepen the account while preserving the limits of each source.

Keep asking

  • Does the source’s expertise match the question?
  • Have I paraphrased accurately and attributed the idea?
  • Have I explained the relationship instead of stacking summaries?
