Reading With Care

Real Events, Academic Language

Read with respect

These events involve real people. We analyze the author’s choices without treating suffering as entertainment.

Name harm precisely

Words such as coercion, punishment, trauma, and dehumanization are more useful than graphic retelling.

Separate then and now

Track what Shin understood during an event and what he understood years later.

Pause when needed

A short reset is responsible reading. Return ready to use evidence and careful language.

Our reading skill

Day 1 • ELA Concept

From Event to Claim

Event

Shin buys rides and enters freight trains without papers.

Context

Famine-era markets and bribery have weakened travel enforcement.

Claim

Informal commerce creates mobility the formal state forbids.

Complication

The same low-trust world leaves Shin vulnerable to theft and betrayal.

Today’s bridge: A strong claim is precise enough to be challenged and supported by several kinds of evidence.

Credibility Audit

Evidence typeStrength / limit
First-person memoryProvides sequence and lived experience; may have gaps.
Named locationsAllow the route to be checked geographically.
Policy historyExplains why enforcement changed.
Interviews and reportsAdd outside perspectives on markets and bribery.
Author qualificationsSignal uncertainty with phrases such as “in all probability.”

Credibility grows through convergence, not through pretending every detail has the same certainty.

Day 2 • ELA Concept

Same Journey, Different Emphasis

Narrative

Emphasizes questions, bribes, hunger, timing, and limited knowledge.

Route map

Emphasizes direction, sequence, distance, and the border crossing.

Shared evidence

Both place travel northward through named locations to the Tumen River.

Different limit

Neither form alone proves every event or internal reaction.

Today’s bridge: Media comparison is not a contest for “best.” Ask what each form makes visible and what it necessarily compresses.

Day 3 • ELA Concept

Freedom and Vulnerability Coexist

Freedom gained

Shin can work, eat, sleep, move, and make some choices.

Legal vulnerability

Chinese authorities can detain and repatriate North Koreans.

Economic vulnerability

Employers can underpay or dismiss undocumented workers.

Social possibility

Korean-speaking communities can provide language and cultural access.

Today’s bridge: A nuanced claim can be supported and complicated at the same time; corroboration does not require simplification.

Day 3 • Mini-Lesson

Media Corroboration Matrix

Companion mediumWhat it can / cannot establish
Map or satellite viewCan support terrain, settlement pattern, river location, and distance.
PhotographCan show visible conditions at a place and time, but not Shin’s exact experience.
Law / policy documentCan establish official obligations and rules, not consistent enforcement.
MemoirCan establish remembered sequence and perception, but needs outside support for broad claims.

Always match the source to the claim. A source can be authoritative and still irrelevant to the exact question.

Compare Representations, Not Just Facts

StepWhat to write
Shared subjectName the same journey, place, or claim.
Written emphasisExplain what prose develops through sequence and detail.

Wrap-Up

Week 7 Quick Reference

The story so far

Beyond the fence, Shin’s movement depends on informal markets, bribery, transport, and strangers. A route map makes the journey’s geography visible, while the memoir reveals uncertainty, exploitation, and conditional freedom that a line cannot show.

Keep asking

  • What kind of source matches the claim being tested?
  • When does corroboration strengthen a claim, and when does it only add context?
  • How do different media distribute attention differently?
