What goes on it?Everything they ask — no blanks, N/A where neededOnly your BEST material — a highlight reel, not a diary

One rule carries over untouched: everything on it must be true — the receipts rule from Week 3. A résumé chooses your best true things; it never invents new ones. And “is not” matters too: not a life story, not a brag with no receipts, not a wall of text — one clean page.

Day 1 • You Try It

Spot the Difference

An application has a blank you can’t use — you write N/A. A résumé section you have nothing for (say, past jobs)…
N/A exists because their form demands an answer for every blank. YOUR page only contains what you have — volunteer work, school experience, and skills can carry a first résumé beautifully. Inventing? The receipts rule never sleeps.
Which belongs on a résumé?
The dog line is the experience formula wearing its Sunday best — what, where, when, duty. Life stories belong in conversations, not on the page. And the SSN is red-zone (Week 5): NEVER on a résumé — it gets handed to too many people.
A résumé is called a “highlight reel.” That means…
A movie trailer shows the best two minutes — and every second of it is really in the movie. That’s the whole game: true things, best ones first, nothing padded.
Employers spend about SIX SECONDS on a first résumé look. Knowing that — what has to be true about the top third of your page?
Think first — then click to reveal
The top third has to do all the first-impression work: your name big and clean, contact info exact (Week 5 standards), and your strongest section starting immediately. Six seconds is why résumés are one page, why neatness wins, and why tomorrow is entirely about choosing what deserves those top lines.

Earning a Spot on the Page

SectionWhat earns a spotLine looks like
EducationCurrent school + expected finish — simple and exactWinfield High School, Winfield MO — expected graduation 2027
SkillsBank entries WITH receipts that fit a job: reliability, multi-step directions, money counting, teamwork“Follows multi-step directions accurately” • “Counts money and makes change”
ExperienceJobs, chores-for-others, volunteering, school roles — run through the formula: WHAT + WHERE + WHEN + ONE duty“Library helper, Winfield HS, 2025–26 — reshelved and organized returns daily”

Résumé language — the two polish moves

Start lines with the doing word: “Reshelved books” beats “I was someone who reshelved books” — shorter, stronger, fits the page. Numbers when you have them: “Cared for 3 dogs daily” beats “cared for dogs.” True + specific + short: that’s the whole style guide.

Make the Cut

Which skills-bank entry earns a résumé spot for a stocking job at a grocery store?
Fit + receipts = a spot. Gaming skill is real but doesn’t map to shelves; “chill” has no receipt anyone can check. Choose for the JOB reading it.
Turn this into a résumé line: you helped your uncle at his car lot last summer, mostly washing and moving cars.
Formula, doing-words, dates — check, check, check. The vague version wastes the spot; the inflated title fails the receipts rule the second anyone asks one question about it.
You have seven true skills but the template fits five. You…
Choosing IS the skill today. The other two live safely in your bank for the day a different job wants exactly them — nothing true is ever lost, just benched.

Day 3 • Learn

The Résumé Sweep

PassChecking for
1. Contact passName spelled right and BIG; phone and email exact, digit by digit — a résumé with a wrong phone number is a paper airplane
2. Truth passEvery line has its receipt — could you back it up if asked in an interview? (You will be.)
3. Style passDoing-words first, dates on experiences, no “I” sentences, nothing red-zone on the page
4. Six-second passHold it at arm’s length: clean? one page? name jumps out? best material in the top third?

Then the final move: someone ELSE’s eyes. Week 8 taught it on applications — same law here. You’ve read your own page so many times your brain autocorrects it; a partner catches in ten seconds what you’ve skipped ten times.

Day 3 • You Try It

Sweep Practice

Which mistake does the SIX-SECOND pass catch that the other passes miss?
Passes 1–3 read words; pass 4 sees the PAGE the way the employer’s six seconds will. Both kinds of wrong lose interviews — that’s why the sweep has four passes, not three.
During the truth pass you hit “organized stockroom weekly” — you did it… twice, total. You…
Trim to true — don’t delete the real thing underneath. The truth pass exists because interviewers pull threads; lines that survive pulling are the only kind worth printing.
Your partner flags that your email on the page reads “gamil.com.” Why is THIS the most valuable catch of the day?
The paper-airplane problem: perfect page, unreachable person. And it’s ALWAYS a partner who catches it — which is the whole argument for the someone-else’s-eyes rule.
Your résumé will change over the years — new jobs, new skills. What’s the habit that keeps it alive instead of letting it rot in a drawer?
Think first — then click to reveal
Feed the bank, refresh the page: every time you finish something with a receipt — a season of work, a new responsibility — it goes in the skills bank, and once or twice a year the best of the bank gets promoted to the page. The résumé you finish today isn’t your last; it’s your FIRST, and updating is a ten-minute job forever after.
