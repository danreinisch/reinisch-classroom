Decide How Fast to Act

Emergency, Urgent, or Routine?

Emergency / immediate danger

Stop, move to safety when possible, alert the appropriate person, and use the workplace emergency procedure.

Urgent problem

Work cannot safely or accurately continue. Stop the affected task and contact the supervisor promptly.

Routine problem

No immediate danger, but a schedule, direction, supply, or record needs clarification. Report it before it causes a larger issue.

Not sure?

Pause the task and ask. Uncertainty is information.

Day 1 • Problem Map

Ask Four Useful Questions

  1. What happened? State the observable event.
  2. What do I know? Name the schedule, instruction, record, or condition you can verify.
  3. What could be affected? Safety, task accuracy, customer, materials, cash, hours, or pay?
  4. What is needed? Clarification, a backup plan, a correction, supplies, or immediate help?

Day 2 • Backup Ladder

Try the Safe, Approved Options

  1. Confirm the shift time and how late the transportation may be.
  2. Use a pre-approved backup ride or transportation option if available.
  3. Contact the supervisor as early as possible using the workplace procedure.
  4. Give an honest arrival estimate; do not promise a time you cannot meet.
  5. Follow the supervisor’s direction about reporting, waiting, or missing the shift.

A backup plan is prepared before the problem whenever possible. It does not include unsafe rides or leaving without permission.

Day 2 • Communicate Early

Before the Shift Is Better Than After

Useful message

“This is Ana. I am scheduled at 4:00 PM. My ride has a delay, and my current arrival estimate is 4:30. I am trying my approved backup ride. What would you like me to do?”

Missing information

“I might be late.” This does not identify the worker, shift, current estimate, action already tried, or needed direction.

Day 3 • Unclear Directions

Repeat What You Understand

Clarification pattern

“I understand that I should _____. I am unsure whether _____ comes before or after _____. Could you please show or explain that step?”

Why this works

It shows the worker listened and identifies the exact missing step.

What to avoid

Do not pretend to understand, copy another worker without permission, or create your own procedure.

Day 4 • Include Relevant Details

Enough Information, Not Every Detail

Include when relevant

  • Worker name and shift
  • Location or equipment
  • Exact schedule, hour, price, or total
  • What was already tried safely
  • What direction is needed

Leave out

  • Guesses presented as facts
  • Blame and insults
  • Unrelated personal stories
  • Private customer or account information
  • Promises outside your authority
