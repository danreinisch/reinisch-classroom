Day 1 • You Try It

Make the Match

Posting A requires “reliable and on time.” Devon hasn’t missed first hour all semester. Does Devon meet the requirement?
Employers want evidence of a habit. School attendance is real evidence — you’re building your work record right now.
A posting requires “basic cash handling.” You’ve done the count-up strategy with practice currency in this class. That requirement is a…
Practice is where every worker starts. “I’ve practiced counting change and making exact amounts” is a real answer.
Posting B requires “comfortable talking with customers all shift.” Riley is honest: talking to strangers all day drains them completely. Best move?
Last week’s rule still rules: requirements are honesty checks, not dares. A true ✗ on one posting points you toward a better one.
Think of one skill you use OUTSIDE school — gaming, cooking, caring for someone, fixing things. Translate it into employer words.

Day 2 • You Try It

Grid Check

Posting A’s schedule is Sat–Sun 8am–2pm. Look at the example grid. Does it fit?
Reading the grid AGAINST the posting is the skill. A partial fit is worth a question, not a guess.
An interviewer asks, “Can you work Wednesday evenings?” The grid says Practice. Best answer?
Honest + specific + offers what you CAN do. Employers respect a clear answer far more than a broken promise.
Why do we lock school hours into the grid FIRST?
A job that costs you school isn’t a fit — it’s a trade you don’t make. Protecting the locked blocks protects your future.
Before you build yours: predict how many OPEN blocks your week really has. High or low, what does that number tell you about the kind of schedule to look for?
Think first — then click to reveal
Lots of open blocks → you can consider more postings. Few open blocks → you hunt for “flexible hours” or weekend-only postings. Neither is bad — the grid just aims your search.

Plan Backward from the Start Time

StepThe questionExample: shift starts 8:00am
1When should I ARRIVE?7:45 — ten to fifteen minutes early, every time
2How do I get there?City bus, stop one block from the clinic
3How long is the trip, door to door?20 minutes ride + 5 minutes walking = 25 minutes
4When do I LEAVE home?7:15 — and set the alarm with time to get ready
5What’s my BACKUP?Missed bus → call my ride list; running late → call the job FIRST

Rides, buses, bikes, and walking all count — what matters is the plan. Workers with a written ride plan and a backup are the ones the schedule can count on.

Day 3 • You Try It

Run the Numbers

Shift starts at 4:00pm. You want to arrive by 3:45. The walk takes 30 minutes. When do you leave?
Backward: 3:45 arrival − 30 minutes = 3:15 departure. The math is subtraction — the habit is the win.
Your ride texts: “Car won’t start.” Your shift starts in an hour. FIRST move?
Problems happen to everyone. Calling EARLY with a plan is what separates a keeper from a no-show.
Two postings, same pay. Job 1: 10 minutes away. Job 2: 55 minutes away, two buses. Which piece of the fit test is this?
An hour each way turns a 6-hour shift into an 8-hour day. Trip time belongs in every fit decision.
Posting A: Saturday shift, 8:00am start. Using YOUR real transportation, when would you have to leave home — and what’s your backup?
Think first — then click to reveal
Sample: “Arrive 7:45. Mom drives me, 15 minutes — leave 7:30, alarm at 6:45. Backup: my uncle, or the 7:10 bus. If both fail, I call the clinic before 7:30.” Notice: names, times, and a phone-first rule.
