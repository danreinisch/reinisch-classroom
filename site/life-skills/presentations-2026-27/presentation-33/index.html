Change Must Fit Between Price and Payment

Price $6.50, payment $10.00. Which change is possible?
$6.50 + $3.50 returns to the $10 payment.
Price $8.00, payment $5.00. What is true?
The payment does not cover the price, so more money is needed.

Day 3 • Receipt Check

ITEMS → TOTAL → PAYMENT → CHANGE

  1. Do the listed items match the purchase?
  2. Does the stated total look correct for the provided simplified example?
  3. Does the payment line match what was handed over?
  4. Does the change line match expected change?
  5. If something differs, name the exact line and ask the cashier/supervisor to check.

Day 3 • Error Hunt

Which Line Is Wrong?

Receipt total $6.00; cash $10.00; change $3.00.
$10 − $6 = $4 expected change.
Receipt says cash $20, but the customer handed over $10.
The payment record affects expected change and should be checked.

Day 3 • Report an Error

Name the Exact Numbers

Professional statement

“Excuse me. My total is $6.00 and I paid $10.00. The receipt shows $3.00 change, but I expected $4.00. Could we please check the transaction?”

Keep the receipt and money organized while the transaction is reviewed. Do not accuse the cashier of stealing based on an unverified mismatch.
