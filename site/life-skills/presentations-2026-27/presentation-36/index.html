Day 1 • Read by Category

Do Not Read Two Paragraphs at Once

  1. Find the job title and main duties in both offers.
  2. Find the location and transportation information.
  3. Find the days, start/end times, and weekly hours.
  4. Find the hourly wage and any listed costs or requirements.
  5. Find available supports or questions that still need answers.

Use a ruler, highlight, or one-row comparison chart to reduce switching errors.

Day 2 • Gross Is Not Take-Home

Use the Right Label

Expected gross

Hours × wage before deductions.

Take-home usefulness

What remains useful after deductions and relevant work costs. A precise net amount is not known unless provided.

Say: “Job B has $224 expected weekly gross earnings before deductions.”

Day 3 • Support Fit

Support Is Part of the Decision

Questions

  • Is the worker trained before working alone?
  • Can written or visual supports be used?
  • Is help available for unclear directions?
  • Are communication expectations manageable?

Self-advocacy

“I complete multi-step tasks accurately with a written checklist. Is an approved task list available or permitted in this position?”

A needed support does not automatically make a job a bad fit. The worker needs accurate information about whether the support can be used.

Day 4 • Evidence Sentence

Sample decision

“I would choose Job A for this fictional worker. Job B pays $16 more gross each week, but Job A has reliable transportation and lower travel cost. Job A also already offers the visual task list the worker uses successfully.”

Strong evidence: acknowledges the pay difference and explains why schedule, cost, transportation, duties, or supports outweigh it.

Day 4 • More Than One Defensible Choice

The Worker’s Situation Controls

Job A may fit if...

Reliable weekday transportation, shorter shifts, visual task support, and lower travel cost matter most.

Job B may fit if...

Weekend availability is strong, transportation becomes reliable, customer communication is a strength, and the support question is resolved.

The goal is evidence-based reasoning, not forcing every student to select the same offer.
