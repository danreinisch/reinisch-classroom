The form asks…Your grid answers…
Days/hours available (Mon–Sun boxes)Copy ONLY your OPEN blocks: “Mon–Fri after 4pm; Sat 8am–8pm; Sun morning”
Full-time or part-time?Part-time — school is your full-time job; this box protects it
Date available to startA real date you could actually begin — not “ASAP” if you need two weeks
Hours desired per weekCount your open blocks and be realistic — 10–15 is a strong student start

The locked blocks from Week 4 stay locked. An application is not the place to “sound flexible” by giving away school hours or practice — a schedule you can’t keep becomes a no-show, and no-shows don’t stay hired.

Who Counts — and Who Doesn’t

Strong references

  • A teacher who sees your work habits — effort, attendance, follow-through
  • A coach, club sponsor, or activity leader
  • Someone you’ve worked FOR: babysitting families, lawn customers, volunteer coordinators
  • A family friend who has watched you handle responsibility — adult, not related

Weak or off-limits

  • Relatives — most forms say no; employers expect family to say nice things
  • Your best friend, same age — loyalty isn’t evidence
  • Someone who barely knows you — a famous name who couldn’t pick you out of a crowd helps nobody
  • Anyone you haven’t ASKED — more on that tomorrow

The test for every candidate: Has this adult actually SEEN me be responsible — and would they say so on the phone? Seen + would say so = a real reference.

The Ask Script + the Info Card

The ask, three sentences

1. “I’m applying for a job as a ___ at ___.”

2. “Would you be willing to be a reference for me?”

3. “They might call or email you to ask about me — is that okay?”

Then: “Thank you” — whatever they answer. If it’s yes, tell them when you apply. If they get a call, thank them again.

Gather for each yes

  • Full name, spelled right
  • Job title (“Teacher, Winfield High School”)
  • Relationship to you (“teacher, two years”)
  • Phone — the one THEY want used
  • Email — letter-perfect, Week 5 rules

Asking first isn’t just polite — it turns a cold call into a warm one. A reference who’s expecting the call answers ready, remembers your name, and has a sentence prepared. That’s the whole game.
