The Four Kinds of Work

KindWho it’s forWhat you getExample
JobAn employerPaycheck (a wage)Bagging groceries at the store
ChoreYour householdA working home; sometimes allowanceTaking out the trash, dishes
VolunteerYour community, by choiceExperience, helping othersWalking dogs at the animal shelter
HobbyYourselfFun, skill, relaxationDrawing, gaming, fishing

The secret connection

Chores, volunteering, and hobbies all build job skills. Walking shelter dogs is how people become paid kennel assistants. Employers love seeing volunteer work on an application.

Day 1 • You Try It

Sort It Out

Mowing YOUR OWN lawn because your family expects it is a…
Same mower, different category: mow a NEIGHBOR’S lawn for $20 and it becomes a job.
Maria spends Saturdays helping serve meals at the community center. No pay. That’s a…
By choice + for the community + unpaid = volunteering. It also looks great on a job application.
DeShawn gets paid $12/hour to stock shelves on weekends. That’s a…
Employer + schedule + paycheck = job. The wage is the giveaway.
Think of one activity YOU do. Which of the four is it — and could it ever become one of the others?

Match the Duty to the Job

“Keep shelves full, face the labels forward, help customers find things.” Which category?
Stocking + customers = retail. Same skills work at any store in town.
“Wear gloves and safety shoes; pick items from bins; pack and label boxes.” Which category?
Picking, packing, labeling, safety gear — that’s warehouse work.
Jordan loves being outdoors and doesn’t mind sweating. Best category to explore first?
Preferences point to categories: outdoors + physical = grounds crew is the natural first look.
Pick the category that sounds MOST like you — and the one that sounds LEAST like you. Why?

Day 3 • Learn

Strengths + Preferences = Fit

Strength words

careful • on time • friendly • strong • patient • organized • good with hands • good memory • follows steps • notices details • keeps going when it’s hard

Preference pairs (circle one from each)

Quiet ↔ Busy  •  Indoors ↔ Outdoors  •  Alone ↔ With people  •  Sitting ↔ Moving  •  Same tasks ↔ Variety

Watch me (I do)

My example: strengths — organized, patient, notices details. Preferences — quiet, indoors, same tasks. Good fit: file clerk, library aide, stocker before store hours. Bad fit: lunch-rush counter server. Same person — the setting decides.

Day 3 • You Try It

Fit Check

Alexis is friendly, talks easily with strangers, and loves variety. Better fit?
People-energy + variety = customer-facing work. Her strengths ARE the job.
Sam is strong, likes moving all day, and prefers not to chat much at work. Better fit?
Moving + strength + less talk = warehouse or grounds work fits like a glove.
A strength is something you…
Strengths need receipts too! “I’m organized — I keep my binder perfect” beats “I’m organized, trust me.”
Name one of YOUR strengths + one preference — then a job that matches both.
Think first — then click to reveal
Example: “patient + likes animals + outdoors” → kennel assistant or dog walker. Strength + preference + interest = the fit formula.

Pick Your Three

Your task Choose three possible jobs and complete a why-it-fits frame for each:

The frame

“___ could fit me because I like ___ (interest), I am good at ___ (strength), and I prefer ___ (preference).”

Worked example

“Kennel assistant could fit me because I like animals, I am good at being patient and following steps, and I prefer moving around instead of sitting.”

These three jobs become our targets: later this year we’ll read real postings, practice applications, and interview for jobs like these.

Interest (what I like) + Strength (what I’m good at) + Preference (conditions where I do my best) = a job that fits

careful • on time • friendly • strong • patient • organized • good with hands • good memory • follows steps • notices details • determined

Preference pairs

Indoors ↔ Outdoors
Alone ↔ With people
Sitting ↔ Moving
