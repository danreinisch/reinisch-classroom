Protect Personal Information

Pay Records Are Private

Class practice

Use only teacher-created fictional pay records. The sample worker in this deck is not a real person.

Real-life safety

Do not post or casually share a pay stub. It may contain an address, employee number, bank information, or other private details.

Today’s habit: When checking a real pay record, work with the employee, an approved support person, or the correct payroll/supervisor contact.

Day 2 • Learn

Deductions Reduce Gross to Net

Gross pay

The full amount earned before deductions. In Taylor’s sample: $240.

Net pay

The amount remaining after listed deductions. In Taylor’s sample: $204.

$240 gross − $36 deductions = $204 net

A smaller net amount does not automatically mean an error. First read the deductions and check the arithmetic.

Day 2 • Direct Deposit

Net Pay May Move Electronically

Direct deposit

An electronic payment to the employee’s selected account. The statement may show the net amount and a masked account reference.

Safety habit

Verify changes to bank information through the employer’s official process. Do not send account numbers in an ordinary message.

A pay statement is still important even when no paper check is handed to the worker.

Day 3 • Comparison Routine

Dates → Hours → Rate → Gross

  1. Do both records cover the same date range?
  2. Do the timecard’s paid hours match the paycheck hours?
  3. Does the hourly rate match the agreed or listed rate?
  4. Does hours × rate match the gross amount?
  5. If not, mark the exact mismatch and ask for help checking it.

Wrap-Up

Week 27 Quick Reference

Read the paycheck

Pay period → hours → rate → gross → deductions → net.

Check the relationship

Hours × rate should connect to gross. Gross − listed deductions should connect to net.

Compare records

Use the same pay period. Check timecard hours against paycheck hours.

Report a mismatch

Name both records and exact numbers, request a check, save evidence, and protect private information.
