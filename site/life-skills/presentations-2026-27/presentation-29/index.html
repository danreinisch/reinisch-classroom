The Customer-Service Routine

StepWorker action
GREETUse a simple professional welcome.
LISTENLet the customer finish and identify the actual need.
RESPONDAnswer only what you know and are authorized to do.
REFERGet a supervisor or correct employee when the answer or decision is outside your role.
Success target: Help accurately without inventing prices, change, refund promises, or policies.

Professional Does Not Mean Robotic

Clear, Calm, and Respectful

Useful behaviors

  • Face or orient toward the interaction as appropriate.
  • Use a clear greeting or agreed communication method.
  • Allow the customer to finish.
  • Repeat key details.
  • Ask for help when needed.

Not required

  • Forced eye contact
  • A perfect smile or scripted personality
  • Knowing every store policy
  • Accepting insults or threats
  • Making decisions without authority

Day 1 • Listen

Do Not Answer the First Few Words

  1. Let the customer finish the request.
  2. Listen for the item, location, price, payment, or problem.
  3. Repeat the key detail: “You are asking whether this is $8.50, correct?”
  4. Answer if you know and are authorized—or explain that you will find help.

Repeating important numbers reduces price and payment mistakes.

Day 2 • Money Accuracy

Never Invent a Price, Total, or Change

Verify

Repeat the amount, check the label/system, and compare the information.

Pause

If amounts conflict or the system behaves unexpectedly, stop the affected transaction step.

Refer

Ask the cashier lead, supervisor, or authorized employee to verify.

Do not promise

A refund, discount, free item, or specific outcome unless your role allows it.

Day 3 • Calm Response

Do Not Match the Customer’s Volume

  1. Keep your own voice and body calm; use your communication support if needed.
  2. Let the customer state the concern without interrupting when it is safe.
  3. Repeat the problem briefly: “You expected $10, but the receipt shows $12.”
  4. State what you can do: verify information or get the supervisor.
  5. Step away and use the safety procedure if there are threats, harassment, or danger.

Day 3 • Acknowledge Without Promising

Recognize the Problem, Not an Unverified Outcome

Useful

“I understand that the two prices are different. I will ask my supervisor to verify the correct price.”

“I can see that this has been frustrating. A manager handles refunds, and I will contact them.”

Avoid

“You are definitely right.”
“I promise you will get a refund.”
“Calm down.”
“That is not my problem.”

Day 4 • Full Routine

From Hello to Help

  1. Greet: open the interaction.
  2. Listen: let the customer explain.
  3. Verify: repeat the item, location, price, total, or concern.
  4. Respond: answer verified information within your role.
  5. Refer: get the authorized person when needed.
  6. Close: explain what happens next and thank the customer for waiting.

Day 4 • Verify the Amount

Repeat Numbers Clearly

Sample interaction

Customer: “This should be two for $8, but the screen shows $10.”
Worker: “You expected a total of $8, and the screen shows $10. I will pause and ask my supervisor to verify the offer.”

What two amounts should the worker report?
Repeat the customer’s expected total and the screen total
$8 expected and $10 displayed. Exact amounts help the supervisor check the issue.

Wrap-Up

Week 29 Quick Reference

The routine

GREET → LISTEN → RESPOND → REFER

Repeat important item, price, total, and payment information accurately.

The boundary

Answer verified information within your role. Do not guess or promise discounts, refunds, or policy exceptions.

If unsafe: Disengage and use the workplace safety procedure. Customer service never outranks personal safety.

Next week: identify workplace hazards, use safety signs and protective equipment, and report injuries or emergencies.
