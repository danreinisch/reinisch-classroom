Posting Anatomy: The Six Pieces

PieceWhat it tells youAsk yourself
1. Job titleWhat the job is calledDo I know what this job actually does?
2. Pay / wageMoney per hour (or per year)Is this per hour or per year?
3. ScheduleDays, hours, part-time or full-timeCan I actually be there at these times?
4. DutiesThe tasks you’ll do all dayWould I want to do these EVERY day?
5. RequirementsWhat you must have or be able to doDo I meet these — honestly?
6. How to applyThe next stepOnline? In person? What do I bring?

Day 1 • You Try It

Find the Piece

“$13.50/hour” is which piece of the posting?
The /hour matters: $13.50/hour ≈ $27,000/year full-time. Always check whether pay is per hour or per year.
“Walk dogs, clean kennels, feed and water animals” is which piece?
Duties are the job’s daily reality. Read them twice — you’ll be doing them, not the title.
A posting lists no pay at all. Best move?
Missing information isn’t a trap or a promise — it’s a question you get to ask.
Look at Posting A. Which piece would matter MOST to you personally — and why?

There’s no wrong answer: some people check schedule first (rides, other commitments), some check duties (can I love this daily?), some check pay. Knowing YOUR first-check piece makes you a faster reader.

Day 2 • Learn

Code Words, Translated

The posting says…It really means…
“Part-time”Usually under 30 hours/week — good for starting out
“Weekends required”Saturdays and Sundays are work days — plan around it
“Must lift 30 lbs”Physical work — about the weight of a full laundry basket
“Reliable transportation”You can get there on time, every time — bus, ride, bike, or walk all count
“Fast-paced environment”Busy, quick, lots happening — think lunch rush
“No experience necessary”They will train you — great first-job signal

Requirements are honesty checks, not dares. “Must lift 30 lbs” with a bad back isn’t a challenge to push through — it’s information that this job isn’t your fit. That’s the posting doing you a favor.

Day 2 • You Try It

Crack the Code

A posting says “reliable transportation required.” You take the city bus that stops one block away. Do you qualify?
Employers care about arrival, not the vehicle. Knowing your bus route IS reliable transportation.
Posting A requires “able to lift 30 lbs.” That’s about…
Translating numbers into real objects keeps requirements honest — and keeps you safe.
“No experience necessary” means…
First jobs exist! This phrase is how postings say “beginners welcome.”
Posting A’s schedule is Sat–Sun 8am–2pm. Name one thing in a person’s life that schedule would collide with — and one kind of person it’s perfect for.
Think first — then click to reveal
Collides with: weekend sports, family events, sleeping in. Perfect for: a student free on weekends who wants weekdays clear. Same schedule, different lives — that’s why fit is personal.

Day 3 • Learn

The Fit Test: Formula Meets Posting

StepQuestionWhere to look
1Does the work match my interests?Duties
2Do my strengths match what they need?Requirements + duties
3Do the conditions match my preferences?Schedule + duties (busy? outdoors? with people?)
4Can I honestly meet the requirements?Your checklist from yesterday

Worked example — Jordan vs. Posting A

Jordan: loves animals (interest ✓), patient and follows routines (strengths ✓), prefers moving around, doesn’t mind mess (preferences ✓), can lift 30 lbs and has a Saturday bus route (requirements ✓). Verdict: strong fit — apply. Same posting for someone allergic to dogs? Instant no — and that’s a WIN: fifteen seconds of reading saved weeks of misery.

Day 3 • You Try It

Make the Call

Posting B: Counter Server — fast-paced lunch rush, constant customers, loud kitchen. Alexis loves talking to people and thrives on variety. Fit?
Match the conditions to the person: “fast-paced” is a warning to some and a welcome mat to others.
Sam is strong and steady but hates noise and crowds. Posting A (kennel, weekend mornings) vs. Posting B (lunch-rush counter). Better fit?
Both are real jobs; only one is Sam’s job. The fit test isn’t about good/bad postings — it’s about the match.
A posting fails your fit test in ten seconds. What did you just win?
Job search is a matching game, not a test you fail. Fast, honest no’s get you to the right yes sooner.
Your turn: take ONE of your three possible jobs from last week. If a posting for it appeared tomorrow, which piece would you check first — and what answer would make it a yes?
