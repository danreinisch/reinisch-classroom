The Cast — and Their Hairs

CoinValueHairsRemember it by
Quarter25¢5 hairsBiggest coin, ridged edge, George faces left

Nickel5¢1 hairThick and smooth-edged
Penny1¢no hairsThe only copper one — counted last, one by one

How Hairy Money works

Draw hairs on each coin: every hair is worth 5. Quarter gets 5 hairs (25), dime gets 2 (10), nickel gets 1 (5), penny gets none. To count ANY pile: count the hairs by 5s, then add pennies by 1s. Bills need no hairs — $1, $5, $10, $20 say their value right on the front.

Day 1 • You Try It

Name That Coin

The smallest-SIZED coin in your hand is worth…
The dime is the trickster of the cast: tiny body, double the nickel’s value. Size lies; hairs don’t.
How many hairs does a quarter get, and why?
Five hairs, count them “5, 10, 15, 20, 25.” One quarter, one hand, done.
Why does the penny get NO hairs?
Hairs are the 5s crew; pennies are the 1s crew. Keeping them separate is the whole system.
A friend asks: “Why draw hairs at all — why not just add the numbers?” What’s your answer?
Think first — then click to reveal
Adding 25 + 10 + 5 in your head means juggling different numbers. Hairs turn every coin into the SAME move — count by 5s — so your brain does one easy job instead of four hard ones. Same answer, way less sweat.

The Line-Up Method

StepDo thisExample: 6 dimes
1Line the coins up in a row — no blobs● ● ● ● ● ●
2Hair each coin (draw or picture them)2 hairs each → 12 hairs total
3Touch and count hairs by 5s5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30… up to 60
4Say the total with the cent sign“60¢”

Bills are the easy crew

Same-bill groups skip-count by the bill: three $5 bills → 5, 10, 15 → $15. Four $10s → 10, 20, 30, 40 → $40. Line up, touch, count — same rhythm, bigger numbers.

Touching each coin as you count is not babyish — it’s what bank tellers do. Touch keeps count; eyes alone lose it.

Count the Group

5 nickels, lined up. Hairs and total?
One hair per nickel, five nickels, five hairs — count them by 5s to 25¢. Notice: 5 nickels = 1 quarter. The hairs agree: 5 hairs either way.
3 quarters. Fastest hair count?
5 hairs each × 3 coins = 15 hairs: 5, 10, 15… 75¢. With practice you’ll jump “25, 50, 75” — the hairs build that shortcut for you.
Seven $10 bills. Total?
Bills say their own value — your only job is a clean skip-count with a touch per bill.
Back to the warm-up: 4 dimes vs. 6 nickels. Settle it with hairs, out loud.
Think first — then click to reveal
4 dimes = 8 hairs = 40¢. 6 nickels = 6 hairs = 30¢. The dimes win — and the hairs knew before the math did. More coins does NOT mean more money.

Big to Small

StepDo thisExample pocket: $5 + $1 + $1 + 2 quarters + 1 dime + 3 pennies
1Sort biggest to smallest5 • 1 • 1 • Q • Q • D • P P P
2Bills first — skip-count5, 6, 7 → $7
3Coin hairs by 5s (pennies wait)Q+Q+D = 12 hairs → 60¢
4Pennies by 1s61, 62, 63 → 63¢
5Say it whole“Seven dollars and sixty-three cents” — $7.63

Big to small isn’t just neat — it front-loads the easy wins. Bills knock out most of the total in seconds, hairs sweep the silver, pennies tip in last. Backwards (pennies first) buries you in 1s before the real money even starts.

Day 3 • You Try It

Tame the Pile

Pocket: one $10, one $1, 1 quarter, 2 dimes. Walk it big to small — total?
Bills: 10, 11. Hairs: quarter 5 + dimes 2+2 = 9 hairs → 5…45. $11.45 — and not a single hard sum anywhere.
Mid-count of a big pile, someone asks you a question and you lose your place. Best move?
Every money counter loses count sometimes; pros just restart the step without drama. Guessing is how registers come up short.
Two quarters, one nickel, four pennies — someone counts “1, 2, 3, 4… then uh…” What went wrong?
Order is the strategy. Hairs first: 11 hairs → 55¢, then 56, 57, 58, 59. Smooth — because the 1s came last.
Warm-up pocket, for real now: $5, two $1s, 2 quarters, 3 pennies. Count it big to small, out loud — then compare to your earlier guess.
Think first — then click to reveal
Bills: 5, 6, 7. Hairs: 10 → 50¢. Pennies: 51, 52, 53. $7.53. If your guess was close — your money sense is waking up. That instinct plus the method is the whole skill.

Day 4 • Do

Build the Amounts

Your task Eight target amounts, easiest to toughest (like 35¢, 80¢, $1.25, $3.41, $7.68). For each: build it big-to-small from your bag, hair-check your build by counting it back, and record which pieces you used. Last two targets: build each a SECOND way.

The builder’s move

Take the biggest piece that fits, subtract it in your head (or on the line), repeat. $3.41 → three $1s → 41¢ left → quarter (16¢ left) → dime (6¢) → nickel (1¢) → penny. Count it back with hairs to prove it.

Check out: Build my mystery amount at the desk — first build, no do-overs. You’re ready.
