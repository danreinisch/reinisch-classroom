Day 1 • Meaning

“$13 per Hour” Is Not $13 Total

One hour

1 hour × $13 = $13

Three hours

3 hours × $13 = $39

A worker earns $13 per hour and works two hours. What gross amount is expected?
Two groups of $13
$26. The worker earns the $13 rate for each of the two hours.

The hourly rate stays the same in this simplified example. The total changes when the hours change.

Day 2 • Calculator Path

Press the Meaning, Not Random Buttons

  1. Find and write the paid hours.
  2. Find and write the hourly wage.

Day 3 • Compare Fit

Higher Pay Is Not Automatically the Better Fit

Questions to ask

  • Can I reliably get there and home?
  • Do the hours conflict with school or other responsibilities?
  • Can I manage the shift length?
  • Do I understand the duties?

Evidence sentence

“I would choose Job __ because ____. It pays about $____ gross, and the schedule [does/does not] fit because ____.”

More hours and a higher wage can increase gross earnings, but an impossible schedule is not a dependable choice.

Day 4 • Weekly Routine

From Schedule to Expected Gross

  1. Read each shift and find the paid hours.
  2. Add the paid hours for the week.
  3. Find the hourly wage.
  4. Multiply total weekly hours × hourly wage.
  5. Write the result as $___ expected gross earnings.

If paid hours are already provided, begin there. If breaks or partial hours make the example too complex, use the provided time chart or teacher-calculated hour total.
