The Purchase Routine

PRICE → HAVE → PAY → CHECK

StepQuestion
PRICEWhat is the stated total?
HAVEHow much money is available? Is it enough?
PAYCan I make exact payment? If not, what appropriate amount covers the price?
CHECKDoes the stated price and money handed over match the transaction?
Practice materials: Fake money and teacher-created prices only. Keep real cash and student financial information out of class demonstrations.

Day 2 • Organize First

Dollars Together, Coins Together

  1. Read the price and place it where you can see it.
  2. Build the dollar amount with bills.
  3. Build the cents with coins or a supported coin-value tool.
  4. Count the full payment once.
  5. Compare the payment to the price before handing it over.
Example: $6.25 = six dollars + twenty-five cents.

Day 3 • Payment Ladder

Exact First, Then the Next Useful Amount

  1. Can I make the exact total accurately?
  2. If not, which bill or combination is at least the total?
  3. Is there a smaller covering amount than the one I selected?
  4. Can I state how much I handed over?
  5. Am I ready to check the expected change next week?

Day 3 • Check Understanding

Which Payment Makes Sense?

Price: $4.50. The worker has a $5 bill and a $20 bill.
The $5 bill covers the total and produces a smaller, easier change amount.
Price: $6.00. The worker can make $6 exactly.
Exact payment is appropriate when the worker can build and verify it accurately.

Catch the Mistake Before Handing It Over

Price card: $9.00. The worker counts $8.00.
Compare the counted payment to the stated price before completing the transaction.

Wrap-Up

Week 32 Quick Reference

Purchase routine

Read the total, compare available money, choose the payment, and verify it.

Payment choices
