Day 2 • Learn

The Review Sweep

PassYou’re hunting…How
1. Blank passEmpty boxesFinger on every box, top to bottom — filled or N/A, no exceptions
2. Contact passUnreachable applicantCount the phone digits; read the email character by character
3. Half-answer passStarted but not finishedExperience lines missing formula parts; references missing phone or email
4. Sense passAnswers that can’t be rightStart date before today? Available “anytime” from a student? Grade 12 completed by a sophomore?

Four passes, one finger, two minutes. The sweep is boring on purpose — boring is what catches things. Pilots and surgeons use checklists for the same reason: not because they’re bad at their jobs, because they’re serious about them.

Day 2 • You Try It

Sweep Practice

An application lists the phone as 63-555-0142. Which pass catches it, and what’s wrong?
Counting digits sounds silly until it saves an interview. Nine digits reaches nobody.
Experience line: “Babysitting.” One word. Which pass flags it, and what’s missing?
A box with one word isn’t blank — but it isn’t done either. The formula is the finish line: WHAT + WHERE + WHEN + ONE duty.
“Date available to start: 1/5/2025” — on an application dated October 2026. Which pass, what’s wrong?
The sense pass asks one question per answer: “could this be true?” A start date before today can’t. Writers skim; reviewers ask.
Why do we sweep with a finger on each box instead of just reading the page?
Think first — then click to reveal
Eyes skim — they see what they expect. A finger forces one box at a time, no skipping. It’s the difference between looking at a page and checking it.

Day 3 • Learn

The Repair Rules

The problemThe repair
Blank boxFill it with the true answer — or N/A if it truly doesn’t apply
“Anything” under PositionReplace with the exact job title from the posting
One-word experienceRebuild with the formula: WHAT + WHERE + WHEN + ONE duty
Skill with no receipt (“best worker ever”)Swap in a bankable skill you can back up out loud
“Anytime” availabilityReplace with real open blocks from a grid
Reference who was never askedRemove — substitute someone who said yes, full info card
Messy, unreadable writingOn paper, that’s a rewrite — which is why we practice the block first

Repairs must stay TRUE. Fixing means completing and correcting — never inventing. A repaired application is the same honest person, presented the way they deserve.

Day 3 • You Try It

Fix the Line

The bad application says: Position applied for — “idk whatever is open lol.” Best repair?
Casual words tell the employer the applicant isn’t serious. The repair is specific, not stiff — just the real title.
Skills section says: “super chill, funny, everyone loves me.” Repair?
There’s a real skill buried in there — people skills. The repair digs it out and dresses it in words a manager can use.
The reference listed is “my mom (she’ll say I’m great).” Repair?
Renaming a relative doesn’t change the relationship — and forms ask. Week 7’s test still rules: seen it + would say so + not related.
The bad application belongs to someone with real skills and real experience — you can tell. So what actually failed here: the person, or the paper? What does that mean for YOUR paper?
Think first — then click to reveal
The paper failed the person. Managers never met the applicant — only the form. That’s the whole reason this month exists: your paper should be as good as you are.
