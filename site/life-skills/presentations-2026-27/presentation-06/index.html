The form asks…You write…
School nameWinfield High School — the full, official name
City / StateWinfield, MO
Dates attendedStart year – Present (“Present” means you’re still there)
Currently attending?Yes — and it’s a GOOD answer, not a weak one
Graduated? / Diploma?“Expected 20__” — your expected graduation year
Highest grade completedThe last grade you FINISHED — not the one you’re in now

“Currently attending” tells an employer you show up somewhere every single weekday and answer to a schedule — that’s reliability with a paper trail. Still in school is a plus, not an apology.

Day 2 • Learn

What Counts — and the Formula

Experience that counts

  • Chores you own: mowing, dishes, laundry, trash routes
  • Babysitting or caring for family members
  • Pet care — yours or the neighbors’
  • Volunteering: church, school events, food drives
  • School jobs: library helper, office aide, team manager
  • Helping a family business or a neighbor — paid or not

The experience formula

WHAT I did + WHERE + WHEN + ONE duty

“Babysitter — for a neighbor family, Winfield — 2025–Present — cared for two children, including meals and bedtime routine.”

“Volunteer helper — school food drive — Fall 2025 — sorted and boxed donations for delivery.”

Unpaid does not mean unreal. The form is asking “have you ever been responsible for something?” — and you have. Write it with the formula and it reads like experience, because it IS.

Day 2 • You Try It

Does It Count?

Marcus has mowed three neighbors’ lawns every week, two summers running, for cash. Application experience?
“Lawn care — neighborhood customers — Summers 2024–2025 — mowed and trimmed three yards weekly, on schedule.” A manager reads that and sees a worker.
Which is the strongest experience line?
All four formula parts, one concrete duty. “Gave medicine” is a trust detail — those are gold.
Jade truly has no chores, no volunteering, nothing yet — she checked honestly. The experience section gets…
Honest N/A today, and experience is buildable THIS month — one food drive, one office-aide gig. The section isn’t a wall; it’s a to-do list.
Think of the most responsible thing you do in a normal week. Run it through the formula out loud: WHAT + WHERE + WHEN + ONE duty.
Think first — then click to reveal
If you got all four parts out, you just wrote an application line. If one part was fuzzy (“when” usually is), pin it down — “2025–Present” or “every weekend” both work.

Day 3 • You Try It

Bank It or Not?

Ty wants to bank “team player.” His receipt: works with a lab partner all year without drama, shares the work evenly. Bank it?
A year of drama-free shared work is exactly what “team player” means. School receipts are real receipts.
Which skill line is application-ready?
Specific skill + receipt. “Good at stuff” says nothing; big talk with no receipt says worse.
Lena’s dream posting wants “experience with cash registers.” She’s never touched one. Her bank has “counts change accurately” and “follows multi-step directions.” Move?
Registers are taught in a day — to people who can count change and follow steps. Lead with the true foundation; the signature at the bottom still promises honesty.
Pick the ONE skill from today’s table you’d put first on every application. What’s your receipt?
Think first — then click to reveal
Any answer with a receipt wins. Managers hire “reliable + proof” over a long list of maybes every single time — one bankable skill is a foundation, not a limit.
