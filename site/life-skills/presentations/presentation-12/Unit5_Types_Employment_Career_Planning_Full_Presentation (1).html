Types of Jobs

Full-time, part-time, seasonal, temporary, and gig work

Not All Jobs Are the Same

When you're looking for work, you'll see different types of jobs. Each type has different hours, pay, and benefits.

The main types of employment are:

  • Full-time — Usually 35-40+ hours per week
  • Part-time — Usually less than 35 hours per week
  • Seasonal — Only during certain times of year
  • Temporary (Temp) — For a limited time only
  • Gig work — Individual tasks or jobs, not a regular schedule

💡 Understanding these types helps you: Know what to expect, ask the right questions in interviews, and find the job that fits your life.

Full-Time Jobs

📅 Full-Time Employment

Hours: Usually 35-40+ hours per week

Schedule: Regular, consistent schedule (like Mon-Fri, 9-5)

Benefits: Usually includes health insurance, paid time off, retirement plans

Stability: Ongoing — no set end date

✅ Pros of full-time work:

  • Steady paycheck
  • Benefits (health insurance, vacation, etc.)
  • More job security
  • Opportunities to advance

⚠️ Things to consider:

  • Less flexibility in your schedule
  • Harder to balance with school
  • More commitment required

Part-Time Jobs

⏰ Part-Time Employment

Hours: Usually less than 35 hours per week (often 15-25)

Schedule: Can vary — might change week to week

Benefits: Usually NO benefits (but some companies offer them)

Stability: Ongoing, but hours can be cut

✅ Pros of part-time work:

  • More flexibility
  • Good for students or people with other commitments
  • Can work multiple part-time jobs
  • Good way to get experience

⚠️ Things to consider:

  • Less money (fewer hours)
  • Usually no health insurance or benefits
  • Hours might vary — paycheck isn't always the same
  • Fewer advancement opportunities

Seasonal Jobs

🎄☀️ Seasonal Employment

What it is: Jobs that only exist during certain times of the year

Length: A few weeks to a few months

Benefits: Usually none

🔹 Examples of seasonal jobs:

  • Holiday season (Nov-Dec): Retail stores, package delivery, gift wrapping
  • Summer: Lifeguard, camp counselor, amusement parks, lawn care
  • Tax season (Jan-Apr): Tax preparation offices
  • Harvest season: Farm work, orchards, pumpkin patches

💡 Good to know: Seasonal jobs are great for earning extra money. Some seasonal workers get invited back year after year, or even offered permanent jobs!

Temporary (Temp) Jobs

📋 Temporary Employment

What it is: A job with a set end date, or until a project is done

Length: Days, weeks, or months

How you find them: Often through a temp agency (staffing agency)

Benefits: Usually none (though some agencies offer them)

Why companies hire temp workers:

  • To cover for someone on leave (vacation, maternity leave)
  • To help with a busy period
  • To complete a specific project
  • To "try out" workers before offering permanent jobs

⭐ "Temp-to-hire" means the company might offer you a permanent job if you do well. Many people get full-time jobs this way!

Gig Work

📱 Gig Work / Gig Economy

What it is: Individual tasks or jobs — you're not an employee, you're a contractor

Schedule: You choose when and how much you work

Pay: Per task or per delivery — no hourly wage

Benefits: None — you're on your own

🔹 Examples of gig work:

  • Uber, Lyft (rideshare driving)
  • DoorDash, Uber Eats, Grubhub (food delivery)
  • Instacart (grocery shopping)
  • TaskRabbit (odd jobs)
  • Freelance work (writing, design, photography)

⚠️ With gig work, remember:

  • No guaranteed income — it depends on how much you work
  • You pay your own taxes (they're not taken out)
  • No benefits (health insurance, paid time off)
  • You may need your own car, phone, or equipment

Employee vs. Independent Contractor

Two different ways to work — and why it matters

Two Types of Workers

When you work for someone, you're either an employee or an independent contractor. These are very different!

👤 Employee

  • Works FOR a company
  • Boss controls when, where, and how you work
  • Company takes taxes out of your paycheck
  • May get benefits
  • Gets a W-2 at tax time

📄 Independent Contractor

  • Works for YOURSELF
  • You control when, where, and how you work
  • YOU pay your own taxes
  • No benefits
  • Gets a 1099 at tax time

⚠️ This matters a lot for taxes! If you're an independent contractor, nobody takes taxes out of your pay. You have to save money and pay taxes yourself.

Employee: The Details

As an employee, you work for a company. They're your employer.

As an employee:

  • Your boss tells you when to work and how to do your job
  • The company provides equipment (computer, tools, uniform)
  • Taxes are taken out of your paycheck automatically
  • You may get benefits (health insurance, paid time off)
  • The company pays part of your Social Security and Medicare taxes
  • You're protected by labor laws (minimum wage, overtime, etc.)

🔹 Examples of employee jobs:

  • Cashier at McDonald's
  • Warehouse worker at Amazon
  • Teacher at a school
  • Nurse at a hospital

Independent Contractor: The Details

As an independent contractor, you're basically running your own small business. You work for yourself.

As an independent contractor:

  • You decide when, where, and how to do the work
  • You provide your own equipment (car, tools, computer)
  • NO taxes are taken out — you get the full amount
  • No benefits — you buy your own health insurance
  • You pay BOTH halves of Social Security and Medicare (self-employment tax)
  • Fewer labor law protections

🔹 Examples of independent contractor work:

  • Uber/Lyft driver
  • DoorDash delivery person
  • Freelance graphic designer
  • House cleaner with your own clients

Taxes: The Big Difference

This is one of the most important differences between employees and contractors.

Employee Taxes

  • Employer takes taxes out of each paycheck
  • You get a W-2 form at year end
  • Employer pays half of Social Security/Medicare
  • Usually just file taxes once a year

Contractor Taxes

  • Nobody takes taxes out — you get the full amount
  • You get a 1099 form at year end
  • YOU pay all of Social Security/Medicare (15.3%!)
  • Should pay estimated taxes 4x per year

🚨 Big warning for contractors: If you earn $1,000 doing gig work, that's NOT $1,000 in your pocket. You'll owe taxes on that money! Save 25-30% for taxes, or you'll have a nasty surprise at tax time.

W-2 vs. 1099

These forms tell the IRS how much you earned.

W-2 Form (for employees)

  • Shows your total wages
  • Shows how much tax was already taken out
  • You get one from each employer
  • Due to you by January 31

1099 Form (for contractors)

  • Shows how much you were paid
  • Shows NO taxes taken out (because none were)
  • You get one from each client who paid you $600+
  • Due to you by January 31

💡 You can have both! If you work a regular job AND do gig work on the side, you'll get a W-2 from your employer AND a 1099 from the gig company.

💡 Neither is "better" — it depends on what you need. If you want stability and benefits, look for employee jobs. If you want flexibility and independence, contractor work might be for you.

Benefits

Extra things employers give you besides your paycheck

What Are Benefits?

Benefits are extras your employer gives you on top of your pay. They're a big part of your total compensation.

Common workplace benefits include:

  • Health insurance
  • Paid time off (vacation, sick days)
  • Retirement plans (401k)
  • Dental and vision insurance
  • Life insurance
  • Employee discounts

💡 Benefits can be worth thousands of dollars! A job paying $15/hour WITH benefits might actually be worth more than a job paying $17/hour with NO benefits.

Health Insurance

Health insurance helps pay for doctor visits, hospital stays, medicine, and other medical care.

🏥 How Employer Health Insurance Works

  • Your employer pays part of the cost (often 50-80%)
  • You pay the rest through a deduction from your paycheck
  • You can often add family members (costs more)
  • Usually only offered to full-time employees

Important health insurance terms:

  • Premium: The monthly cost of your insurance
  • Deductible: How much you pay before insurance kicks in
  • Copay: What you pay for each doctor visit or prescription

🚨 Without health insurance, medical bills can be HUGE. A single ER visit can cost thousands. That's why employer health insurance is so valuable.

Paid Time Off (PTO)

Paid time off means you get paid even when you're not working. This is a big deal!

🏖️ Types of Paid Time Off

  • Vacation days: Days off for rest, travel, or whatever you want
  • Sick days: Days off when you're too sick to work
  • Personal days: Days off for appointments or personal needs
  • Holidays: Paid days off on holidays (Thanksgiving, Christmas, etc.)

🔹 Example:

You earn $15/hour and get 10 vacation days per year.

That's 10 days × 8 hours × $15 = $1,200 in paid time off!

⚠️ Part-time and gig workers usually don't get PTO. If you don't work, you don't get paid.

Retirement Plans (401k)

A 401(k) is a retirement savings plan offered by employers. It helps you save money for when you're older.

💰 How a 401(k) Works

  • Money comes out of your paycheck BEFORE taxes
  • It goes into an investment account
  • The money grows over time
  • You can't take it out until retirement (usually age 59½) without penalties

⭐ Employer Match — FREE MONEY!

Many employers will match some of what you put in. For example:

  • You put in 3% of your paycheck
  • Your employer adds another 3%
  • That's FREE money for your retirement!

💡 If your employer offers a match, try to contribute at least enough to get the full match. Otherwise you're leaving free money on the table!

Other Common Benefits

🦷 Dental & Vision Insurance

Helps pay for dentist visits, glasses, contacts, eye exams. Usually a small monthly cost.

💀 Life Insurance

Pays money to your family if you die. Many employers offer basic coverage for free.

🛒 Employee Discounts

Discounts on the company's products or services. Example: 20% off at the store where you work.

📚 Tuition Assistance

Some employers help pay for college or training programs. This can be worth thousands!

🍔 Free/Discounted Meals

Common at restaurants. You might get a free meal each shift.

Questions to Ask About Benefits

When you're offered a job, ask about benefits! Here's what to ask:

Questions to ask:

  • "Do you offer health insurance? When am I eligible?"
  • "How much PTO do employees get?"
  • "Do you have a 401(k)? Is there an employer match?"
  • "What other benefits do you offer?"
  • "When do benefits start?" (Some start day 1, others after 30-90 days)

Watch out for:

  • Waiting periods — You might not get benefits until you've worked there 30, 60, or 90 days
  • Hours requirements — Often only full-time workers get benefits
  • Costs — Some benefits aren't free. Ask what you'll pay.

🤔 Think About It #1:

Answer: Job B! The health insurance alone is worth about $2.50/hour extra. $14 + $2.50 = $16.50/hour total value.

Answer: Free money! If you contributed 4%, your employer would add another 4% — that's like getting a 4% raise.

Job Advancement

How to move up, get raises, and build your career

What Is Job Advancement?

Job advancement means moving up in your career — getting better jobs, more responsibility, and higher pay.

Advancement can look like:

  • Promotion: Moving to a higher position (cashier → shift lead → manager)
  • Raise: Getting paid more for the same job
  • New responsibilities: Learning to do more things
  • New job: Moving to a better job at a different company

💡 Your first job doesn't have to be your forever job. Most people start at entry-level positions and work their way up over time.

How to Get Promoted

Promotions don't just happen — you have to earn them. Here's how:

1
Do your current job WELL. Be reliable, show up on time, complete your tasks, have a good attitude.
2
Go above and beyond. Show initiative. Look for ways to help without being asked.
3
Learn new skills. Ask to be trained on new tasks. Show you want to grow.
4
Be professional. Get along with coworkers, stay out of drama, accept feedback well.
5
Express your interest. Tell your boss you're interested in moving up. Ask what you need to do.

⚠️ Promotions take time. Don't expect to become a manager after 2 weeks. Build a track record of doing great work.

How to Get a Raise

A raise is when your employer increases your hourly rate or salary.

When raises might happen:

  • After a set time (6 months, 1 year)
  • During annual reviews
  • When minimum wage goes up
  • When you get promoted
  • When you ask (and make a good case)

How to ask for a raise:

  • Pick a good time (after a success, during your review)
  • Be prepared — list your accomplishments and contributions
  • Be professional and confident, not demanding
  • Know what you're asking for (a specific amount)

🔹 Example: "I've been here a year and I've taken on extra responsibilities like training new employees. I'd like to discuss a raise. I was hoping for $1 more per hour."

Building Your Skills

The more skills you have, the more valuable you are — and the more opportunities you'll have.

Ways to build skills at work:

  • Cross-train: Learn to do other jobs in your workplace
  • Ask questions: Learn from experienced coworkers
  • Take on new tasks: Volunteer for projects or responsibilities
  • Get certifications: Some industries have certifications you can earn
  • Take company training: If your employer offers training, do it!

⭐ Skills that help in almost any job:

  • Customer service
  • Communication
  • Basic computer skills
  • Teamwork
  • Problem-solving

Career Paths: Moving Up

Many jobs have a clear path from entry-level to higher positions.

🔹 Example: Fast Food Career Path

Crew Member → Shift Lead → Assistant Manager → General Manager

🔹 Example: Retail Career Path

Sales Associate → Department Lead → Assistant Manager → Store Manager

🔹 Example: Warehouse Career Path

Picker/Packer → Team Lead → Supervisor → Operations Manager

When to Look for a New Job

Sometimes the best way to advance is to find a better job somewhere else.

Signs it might be time to look elsewhere:

  • There's no room to move up at your current job
  • You've asked for a raise and been denied repeatedly
  • You've learned all you can in your current position
  • You found a job that's a better fit for your goals
  • The work environment is unhealthy or toxic

Important tips:

  • Don't quit until you have another job lined up
  • Give proper notice (usually 2 weeks)
  • Leave on good terms — you might need a reference
  • Don't badmouth your old employer in interviews

Career Exploration

Finding a career that fits YOU

Job vs. Career

What's the difference between a job and a career?

💼 A Job

  • Work you do to earn money
  • Might be temporary
  • Doesn't have to connect to your long-term goals
  • Example: Working at a grocery store in high school

📈 A Career

  • Long-term path in a field
  • Builds over time
  • Usually involves growth and advancement
  • Example: Becoming an electrician and advancing to master electrician

💡 It's okay to have jobs that aren't your "career"! Many people work different jobs while figuring out what they want to do long-term.

Know Your Interests

A good career fits what you're interested in. Think about what you enjoy!

Ask yourself:

  • What subjects do I like? (Art? Math? Science? Working with people?)
  • What do I do for fun?
  • What kind of work sounds interesting to me?
  • Do I like working with my hands? With computers? With people?
  • Do I prefer being inside or outside?

🔹 Examples of matching interests to careers:

  • Like cars? → Auto mechanic, auto body technician
  • Like helping people? → Healthcare, social work, teaching
  • Like building things? → Construction, carpentry, welding
  • Like computers? → IT support, web design, data entry
  • Like food? → Culinary arts, restaurant management

Know Your Strengths

Your strengths are things you're good at. A good career uses your strengths.

Think about your strengths:

  • Am I good at talking to people?
  • Am I good at solving problems?
  • Am I detail-oriented and careful?
  • Am I good with my hands?
  • Am I organized?
  • Am I patient?
  • Am I creative?

Matching strengths to careers:

If You're Good At...Consider...
Talking to peopleSales, customer service, healthcare
Working with your handsTrades (plumbing, electrical, HVAC)
Being organizedOffice work, logistics, accounting
Being patient and caringChildcare, teaching, nursing

Career Clusters

Careers are often grouped into clusters — areas with similar jobs.

🏥 Health Science
Nurse, CNA, medical assistant, pharmacy tech

🔧 Manufacturing & Skilled Trades
Welder, machinist, HVAC tech, electrician

🍳 Hospitality & Food Service
Chef, restaurant manager, hotel staff

🚗 Transportation
Truck driver, mechanic, logistics

💼 Business & Office
Administrative assistant, accounting, HR

🏗️ Construction
Carpenter, plumber, roofer, project manager

💻 Information Technology
IT support, web developer, network admin

👶 Education & Childcare
Teacher, teacher's aide, daycare worker

Vocational Programs

Vocational programs (also called trade schools or career/technical programs) teach specific job skills.

Benefits of vocational training:

  • Learn real, hands-on job skills
  • Often shorter than a 4-year college degree
  • Can lead to well-paying careers
  • Many programs offer certifications
  • Some are available while still in high school!

🔹 Examples of vocational programs:

  • Certified Nursing Assistant (CNA)
  • Welding certification
  • Automotive technology
  • Cosmetology (hair/beauty)
  • HVAC (heating & cooling)
  • Culinary arts
  • Computer networking

How to Explore Careers

Not sure what career is right for you? Here's how to explore:

1
Take a career assessment. Online quizzes can suggest careers based on your interests. Try O*NET Interest Profiler (free).
2
Talk to people. Ask people about their jobs. What do they do? What do they like about it?
3
Job shadow. Spend time watching someone do their job.
4
Try different jobs. Part-time and summer jobs help you figure out what you like.
5
Research online. Look up career information, salaries, and requirements on sites like BLS.gov.

💡 It's okay not to know yet! Most people try several jobs before finding their career path. The important thing is to keep exploring and learning.

Setting Work Goals

How to plan for your future with SMART goals

Why Set Goals?

A goal is something you want to achieve. Goals help you know what you're working toward.

Benefits of setting goals:

  • Give you direction — you know where you're going
  • Keep you motivated — something to work toward
  • Help you make decisions — does this help me reach my goal?
  • Let you measure progress — you can see how far you've come
  • Help you succeed — people with goals accomplish more

💡 Without goals, you're just going through the motions. With goals, you're building something.

Short-Term vs. Long-Term Goals

📅 Short-Term Goals

Goals you want to achieve soon (days, weeks, or months)

  • Get a job this month
  • Learn to use the cash register
  • Be on time every day this week
  • Save $500 by summer

📆 Long-Term Goals

Goals that take longer (1+ years)

  • Become a shift manager
  • Get my CNA certification
  • Save $5,000 for a car
  • Have a career in healthcare

💡 Short-term goals help you reach long-term goals. Break big goals into smaller steps you can achieve along the way.

SMART Goals

SMART is a way to write goals that actually work. Each letter stands for something:

S Specific

Be clear about exactly what you want. Not "get better at work" but "learn to close the register."

M Measurable

How will you know when you've achieved it? Include numbers or clear outcomes.

A Achievable

Is it realistic? Challenge yourself, but make sure it's actually possible.

R Relevant

Does this goal matter to you? Does it fit with your other goals?

T Time-bound

Set a deadline. When will you achieve this by?

SMART Goal Examples

❌ NOT a SMART goal: "I want to make more money."

Too vague! No specifics, no deadline, can't measure it.

✅ SMART version: "I will ask for a $1/hour raise at my next review in March. To prepare, I will document 3 accomplishments this month."

  • Specific: Ask for $1 raise
  • Measurable: $1 raise, 3 accomplishments
  • Achievable: Yes, with preparation
  • Relevant: Yes, earning more money
  • Time-bound: By March review

🔹 More SMART goal examples:

  • "I will get a part-time job by the end of this month."
  • "I will save $100 per paycheck for the next 6 months to buy a car."
  • "I will complete my CNA certification by December."

Breaking Goals Into Steps

Big goals can feel overwhelming. Break them into smaller steps!

🔹 Big Goal: Get a part-time job by the end of the month

Steps:

1
Update my resume (this week)
2
Make a list of 10 places I want to apply (this week)
3
Apply to 3 places per week
4
Practice interview questions
5
Follow up on applications after 1 week

💡 Completing small steps builds momentum. Each step you finish gets you closer to your goal!

Tracking Your Goals

Write down your goals and check on them regularly.

Ways to track your goals:

  • Write them in a notebook or planner
  • Put them on your phone (notes app, reminders)
  • Tell someone who will check in with you
  • Post them somewhere you'll see them
  • Review them weekly — are you on track?

If you're not making progress:

  • Is the goal still important to you?
  • Is it too big? Break it into smaller steps
  • Is the deadline realistic?
  • Do you need help or resources?
  • It's okay to adjust goals — but don't give up!

Continuing Education & Training

Ways to keep learning and improve your career

Learning Doesn't Stop at School

Your education doesn't have to end when you leave high school. There are many ways to keep learning and building skills for your career.

Why continue learning?

  • Get better jobs with higher pay
  • Learn skills that make you more valuable
  • Keep up with changes in your field
  • Open doors to new opportunities
  • Feel more confident in your abilities

💡 Many great careers don't require a 4-year college degree. There are many other paths to success!

On-the-Job Training

On-the-job training (OJT) is learning while you work. Your employer teaches you what you need to know.

✅ Benefits of On-the-Job Training

  • You get paid while you learn
  • Learn exactly what you need for THAT job
  • Hands-on experience
  • No extra school required

🔹 Examples:

  • Learning the cash register at a new job
  • Training on how to use equipment
  • Shadowing an experienced worker
  • Company training programs

⭐ Ask about training opportunities! Many employers will train you on new skills if you show interest.

Certifications

A certification is official proof that you have certain skills. It's like a credential you can put on your resume.

How certifications work:

  • Take a training course or study
  • Pass a test
  • Get a certificate or credential
  • Some need to be renewed periodically

🔹 Examples of certifications:

  • CNA — Certified Nursing Assistant
  • ServSafe — Food safety certification
  • Forklift certification — Operate a forklift
  • CPR/First Aid — Emergency response
  • OSHA 10 — Workplace safety
  • CompTIA A+ — Computer technician

Trade Schools

Trade schools (also called vocational schools or technical colleges) teach specific job skills.

✅ Benefits of Trade Schools

  • Shorter than 4-year college (often 6 months to 2 years)
  • Less expensive than traditional college
  • Learn hands-on, practical skills
  • High demand for many trade jobs
  • Can lead to good-paying careers

🔹 Trade school programs:

  • Electrician
  • Plumber
  • HVAC technician
  • Welder
  • Cosmetologist
  • Automotive technician
  • Medical assistant
  • Dental hygienist

Apprenticeships

An apprenticeship is a program where you learn a trade by working alongside an experienced professional.

How apprenticeships work:

  • You work and get paid (starting at a lower wage)
  • You learn from a skilled "journeyman" or master
  • Usually lasts 2-5 years
  • You might also take classes
  • At the end, you become a "journeyman" yourself

🔹 Apprenticeship fields:

  • Electrician
  • Plumber
  • Carpenter
  • HVAC technician
  • Ironworker

⭐ Apprenticeships are "earn while you learn." You get paid AND get trained — no student debt!

Other Learning Options

🏫 Community College

2-year degrees (Associate's) or certificates. Affordable, flexible schedules, many career programs.

💻 Online Courses

Learn from home on your own schedule. Many free or low-cost options. Great for specific skills.

🏢 Employer Tuition Assistance

Some employers pay for school! Ask if your company offers tuition reimbursement.

🎖️ Military Training

The military teaches many valuable skills. Plus education benefits (GI Bill) after service.

💡 There's no one "right" path. The best path is the one that fits your goals, your situation, and your learning style.
