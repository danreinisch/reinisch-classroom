Attendance & Punctuality

Showing up — on time, every time

What Do These Words Mean?

📅 Attendance = Showing up to work when you're scheduled.

⏰ Punctuality = Being on time. Not early, not late — ON TIME (or a few minutes early).

🤝 Reliable = Your boss can count on you. When you say you'll be there, you're there.

These are three of the most important things any employer looks for. You could be the best worker in the world, but if you don't show up, it doesn't matter.

💡 Think of it this way: If your friend said "I'll pick you up at 5:00" and they never showed up, would you trust them next time? That's how your boss feels when you don't come to work.

Why Attendance Matters So Much

When you don't come to work, it affects a lot of people — not just you.

When you miss work:

  • Someone else has to do YOUR job on top of theirs
  • Your boss has to scramble to find a replacement
  • Customers might not get helped
  • Your coworkers get frustrated
  • You lose money (no work = no pay)

🚨 Missing work too much is the #1 reason people get fired. Even if you're good at your job, too many absences will cost you.

⭐ Good attendance shows your boss three things:

  • You care about your job
  • You can be trusted
  • You respect other people's time

Being On Time (Punctuality)

"On time" at work does NOT mean walking in the door at your start time. It means being ready to work at your start time.

What "on time" really looks like:

  • Your shift starts at 9:00 AM
  • You arrive at 8:50 AM
  • You put your stuff away, clock in, and are ready to go at 9:00

What "late" looks like:

  • Your shift starts at 9:00 AM
  • You walk in at 9:00 AM
  • You still need to put your stuff away, get your apron, clock in...
  • You don't actually start working until 9:10 or 9:15

💡 A good rule: Plan to arrive 10–15 minutes before your shift. That gives you time to get settled and be truly ready at your start time.

What Happens When You're Late Too Much

Being late once because of traffic or an emergency? That happens to everyone. But being late over and over is a big problem.

What usually happens:

1
Verbal warning — Your boss talks to you about it. "Hey, you've been late three times this week. What's going on?"
2
Written warning — It goes on your record. This is serious.
3
Final warning or suspension — One more time and you could lose your job.
4
Termination (fired) — You lose your job.

💡 Some things that help you be on time:

  • Set an alarm (or two!) on your phone
  • Pick out your clothes the night before
  • Know how long it takes to get to work — and add extra time
  • Have a backup ride plan (bus, another person, ride-share)

How to Call In Sick the Right Way

Everyone gets sick. That's life. But HOW you handle it matters a lot.

The Right Way to Call In:

1
Call or text as early as possible — don't wait until your shift already started.
2
Say who you are. Don't assume they know your number.
3
Be short and clear. You don't need to give a long story.
4
Apologize for the inconvenience.

🔹 Good Example:

"Hi Mr. Thompson, this is Jake. I woke up feeling really sick today and I'm not going to be able to come in. I'm sorry for the short notice."

🔹 Bad Examples:

  • "I can't come in." (no name, no reason, no apology)
  • Just not showing up and never calling
  • Texting "sick lol" with no other info
  • Calling in sick and then posting on social media that you went to the lake

Being Reliable

Reliable means people can count on you. When you say you'll be there, you're there. When you say you'll do something, you do it.

✅ A Reliable Worker:

  • Shows up every day they're scheduled
  • Arrives on time or early
  • Calls in ahead of time if they truly can't make it
  • Doesn't make up excuses
  • Finishes tasks they start
  • Doesn't leave early without permission

❌ An Unreliable Worker:

  • Calls in "sick" often (but isn't really sick)
  • Is always 5–10 minutes late
  • No-shows without calling
  • Leaves early all the time
  • Always has an excuse
  • Tries to get out of shifts constantly

⭐ Being reliable is one of the easiest ways to stand out at work. A lot of people aren't reliable. If YOU are, your boss will notice — and remember it when it's time for raises or promotions.

Accepting Feedback & Correction

How to handle it when your boss tells you to do something differently

What Is Feedback?

Feedback is when someone — usually your boss — tells you how you're doing at your job. It can be good or it can be about things you need to fix.

👍 Positive feedback = telling you what you're doing well.

"Great job on those shelves today. They look really organized."

📝 Constructive feedback = telling you what needs to change or improve.

"You need to make sure you're sweeping under the tables, not just around them."

💡 Key point: Constructive feedback is NOT the same as getting yelled at. It's NOT the same as being in trouble. It's your boss trying to help you do better.

Everyone gets feedback — even your boss gets feedback from THEIR boss. It's a normal part of every job.

Why Feedback Feels Hard Sometimes

Let's be honest — hearing that you did something wrong doesn't feel great. That's normal. But how you REACT to it is what matters.

Why feedback can feel bad:

  • It can feel like a personal attack (but it's not)
  • It can feel embarrassing, especially in front of others
  • You might think it means you're bad at your job (it doesn't)
  • You might feel defensive — "But I was trying!"

🔑 The truth is: Your boss gives you feedback because they want you to KEEP your job and do well. If they didn't care, they wouldn't bother telling you — they'd just fire you.

Think of it like a coach: A basketball coach doesn't yell "move your feet!" because they hate you. They say it because they want you to play better. Your boss is the same way.

How to Accept Feedback the Right Way

1
Stop and listen. Don't interrupt. Let your boss finish talking.
2
Stay calm. Take a breath. Don't cross your arms or roll your eyes.
3
Don't make excuses. Even if you have a reason, just listen first.
4
Say something positive. "Okay, I understand. I'll work on that." or "Thanks for letting me know."
5
Ask questions if you're confused. "Can you show me how you'd like me to do it?"
6
Actually make the change. Show your boss you listened by doing it differently next time.

⭐ The magic phrase: "Okay, I'll work on that. Thanks for telling me." — This works in almost every feedback situation.

Good Reactions vs. Bad Reactions

Your boss says: "You need to stop being on your phone during your shift."

✅ Good Reactions:

  • "You're right. I'll put it in my locker. Sorry about that."
  • "Okay, I didn't realize it was a problem. I'll stop."
  • "My bad. I'll keep it put away from now on."

❌ Bad Reactions:

  • "Everyone else is on their phone too!"
  • "I was only checking the time!"
  • Rolling your eyes and walking away
  • "Whatever." or "Fine."
  • Getting angry or arguing

⚠️ Even if you think the feedback is unfair, arguing about it in the moment makes things worse. Accept it calmly, and if you really think it's wrong, talk to your boss privately later when you've both cooled down.

Don't Take It Personally

This is one of the hardest parts of working — but one of the most important skills you can learn.

"Taking it personally" means you think the feedback is about YOU as a person, instead of about what you DID.

What Your Boss SaysWhat It Does NOT MeanWhat It DOES Mean
"You need to be faster.""You're stupid and slow.""I need you to pick up the pace a little."
"This wasn't done right.""You're terrible at your job.""Let me show you the correct way."
"Don't do it that way.""I don't like you.""There's a better way, let me explain."
"You've been late a lot.""You're a bad person.""I need you to fix your schedule."

💡 Feedback is about what you DID, not who you ARE. You can always change what you do. That's the whole point.

What If You Disagree with the Feedback?

Sometimes you might think the feedback isn't fair. Maybe you were doing it the way someone else showed you. Maybe there's a misunderstanding.

How to handle it:

1
Accept it in the moment. Don't argue or get defensive right away.
2
Wait until you've calmed down. Give it at least a few minutes (or hours).
3
Ask to talk privately. "Hey, can I talk to you about what you mentioned earlier?"
4
Explain your side calmly. "I just wanted you to know that Marcus showed me to do it that way. I want to make sure I'm doing it the way you prefer."

⚠️ Even if you explain your side, your boss has the final say. If they want it done a certain way, do it their way.

Day 2 Review: Accepting Feedback

🤔 Think About It #1:

Your boss says "You missed a spot when you were mopping." What's a good response?

Answer: "Oh, okay! I'll go back and get it. Thanks for pointing it out."

🤔 Think About It #2:

Your boss corrects you in front of a coworker and you feel embarrassed. What should you do?

Answer: Stay calm, say "Okay, I'll fix that." Don't make a scene. If it really bothers you, talk to your boss privately later.

🤔 Think About It #3:

Your boss says you've been moving too slowly. You think you're working hard. How do you handle it?

Answer: Accept it and try to work faster. Later, if needed, you could say: "I'm really trying to do a good job. Can you show me what 'faster' looks like so I know what to aim for?"

Workplace Attitude & Initiative

How your attitude affects your job — and what "initiative" means

What Is "Attitude" at Work?

Your attitude is how you act, how you talk, and how you come across to other people. At work, your attitude matters just as much as your skills.

😀 Positive Attitude:

  • Saying "yes" when asked to do something
  • Being friendly to coworkers and customers
  • Doing your work without complaining
  • Looking for ways to help
  • Staying calm when things get stressful

😤 Negative Attitude:

  • Complaining about tasks
  • Rolling your eyes or sighing
  • Saying "that's not my job"
  • Being rude or short with people
  • Doing the bare minimum and nothing else

🚨 A bad attitude can get you fired — even if you do your job correctly. Nobody wants to work with someone who complains all the time or makes everyone feel uncomfortable.

What Is "Initiative"?

Initiative means doing things without being told. It means you see something that needs to be done, and you just do it.

🔹 Examples of initiative:

  • The trash is full — you take it out without being asked
  • You finished your task — you ask your boss "What else can I do?"
  • You see a spill — you grab the mop and clean it up
  • A customer looks confused — you walk up and offer to help
  • The shelves look messy — you straighten them up

⭐ Workers who show initiative get noticed. Your boss will think: "This person doesn't just do what I tell them — they actually look for ways to help." That leads to better hours, raises, and promotions.

⚠️ The opposite of initiative: Standing around waiting to be told what to do. If you run out of work, DON'T just stand there — ask: "What else can I help with?"

Stay Off Your Phone

This is a big one. Using your personal phone during your shift is one of the fastest ways to get in trouble at work.

Why phones are a problem at work:

  • You look like you're not working (even if you "were just checking the time")
  • Customers see you on your phone and think you don't care
  • You miss things that need to be done
  • Your boss will lose trust in you

Good phone habits at work:

  • Put your phone in your locker, pocket, or bag — on silent
  • Only check it during your break
  • If you're expecting an important call (doctor, family emergency), tell your boss ahead of time
  • Never take selfies, scroll TikTok, or text while you're on the clock

🔹 If you need your phone for an emergency:

"Hey Mrs. Davis, I'm waiting for a call from my doctor today. If my phone rings, can I step away for a minute to answer it?"

Looking for Things to Do

One of the biggest differences between a GOOD worker and an AVERAGE worker is what they do when their main task is done.

Average WorkerGreat Worker
Finishes their task and stands aroundFinishes their task and asks "What else can I do?"
Waits to be told what to doLooks around and finds something to do
Does only what's assignedDoes what's assigned PLUS anything else they notice
Checks their phone when it's slowCleans, organizes, or restocks when it's slow

Things you can ALWAYS do when you have nothing to do:

  • Clean something
  • Organize or restock
  • Ask your boss or coworkers if they need help
  • Wipe down counters, sweep, take out trash

Body Language Matters Too

Your body language is what you "say" without using words. People can tell your attitude by how you stand, look, and move.

✅ Positive Body Language:

  • Making eye contact
  • Standing up straight
  • Smiling or having a neutral expression
  • Nodding when someone talks to you
  • Moving with purpose (looking busy)

❌ Negative Body Language:

  • Rolling your eyes
  • Crossing your arms
  • Sighing loudly
  • Slouching or leaning against the wall
  • Looking at your phone while someone talks

⚠️ You might not even realize you're doing these things. But your boss and coworkers notice. Try to be aware of how you look and stand at work.

Handling Bad Days

Everyone has bad days. You might be tired, stressed, or dealing with something at home. But you still have to show up and act professional.

Tips for getting through a bad day at work:

  • Leave your personal problems at the door. When you walk in, focus on work.
  • Don't take it out on coworkers or customers. They didn't cause your bad day.
  • Take a deep breath. If you feel frustrated, step away for a second (like going to the bathroom to cool down).
  • Remember it's temporary. Your shift will end. Get through it.
  • If something serious is going on, it's okay to tell your boss: "I'm having a rough day, but I'm doing my best."

💡 Your boss will respect honesty. Saying "I'm having a tough day but I'm pushing through" sounds a lot better than just being grumpy to everyone.

🤔 Think About It #1:

Answer: Focus on your own work. It's not your job to police coworkers. Your boss will notice on their own.

Common Reasons People Get Fired

What to avoid — and how to protect your job

What Does "Getting Fired" Mean?

Getting fired (also called termination) means your employer ends your job. You don't work there anymore. It's different from quitting, because YOU didn't choose to leave — they told you to go.

What happens when you get fired:

  • You lose your income (paycheck)
  • You have to find a new job
  • Future employers might ask why you left your last job
  • It can be embarrassing and stressful

💡 The good news: Most of the reasons people get fired are 100% preventable. If you know what to avoid, you can keep your job for a long time.

Top Reasons People Get Fired (Part 1)

1
Too Many Absences / Being Late Too Much

This is the #1 reason. If you don't show up, your boss will find someone who does.

✅ FIX: Be reliable. Show up on time. Call in early if you're truly sick.
2
Bad Attitude

Complaining, arguing, being rude, or acting like you don't want to be there.

✅ FIX: Stay positive. Say "okay" instead of arguing. Keep complaints to yourself.
3
Not Doing Your Work

Standing around, being on your phone, doing the bare minimum, or not finishing tasks.

✅ FIX: Stay busy. Show initiative. Do your work and then look for more.

Top Reasons People Get Fired (Part 2)

4
Not Following Rules or Instructions

Every job has rules. If your boss tells you to do something a certain way, do it that way.

✅ FIX: Listen to instructions. Follow rules even when nobody is watching.
5
Dishonesty (Lying or Stealing)

This includes lying about being sick, stealing money or products, or lying to a customer.

✅ FIX: Always be honest. Even small lies can get you fired on the spot.
6
Causing Drama or Fighting

Gossiping, arguing loudly, threatening people, or creating a hostile environment.

✅ FIX: Stay out of drama. If there's a problem, talk calmly or go to your boss.

Top Reasons People Get Fired (Part 3)

7
Being on Your Phone Too Much

Texting, scrolling, watching videos during your shift.

✅ FIX: Put your phone away. Only use it on break.
8
Being Rude to Customers

Ignoring customers, being short with them, or arguing back.

✅ FIX: Treat every customer with respect, even the difficult ones.
9
Refusing to Do Tasks

Saying "that's not my job" or refusing when your boss asks you to do something.

✅ FIX: Be flexible. Do what's asked. You can discuss it later if needed.

The Warning System

Most jobs don't fire you for one small mistake. They use a warning system to give you chances to fix things.

How it usually works:

1
Verbal Warning — Your boss talks to you about the problem. Nothing is written down yet, but they're watching.
2
Written Warning — You sign a paper that says what happened. This goes in your file. It's official.
3
Final Warning / Suspension — You might be sent home without pay for a few days. One more mistake and you're done.
4
Termination — You're fired.

🚨 Some things get you fired IMMEDIATELY — no warnings:

  • Stealing
  • Fighting or threatening someone
  • Coming to work drunk or high
  • Major dishonesty

How to Keep Your Job — The Checklist

If you do these things consistently, you will almost certainly keep your job and do well.

✅ The Job-Keeping Checklist:

  • Show up every day you're scheduled
  • Be on time (arrive 10–15 minutes early)
  • Keep your phone put away
  • Have a positive attitude — say "okay" not "ugh"
  • Accept feedback without arguing
  • Show initiative — look for things to do
  • Be honest — always
  • Treat everyone with respect — boss, coworkers, and customers
  • Follow the rules — even small ones
  • Stay out of drama

🔑 None of these require special skills or talent. They just require YOU choosing to show up, try hard, and be respectful.
