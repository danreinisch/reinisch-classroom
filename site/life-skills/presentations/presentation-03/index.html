Day 1: What Does Each Helper Do?

Let's Break It Down!

👨‍🏫 Job Coach - Your Work Buddy

What they do: Comes to your actual job with you! They show you how to do tasks, help you learn the routine, and support you at work.

Example: You get hired at a grocery store. Your job coach comes with you for the first few weeks. They show you how to stock shelves properly, help you learn where items go, and teach you how to use the pricing gun.

Where to find them: Vocational Rehabilitation, Disability Services agencies, your school's transition program

💼 Employment Counselor - Your Job Search Guide

What they do: Helps you BEFORE you have a job. They help you write a resume, practice interviews, and find job openings that match your skills.

Example: You meet with a counselor at the Workforce Center. They help you make a resume, search job websites, and practice answering "Tell me about yourself."

Where to find them: Workforce Development Centers, Career Centers, Vocational Rehabilitation offices

These two are DIFFERENT! One helps you GET a job (counselor), the other helps you KEEP a job (coach)!

Day 1: Even More Helpers!

🎯 Skills Trainer - Your Practice Teacher

What they do: Teaches you work skills in a safe practice environment before you try them at a real job.

Example: Before you work in a restaurant, a skills trainer teaches you food safety, how to wash dishes properly, and how to handle hot pans - all in a training kitchen.

Where to find them: Community colleges, training programs, community centers, disability services organizations

🚗 Transportation Helper - Your Travel Planner

What they do: Helps you figure out HOW to get to work. They teach you bus routes, help you apply for paratransit (special transport for people with disabilities), or set up carpools.

Example: Your job is 5 miles away and you don't drive. A transportation helper shows you which bus to take, practices the route with you, and helps you get a bus pass. OR they help you apply for paratransit if regular buses don't work for you.

Where to find them: Your local transit authority, disability services, independent living centers, call 211

Fun Fact: Paratransit is a special door-to-door bus service for people who can't use regular buses!

Day 1: Who Should You Ask?

Match the Problem to the Right Helper

Situation 1: You got hired at Walmart but don't know which bus to take

Who helps? Transportation Helper (They'll teach you the route!)

Situation 2: You want to work but you've never had an interview before

Who helps? Employment Counselor (They'll practice interviews with you!)

Who helps? Job Coach (They can come to work with you and help you learn!)

Situation 4: You want to work in an office but you don't know how to use a copy machine

Who helps? Skills Trainer (They'll teach you office skills!)

Day 2: Let's Explore Jobs!

What Jobs Interest You?

🛒 Retail Jobs (Stores like Walmart, Target, Dollar General)

  • Cashier - Scan items, take money, give change. Usually standing. Pay: $10-13/hour
  • Stocker - Put products on shelves, organize backroom, use box cutters. Physical work. Pay: $11-14/hour
  • Greeter - Welcome customers, hand out carts, help find items. Friendly job. Pay: $10-12/hour
  • Cart Attendant - Collect shopping carts from parking lot, bring them inside. Outside work. Pay: $10-12/hour

🍔 Food Service Jobs (McDonald's, Subway, local restaurants)

  • Food Prep - Chop vegetables, measure ingredients, make sandwiches. Standing job. Pay: $10-13/hour
  • Server/Waitstaff - Take orders, bring food, clean tables. Fast-paced, interact with people. Pay: $2-5/hour + tips
  • Dishwasher - Wash dishes, clean kitchen, take out trash. Hot and wet. Pay: $10-12/hour
  • Host/Hostess - Greet customers, seat them at tables, answer phones. People skills needed. Pay: $9-11/hour
Activity: Circle or point to 2 jobs that sound interesting to you. Why do they interest you?

Day 2: Even More Job Options!

More Ways to Work

🧹 Cleaning & Maintenance Jobs

  • Janitor - Clean buildings, empty trash, vacuum, mop floors. Often evening or night shifts. Pay: $11-14/hour
  • Housekeeper (Hotels) - Clean hotel rooms, change sheets, restock supplies. Physical work. Pay: $11-13/hour
  • Grounds Keeper - Mow lawns, rake leaves, pull weeds, shovel snow. Outside work, seasonal. Pay: $12-15/hour

📦 Warehouse & Delivery Jobs

  • Warehouse Worker - Pick items from shelves, pack boxes, load trucks. Standing and walking. Pay: $12-16/hour
  • Sorter - Sort packages by zip code or type. Fast-paced, repetitive. Pay: $12-14/hour
  • Delivery Helper - Ride with driver, carry packages to doors. Physical, outside work. Pay: $11-13/hour

🏨 More Options

  • Laundry Worker - Wash, dry, fold linens for hotels or hospitals. Hot, fast-paced. Pay: $10-12/hour
  • Office Helper - Make copies, sort mail, organize files, shred papers. Inside work. Pay: $11-13/hour

Match Your Skills to Jobs

  • ✨ Good at organizing things? → Try: Stocker, warehouse worker, office helper
  • 😊 Like talking to people? → Try: Cashier, greeter, server, host
  • 🏋️ Strong and like physical work? → Try: Warehouse, cleaning, grounds keeping, delivery helper
  • 🎯 Good at following steps? → Try: Food prep, packer, laundry worker, maintenance
  • 🌙 Night owl? → Try: Janitor (many work evenings), warehouse (some night shifts)

Day 4: Let's Practice Interviews!

Common Interview Questions

Question 1: "Tell me about yourself"

Good Answer Example: "My name is ___. I just graduated from ___. I'm looking for a job where I can help people and learn new skills."

Question 2: "Why do you want to work here?"

Good Answer Example: "I shop here a lot and I like how friendly everyone is. I want to be part of a good team."

Question 3: "What are your strengths?"

Good Answer Example: "I'm a hard worker. I show up on time and I don't give up when things are hard."

Practice Activity: Answer these questions out loud with a partner!

Day 4: What Would You Do?

Solving Workplace Problems

Problem 1: You're going to be late for work

❌ Bad Choice: Don't call, just show up late

✅ Good Choice: Call your manager right away and tell them you'll be late

Problem 2: A customer is rude to you

❌ Bad Choice: Be rude back or argue

✅ Good Choice: Stay calm, be polite, ask your manager for help

Problem 3: You don't understand your task

❌ Bad Choice: Guess and do it wrong

✅ Good Choice: Ask questions until you understand

Discussion: Can you think of other workplace problems? What would you do?

Day 6: Your Independence Plan

What Does Independence Mean to YOU?

Independence means different things:

  • 🏠 Living on your own or with roommates
  • 💼 Having a job and earning money
  • 🚗 Getting around by yourself
  • 🛒 Shopping and cooking for yourself
  • 💰 Managing your own money
  • 👥 Making your own choices

SMART Goals

  • Specific - Be exact about what you want
  • Measurable - You can tell when you did it
  • Achievable - It's possible for you to do
  • Relevant - It matters to you
  • Time-bound - You have a deadline
Example: "I will get a part-time job by the end of this school year"
