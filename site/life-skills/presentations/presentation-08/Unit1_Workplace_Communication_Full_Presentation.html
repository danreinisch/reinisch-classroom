Talking to Your Boss

How to communicate with your supervisor at work

Why Does This Matter?

Your boss (also called your supervisor or manager) is the person in charge of you at work. You need to talk to them about many things.

💡 Your boss is NOT your enemy. They want you to do well at your job. Good communication helps both of you.

You might need to talk to your boss when you:

  • Have a question about your work
  • Need a day off
  • Have a problem you can't fix alone
  • Don't understand what they want you to do
  • Are going to be late or absent
  • Need help with a coworker issue

✅ People who communicate well with their boss are more likely to keep their job and get raises.

How to Ask Your Boss a Question

It is always okay to ask questions at work. It is better to ask than to guess and make a mistake.

Step-by-Step: Asking a Question

Step 1: Wait for a good time. Don't interrupt if your boss is on the phone or talking to someone else.

Step 2: Get their attention politely. Say: "Excuse me, can I ask you a quick question?"

Step 3: Ask your question clearly. Be specific about what you need help with.

Step 4: Listen carefully to the answer. If you still don't understand, say: "Can you show me what you mean?"

Step 5: Say thank you.

🔹 Example:

"Excuse me, Mrs. Johnson. I'm stocking the shelves but I'm not sure where the cereal goes. Can you show me?"

⚠️ Never say: "Nobody told me" or "That's not my job." These phrases make you look bad, even if they're true.

How to Request Time Off

Sometimes you need a day off — maybe for a doctor's appointment, a family event, or a personal reason. Here's how to ask the right way.

Step-by-Step: Requesting Time Off

Step 1: Ask ahead of time. Try to ask at least 1–2 weeks before you need the day off.

Step 2: Talk to your boss in person (or follow the method they prefer — some want it in writing).

Step 3: Be polite and direct. Tell them the date you need off.

Step 4: You do NOT have to give personal details. You can just say "I have an appointment" or "I have a family thing."

Step 5: Accept the answer. If they say no, don't argue. Ask if there's another day that works.

🔹 Good Example:

"Hey Mr. Thompson, I need to request off on Friday, March 14th. I have a doctor's appointment. Is that okay?"

🔹 Bad Example:

"I'm not coming in Friday." (This is telling, not asking. It can get you in trouble.)

How to Report a Problem

Sometimes things go wrong at work. A machine breaks. A customer is angry. You see something unsafe. It's your job to let your boss know.

Step-by-Step: Reporting a Problem

Step 1: Stay calm. Don't panic or get angry.

Step 2: Go to your boss (or the manager on duty).

Step 3: Explain what happened clearly. Stick to the facts.

Step 4: Don't blame people or make stuff up. Just say what happened.

Step 5: Ask what they want you to do next.

🔹 Example:

"Mrs. Davis, I wanted to let you know that the mop bucket tipped over in aisle 3 and the floor is wet. I put a caution sign up. Should I clean it up or is there something else you want me to do?"

⭐ Reporting problems shows your boss that you are responsible and paying attention. That's a good thing!

Accepting Feedback from Your Boss

Feedback is when your boss tells you how you're doing — what you're doing well and what you need to work on. Everyone gets feedback. It doesn't mean you're in trouble.

Two types of feedback:

👍 Positive feedback: "Great job stocking those shelves! They look really organized."

📝 Constructive feedback: "Next time, make sure the labels face forward when you stock the shelves."

Constructive feedback is NOT the same as being yelled at. It's your boss helping you do better.

Step 1: Listen without interrupting.

Step 2: Don't get defensive or make excuses.

Step 3: Say: "Okay, I'll do that next time. Thanks for letting me know."

Step 4: Actually make the change. Show your boss you listened.

✅ DO Say:

  • "Okay, I understand."
  • "Thanks for telling me."
  • "Can you show me the right way?"

❌ DON'T Say:

  • "That's not fair."
  • "Nobody told me."
  • "Whatever." (or eye rolling)

Getting Along with Coworkers

How to work well with the people around you

Who Are Your Coworkers?

Coworkers are the people who work at the same place as you. They might do the same job as you, or a completely different job. Either way, you have to get along with them.

💡 You don't have to be best friends with your coworkers. But you DO have to be polite, respectful, and able to work as a team.

Why does getting along with coworkers matter?

  • Work goes smoother when people cooperate
  • You'll be happier at your job
  • Your boss notices when people work well together
  • Drama and fighting can get you fired

Small Talk: What Is It?

Small talk is casual, friendly conversation. It's how people get comfortable with each other. You don't have to share deep personal stuff — just be friendly.

Good small talk topics at work:

  • "How's your day going?"
  • "Did you do anything fun this weekend?"
  • "It's really busy today, huh?"
  • Sports, weather, TV shows, food

Topics to AVOID at work:

  • How much money you or they make
  • Religion or politics (can start arguments)
  • Gossip about other coworkers
  • Very personal problems

🔹 Example: You walk into the break room and a coworker is there.

You: "Hey! How's it going today?"

Them: "Pretty good. It's been busy though."

You: "Yeah, it really has. Hopefully it slows down after lunch."

That's it! Simple and friendly.

Teamwork at Work

Most jobs require you to work with other people as a team. That means helping each other out and doing your fair share.

What good teamwork looks like:

  • Do your part. Finish your own tasks before standing around.
  • Help others. If you're done and someone else is behind, offer to help.
  • Communicate. Let your team know if you need help or if something changes.
  • Don't keep score. Don't say "I did more than you." Just do the work.

🔹 Example: You work at a restaurant. You finished wiping tables, but your coworker is still carrying dishes. You walk over and say: "Hey, need help with those dishes?"

That's teamwork. Your boss will notice.

⚠️ Not teamwork: Standing around on your phone while your coworker does all the work.

Handling Disagreements

Sometimes you and a coworker won't agree on something. That's normal. What matters is how you handle it.

Steps to Handle a Disagreement:

Step 1: Stay calm. Take a deep breath. Don't raise your voice.

Step 2: Talk to the person privately (not in front of customers or other coworkers).

Step 3: Use "I" statements. Say "I feel frustrated when..." instead of "You always..."

Step 4: Listen to their side. You don't have to agree, but let them talk.

Step 5: If you can't solve it, ask your boss for help.

🔹 Good Example: "Hey, I noticed I've been taking the trash out every night. Can we take turns?"

🔹 Bad Example: "You NEVER do the trash! I'm sick of it!" (This starts a fight.)

Friend vs. Coworker

Some coworkers might become your friends — and that's great! But there's a difference between a friend and a coworker at work.

👤 Coworker🤝 Friend
You're polite and respectfulYou hang out outside of work
You make small talkYou share personal stuff
You work as a teamYou trust them deeply
You keep conversations professionalYou joke around and relax together

⚠️ Be careful about:

  • Sharing too much personal information with coworkers you don't know well
  • Taking sides in workplace drama
  • Treating a coworker-friend differently than other coworkers (favoritism)
  • Letting a friendship get in the way of doing your job

Day 2 Review: Getting Along with Coworkers

🤔 Think About It #1:

Answer: How their day is going, the weather, weekend plans, sports, or food. Keep it light and friendly.

🤔 Think About It #2:

Answer: Talk to them calmly and privately first. If it doesn't change, let your boss know.

🤔 Think About It #3:

Answer: Don't join in. Say "I'd rather not get involved" and change the subject.

Customer Service Basics

How to help customers and handle tough situations

What Is Customer Service?

Customer service means helping the people who come into your workplace. Customers are the people who buy things or use the services at the place where you work.

💡 Without customers, there is no business. Without a business, there are no jobs. That's why treating customers well is so important.

Jobs that involve customer service:

  • Cashier at a store or restaurant
  • Stocking shelves at a grocery store
  • Working the front desk at a hotel or gym
  • Bagging groceries
  • Serving food
  • Answering phones

Even if your job isn't "customer service," you'll probably deal with customers at some point.

Greeting Customers

When a customer comes in or walks up to you, greet them. A greeting is just a friendly hello. It makes people feel welcome.

How to Greet a Customer:

1
Make eye contact — look at them, not at the floor or your phone.
2
Smile — even a small smile makes a big difference.
3
Say something friendly — "Hi! Welcome in!" or "Hello, how can I help you?"

Example at a fast food restaurant:

👤 You:
"Hi! Welcome to Burger Palace. What can I get for you?"
🧑 Customer:
"Can I get a cheeseburger and a small fries?"
👤 You:
"Sure thing! Anything to drink with that?"

⚠️ Never ignore a customer. Even if you're busy, say "I'll be with you in just a moment!"

Helping Customers Find What They Need

Sometimes customers need help finding something, understanding a product, or figuring out how something works.

1
Listen carefully to what they need. Don't interrupt them.
2
If you know the answer, help them right away. Walk them to the product if you can — don't just point and say "over there."
3
If you DON'T know, say: "I'm not sure, but let me find someone who can help you."
4
Follow up. Ask: "Is there anything else I can help you with?"

🔹 Example at a grocery store:

Customer: "Where is the pasta sauce?"

You: "It's in aisle 5. Follow me, I'll show you!" (Then walk them there.)

⭐ Walking a customer to what they need instead of just pointing is one of the easiest ways to give great service.

When a Customer Is Upset

Sometimes customers get frustrated or angry. Maybe their order was wrong, they waited too long, or they're just having a bad day. It's usually not about you personally.

How to Handle an Upset Customer:

1
Stay calm. Don't raise your voice, even if they raise theirs.
2
Listen. Let them explain what's wrong. Don't interrupt.
3
Apologize. Say: "I'm sorry about that." Even if it wasn't your fault.
4
Fix it if you can. "Let me get you the right order."
5
Get your manager if you need to. "Let me get my manager to help you."

🚫 NEVER: Argue with a customer, call them names, walk away, or say "that's not my problem."

When to Get Your Manager

You don't have to handle everything yourself. There are times when the best thing to do is get your manager involved.

Get your manager when:

  • A customer is yelling or threatening you
  • A customer asks for a refund or discount and you're not sure
  • You made a mistake and don't know how to fix it
  • A customer asks a question you can't answer
  • Something feels unsafe or wrong

🔹 How to tell the customer:

"I want to make sure we get this right for you. Let me get my manager — they'll be able to help."

✅ Getting help is not a sign of weakness. It shows you care about solving the problem.

⚠️ If a customer ever makes you feel unsafe — they're screaming, threatening, or acting dangerous — step back and get help right away. Your safety comes first.

Day 3 Review: Customer Service Basics

🤔 Think About It #1:

A customer walks up to you at a store. What's the first thing you should do?

Answer: Make eye contact, smile, and greet them. "Hi! How can I help you?"

🤔 Think About It #2:

A customer says their food order is wrong and they're upset. What do you do?

Answer: Stay calm. Apologize. Try to fix it. If you can't, get your manager.

🤔 Think About It #3:

A customer asks where something is but you don't know. What do you say?

Answer: "I'm not sure, but let me find someone who can help you."

Professional Phone & Email

How to communicate professionally using phones and email

Why Professional Communication Matters

At work, you'll need to use your phone, text, or email to communicate with your boss, coworkers, or customers. How you communicate says a lot about you.

💡 The way you talk and write at work should be different from how you talk and text with your friends. It needs to be more polite and professional.

At work, you might need to:

  • Answer the phone at work
  • Take a message for someone
  • Call in sick
  • Text or email your boss
  • Leave a voicemail

Answering the Phone at Work

Some jobs require you to answer the phone. This is different from answering your personal phone.

How to Answer the Work Phone:

1
Pick up within 2–3 rings. Don't let it ring and ring.
2
Say the business name and your name. "Thank you for calling Burger Palace, this is Jake. How can I help you?"
3
Listen carefully. If you need to, say: "Can you hold on one moment, please?"
4
Help them or transfer them. "Let me transfer you to someone who can help."

🚫 Never: Answer with just "Hello?" or "Yeah?" or "What?" at work.

Taking a Message

Sometimes the person someone is calling for isn't available. You'll need to take a message.

What to include in a message:

  • Who called — their name (ask them to spell it if needed)
  • Their phone number — read it back to make sure it's right
  • What they're calling about — a short summary
  • When they called — the date and time

🔹 Example:

"Mrs. Johnson isn't here right now. Can I take a message?"

Caller: "Yes, this is Mark Davis. My number is 555-1234. I'm calling about my order."

You write: Mark Davis — 555-1234 — calling about his order — 10:30 AM Tuesday

You say: "Got it. I'll make sure she gets the message."

✅ Always read the phone number back to the caller to make sure you got it right!

Calling In Sick or Running Late

If you're sick or going to be late, you must contact your boss. Don't just not show up.

Calling in sick:

  • Call or text as early as possible (before your shift starts)
  • Keep it short and simple
  • You don't need to give lots of details
Text to: Boss (Mr. Thompson)
You:
Hi Mr. Thompson, this is Jake. I'm not feeling well today and won't be able to come in. I'm sorry for the short notice.

🔹 If you're going to be late:

"Hi Mrs. Davis, this is Sam. I'm running about 15 minutes late — there's an accident on the highway. I'm sorry and I'll be there as soon as I can."

⚠️ Bad ways to call in:

  • "I can't come in today." (no name, no reason, no apology)
  • Having someone else call for you (unless you physically can't)
  • Not calling at all and just not showing up

Professional vs. Casual Language

When texting or emailing your boss, your messages should look different from texts to your friends.

✅ Professional (for work):

  • "Hi Mr. Thompson, I have a question about my schedule."
  • "Thank you for letting me know."
  • "I won't be able to make it in today."
  • Use full words and sentences
  • Use punctuation

❌ Casual (NOT for work):

  • "yo wats my schedule"
  • "k thx"
  • "cant come in lol"
  • "bruh idk"
  • Too many emojis 😂🤣💀

💡 Rule of thumb: If you wouldn't say it to your boss's face, don't type it either.

Writing a Professional Email

Some jobs use email. Here's how to write one that looks professional.

To: manager@burgerpalace.com
Subject: Time Off Request — March 14

Hi Mrs. Johnson,

I'm writing to request Friday, March 14th off. I have a doctor's appointment that day.

Please let me know if that works. Thank you!

Jake Behringer

Parts of a Professional Email:

  • Subject line: Short and clear — tells them what it's about
  • Greeting: "Hi" or "Hello" + their name
  • Body: Keep it short. Say what you need.
  • Closing: "Thank you" or "Thanks"
  • Your name: Always sign your name at the bottom

Do's and Don'ts: Phone & Email at Work

✅ DO:

  • Answer work phones professionally
  • Write down messages carefully
  • Call in early if you're sick
  • Use full words and sentences
  • Include your name in messages
  • Be polite — use "please" and "thank you"
  • Read your message before sending
  • Keep work messages short and clear

❌ DON'T:

  • Answer with "Yeah?" or "What?"
  • Use slang, abbreviations, or lots of emojis
  • Forget to include your name
  • Text personal stuff during work hours
  • Send angry messages — calm down first
  • Use ALL CAPS (looks like yelling)
  • Ignore messages from your boss
  • Have someone else call in for you
