🎯Why This Matters

When you get your first job, you need to know how to read your paycheck! This lesson will help you understand where your money goes and make sure you're getting paid correctly.

DAY 1

What is a Paycheck?

Understanding the Basics

💵What is a Paycheck?

A paycheck is the money you get paid when you work for an employer. It's your reward for doing your job!

Think of it like this: You do work → Your employer owes you money → They pay you with a paycheck

📖Real Life Example

Maria works at a fast food restaurant. She worked 20 hours this week. Her boss pays her $15 per hour. At the end of the week, she gets a paycheck!

20 hours × $15/hour = $300

But... Maria notices her paycheck isn't exactly $300. Why? We'll find out!

📄What is a Pay Stub?

A pay stub (also called a pay statement) is a paper that comes with your paycheck. It's like a receipt that shows:

  • How much you earned
  • How many hours you worked
  • What money was taken out
  • How much you actually get to take home

Always keep your pay stubs! They're important records you might need later.

DAY 1

Gross Pay vs. Net Pay

The Most Important Concept!

Two Very Important Words

💰GROSS PAY

Gross pay means the total amount of money you earned BEFORE anything is taken out.

This is your hours worked × your pay per hour

Example:

You worked 40 hours

You earn $12 per hour

⬇
$480

This is your GROSS PAY

🏠NET PAY (Take-Home Pay)

Net pay means the actual amount of money you get AFTER things are taken out (like taxes).

This is the money that goes into your bank account or the cash you get!

Net pay is always LESS than gross pay.

DAY 1

Why Are They Different?

Understanding the Difference

The Journey of Your Money

Step 1: You work and earn $480 (Gross Pay)

⬇

Step 2: Government takes out taxes -$72

Step 3: Other deductions -$48

⬇

Step 4: You get $360 (Net Pay)

❓Why Is Money Taken Out?

Don't worry - your boss isn't keeping your money! Here's what happens:

  • Taxes: The government requires everyone to pay taxes. Your employer takes it out of your check and sends it to the government for you.
  • Social Security & Medicare: Money saved for when you retire or need healthcare
  • Other things you signed up for: Like health insurance or retirement savings (optional)

This is totally normal and happens to everyone who works!

DAY 2

What Gets Taken Out?

Understanding Deductions

📋What is a Deduction?

A deduction is money that is taken out of your gross pay.

Think of it like this: You have $100 → Someone takes out $25 → You have $75 left

That $25 that was taken out is a "deduction"

The Main Types of Deductions

Deduction Type What It Means (In Plain English)
Federal Tax Money for the U.S. government. They use it for things like roads, military, and government programs.
State Tax Money for your state government. They use it for schools, police, and state programs.
Social Security Money saved for when you retire (stop working when you're older). You'll get this back later in life!
Medicare Money for healthcare when you're older (over 65). Helps pay for doctor visits and hospital stays.
DAY 2

Understanding FICA

What's That Weird Word?

🔤What Does FICA Mean?

FICA stands for "Federal Insurance Contributions Act" (don't worry about remembering that long name!)

What you NEED to know: FICA is just a fancy name for two things combined:

  • Social Security tax
  • Medicare tax

When you see "FICA" on your pay stub, it means money going to Social Security and Medicare.

📖Let's See How Much FICA Takes

Let's say you earn $600 gross pay this paycheck.

Social Security: About 6.2% of your pay

$600 × 0.062 = $37.20


Medicare: About 1.45% of your pay

$600 × 0.0145 = $8.70


Total FICA: $45.90

So out of $600, about $46 goes to FICA. That's about 7.65% total.

💡Don't Panic!

FICA might seem like you're losing money, but you're actually saving for your future! When you're 65+ years old and retired, Social Security will pay you money every month. Medicare will help pay for your medical care. So you're getting this money back later!

DAY 2

Other Common Deductions

Things You Might See

Deductions You Choose (Optional)

🏥Health Insurance

If you sign up for health insurance through your job, money will be taken out each paycheck to pay for it.

Why it's good: If you get sick or hurt, insurance helps pay your medical bills so you don't have to pay thousands of dollars yourself!

Example: You might pay $50 per paycheck for health insurance. But if you break your arm, insurance might pay $10,000 of the hospital bill!

🏦Retirement Savings (401k)

Some jobs let you save money for retirement (when you stop working when you're older). You can choose to have money taken out of each paycheck and put into savings.

Why it's good: When you're 65 and want to stop working, you'll have money saved up! Some employers even add extra money to your savings for free!

⚖️Required vs. Optional

Required (Must Happen) Optional (Your Choice)
• Federal tax
• State tax

• Life insurance

  2. FICA = Social Security + Medicare (money for your future!)
  3. Some deductions are required (taxes, FICA)
  4. Some deductions are optional (health insurance, retirement)

🧮Quick Math Example

If you earn $800 gross pay...

Expect about $200 in deductions (25%)

Take home about $600 net pay (75%)

✅This is Normal!

Everyone who works has money taken out for deductions. It's not your boss stealing from you - it's the law! The government requires these deductions, and your employer is just following the rules.

DAY 3

Reading a Real Pay Stub

Let's Look at Every Part!

🔍What We'll Learn Today

Today we're going to look at a real pay stub and learn what every single section means. By the end of today, you'll be able to read your own pay stub like a pro!

The Four Main Parts of a Pay Stub

1️⃣ Your Information

Your name, address, employee ID number, and when you're getting paid

2️⃣ Earnings

How many hours you worked and how much you earned (gross pay)

3️⃣ Deductions

All the money that was taken out and why

4️⃣ Net Pay

The final amount you're taking home!

📍Let's Look at Each Part in Detail

We're going to go through a sample pay stub step by step. Follow along carefully!

DAY 3

Sample Pay Stub - Part 1

Employee Information Section

🍔 BURGER PALACE - Pay Statement

Employee Name: Alex Johnson

Employee ID: BG-12345

Pay Period: 10/01/2025 - 10/15/2025

Pay Date: 10/20/2025

📝What This Section Tells You

  • Employee Name: Makes sure this is YOUR paycheck, not someone else's!
  • Employee ID: Your unique number at the company (like a student ID at school)
  • Pay Period: The dates you worked during this time. This paycheck is for work done between Oct 1-15.
  • Pay Date: When you actually get the money (Oct 20). Notice it's a few days after the work period ends - this is normal!

💡Important!

Always check that your name is spelled correctly and the dates make sense. If you see someone else's name or wrong dates, tell your boss right away!

DAY 3

Sample Pay Stub - Part 2

Earnings Section

EARNINGS

Regular Hours: 40 hours
Pay Rate: $15.00 per hour
Regular Pay: $600.00
GROSS PAY: $600.00

💰Breaking Down the Earnings

Regular Hours: How many hours Alex worked (40 hours over 2 weeks)

Pay Rate: How much Alex earns per hour ($15.00)

The Math: 40 hours × $15.00 = $600.00

GROSS PAY: The total earned before anything is taken out = $600.00

📖Other Things You Might See

  • Overtime Hours: If you work more than 40 hours in a week, you usually get paid extra (like $22.50/hour instead of $15/hour)
  • Holiday Pay: Extra money for working on holidays
  • Bonus: Extra money your employer gives you for doing good work

All these get added together to make your gross pay!

DAY 3

Sample Pay Stub - Part 3

Deductions Section

DEDUCTIONS

Federal Income Tax: -$72.00
State Income Tax: -$30.00
Social Security (FICA): -$37.20
Medicare (FICA): -$8.70
TOTAL DEDUCTIONS: -$147.90

💸Where the Money Goes

Federal Tax ($72): Goes to U.S. government - about 12% of gross pay

State Tax ($30): Goes to Missouri state government - about 5% of gross pay

Social Security ($37.20): Savings for retirement - about 6.2% of gross pay

Medicare ($8.70): Healthcare for when you're older - about 1.45% of gross pay

TOTAL: $147.90 taken out (about 25% of $600)

✅Check Your Math!

Add up all the deductions: $72 + $30 + $37.20 + $8.70 = $147.90

This should match the "Total Deductions" line. If it doesn't, something might be wrong!

Sample Pay Stub - Part 4

Net Pay (The Important Part!)

NET PAY (TAKE HOME): $452.10

The Final Calculation

Gross Pay: $600.00

MINUS

Total Deductions: $147.90

⬇
$452.10

This is what goes in your bank account!

🏦What Happens Next?

If Alex signed up for direct deposit: The $452.10 automatically goes into Alex's bank account on Oct 20.

If Alex gets a paper check: Alex gets a physical check for $452.10 and needs to take it to the bank to deposit or cash it.

Most people use direct deposit because it's faster and safer!

DAY 3

Understanding YTD

Year To Date Totals

📅What Does YTD Mean?

YTD stands for "Year To Date"

It means: All the money you've earned so far THIS YEAR (since January 1st)

YEAR TO DATE (YTD) TOTALS

YTD Gross Pay: $6,000.00
YTD Deductions: -$1,479.00
YTD Net Pay: $4,521.00

🔍What This Tells Us

Looking at Alex's YTD section, we can see:

  • Alex has earned $6,000 total this year (all paychecks combined)
  • $1,479 has been taken out in deductions
  • Alex has taken home $4,521 total this year

Why is YTD important? When you do your taxes next year, you'll need to know how much you earned total in 2025. YTD tells you!

💾Save Your Last Pay Stub!

At the end of December, your last pay stub will show your total earnings for the whole year. Keep that one safe - you'll need it to do your taxes in April!

Example 1: Simple Calculation

Sarah's Paycheck

The Information:

  • Sarah worked 30 hours
  • She earns $14 per hour
  • Her total deductions are $105

Let's figure out her pay!

🧮Step-by-Step Solution

  1. Calculate Gross Pay
    30 hours × $14/hour = $420
  2. Subtract Deductions
    $420 - $105 = $315
  3. Answer:
    Sarah's gross pay is $420
    Sarah's net pay (take home) is $315

Example 2: Calculating Deductions

Marcus's Paycheck

The Information:

  • Marcus worked 40 hours
  • He earns $16 per hour
  • His deductions are:
    • Federal tax: $77
    • State tax: $32
    • Social Security: $39.68

  3. Subtract Deductions from Gross
    $640 - $157.96 = $482.04
  4. Answer:
    Marcus takes home $482.04

Real World Scenarios

What Would You Do?

Scenario 1: Wrong Hours

😟The Problem

Jamie worked 35 hours this week at $13/hour. She gets her paycheck and sees:

Hours Worked: 25 hours
Gross Pay: $325

What should Jamie do?

✅The Solution

  1. Jamie should check her work schedule or time sheet to confirm she worked 35 hours, not 25
  2. She should talk to her boss or HR person right away and say: "I think there's a mistake on my paycheck. I worked 35 hours but it only shows 25."
  3. Her employer will check the records and fix the mistake on her next paycheck

Important: Always speak up if your paycheck looks wrong! It's your money and you earned it!

Real World Scenarios

What Would You Do?

Scenario 2: Understanding Your First Paycheck

🤔The Situation

Carlos just got his very first paycheck ever! He worked 40 hours at $15/hour. He calculated:

40 × $15 = $600

But when he opens his paycheck, he only gets $450!

Carlos is confused. Is his boss cheating him?

✅The Explanation

NO! This is completely normal. Here's what happened:

  • Carlos earned $600 (gross pay) ✓
  • $150 was taken out for deductions (taxes, FICA) ✓
  • $600 - $150 = $450 (net pay) ✓

The $600 is correct! But after deductions, Carlos takes home $450. This happens to EVERYONE, not just Carlos!

💡Pro Tip

A quick rule of thumb: Expect to take home about 75% of your gross pay.

So if you earn $600 gross, expect about $450 net. If you earn $800 gross, expect about $600 net.

DAY 4

Important Things to Remember

Being Smart About Your Paycheck

📋Your Paycheck Checklist

Every time you get paid, check these things:

  1. Are the dates correct? (Pay period and pay date)
  2. Are your hours correct? (Check against your schedule)
  3. Is your pay rate correct? (Should match what your boss told you)
  4. Does the math work? (Hours × Rate = Gross Pay)
  5. Do the deductions look normal? (Usually 20-30% of gross pay)
  6. Is your net pay in your bank account? (If you have direct deposit)

🚨Red Flags - When to Ask Questions

  • Your hours are way off from what you worked
  • Your pay rate is different than agreed
  • There are deductions you don't recognize
  • Your paycheck didn't arrive on payday
  • Your bank account shows a different amount than your pay stub

Don't be afraid to ask! It's your money, and it's better to ask and be sure than to wonder if something is wrong.

DAY 4

Helpful Tips for Success

Setting Yourself Up Right

🏦Setting Up Direct Deposit

Most jobs will ask if you want "direct deposit." Say YES! Here's why:

  • Faster: Money goes in your account on payday automatically
  • Safer: Can't lose a paper check
  • Easier: Don't have to go to the bank to deposit it

What you'll need: Your bank account number and bank routing number (your bank can give you these)

💾Organizing Your Pay Stubs

Keep your pay stubs organized:

  • Get a folder or envelope at home labeled "Pay Stubs"
  • Put each pay stub in there when you get it
  • Keep them for at least 1 year
  • Keep the December pay stub (shows yearly totals) for 3+ years

Why? You might need them for taxes, applying for loans, getting an apartment, or proving your income!
